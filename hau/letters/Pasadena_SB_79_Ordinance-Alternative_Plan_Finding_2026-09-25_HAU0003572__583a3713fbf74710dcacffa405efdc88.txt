          Ordinance and supporting documents, adopted under Uncodified Ordinance No. 7472

          65912.161, subdivisions (b)(1)(B)(i) and (b)(1)(F).

          Pursuant to Government Code section 65912.160, subdivision (d)(3)(A), this letter
          consists of the California Department of Housing and Community Development’s (HCD)
          findings that the Ordinance is not in substantial compliance with SB 79 as of the date
          of this letter. The standards and provisions of Government Code section 65912.157
          apply in the City unless and until the City adopts and enacts an amended Ordinance
          and zoning map to comply with HCD's findings. 1

          Finding

          1. Section 1, Subsection 2 – Within the Memorial Park, Del Mar, and Lake Station
             TOD zones: Section 1 of the ordinance states: “The following sites are excluded
             from Government Code Section 65912.157 until 18 months from July 1, 2026” and
             Subsection 2 states: “Within the Memorial Park, Del Mar, and Lake Station Transit-
             Oriented Development (TOD) zones: a) Sites with historic resources designated as
             of January 1 , 2025 on the state register (this would include sites designated at the
             national level as they are protected together) b) Sites zoned Multi-Family Residential
             (RM-12 and RM-16) and Single-Family Residential (RS).”

               HCD understands that the City’s Ordinance temporarily excludes sites identified in
               Subsection 2 pursuant to Government Code section 65912.161, subdivision
               (b)(1)(B)(i). To utilize this provision, the City must demonstrate that at least 33

           percent of the sites in a TOD zone allow at least 50 percent of the density and the
           residential floor area ratio (FAR) of what is allowed under Government Code section
           65912.157, subdivision (a). 2 In addition, the TOD zone must allow at least 75
           percent of the aggregate density allowed under Section 65912.157, subdivision (a)

           However, the City excluded certain SB 79-eligible sites from its calculations of the
           densities and FAR allowed under Government Code section 65912.157, subdivision
           (a) and therefore HCD is unable to confirm that the sites temporarily excluded
           pursuant to Government Code section 65912.161, subdivision (b)(1)(B)(i) would
           meet the criteria required by statute.

           Specifically, the City designated the following general plan land use designations as
           ineligible for a SB 79 transit-oriented housing development project: Institutional;
           Open Space; R&D Flex space; and areas that do not have a land use designation. 4
           HCD understands that, while the City considers these general plan land use
           designations as “non-residential sites,” the City’s corresponding zoning does
           principally or conditionally permit residential, mixed, or commercial development in
           these land use designations.

           Government Code section 65912.157, subdivision (a) states (emphasis added): “A
           housing development project shall be an allowed use as a transit-oriented housing
           development on any site zoned for residential, mixed, or commercial development
           within one-half or one-quarter mile of a transit-oriented development stop….” While
           the statute does not explicitly define “mixed” or “commercial” uses, if the
           jurisdiction’s zoning district principally or conditionally permits residential, residential
           mixed use, or commercial land use on a parcel within a TOD zone over which the
           jurisdiction has zoning authority, then SB 79 shall apply. HCD previously issued
           technical assistance explaining that “zoned for” means principally or conditionally
           permitted land use within a zone. 5 For example, if a site has a general plan land use
           designation of Institutional, yet the zoning district allows for commercial development
           with a conditional use permit, then SB 79 applies to the site.

      Conclusion and Next Steps

      The City has until Tuesday, November 24, 2026 (60 days from this letter), to respond to
      the written findings. 8 Pursuant to Government Code section 65912.160, subdivision
      (d)(5), the City must consider the findings provided here and must either amend the
      ordinance to comply with HCD’s findings or enact the ordinance without changes.
      Please note, pursuant to Government Code section 65912.161, subdivision (b)(2), the
      City’s public zoning map must be updated to reflect any amendments to the sites or
      TOD zones that are and are not covered by Section 65912.157. If the City chooses to
      enact the ordinance without changes, it must include findings in its resolution to adopt
      the ordinance that explain the reasons the City believes the ordinance complies with
      Government Code section 65912.161 despite the findings provided by HCD.

      HCD appreciates the cooperation of the City of Pasadena staff during the course of this
      review. If you have any questions or need additional information, please contact
      Auzzie Sheard at auzzie.sheard@hcd.ca.gov. HCD is committed to assisting the City in
      addressing all statutory requirements of SB 79.
