         RE: Fillmore Terrace Project – Notice of Potential Violation

         This letter follows up on the California Department of Housing and Community
         Development’s (HCD) ongoing communication with the City of Fillmore (City) regarding its
         review of a proposed 50-unit affordable and supportive housing project located at Palm
         Street and Santa Clara Avenue, known as the Fillmore Terrace Project (Project). HCD
         understands that the Project application was submitted on November 29, 2021. Over one
         year later, and after issuing three incomplete letters, the City has yet to deem the
         application complete. HCD sent a letter to the City on August 24, 2022 (enclosed for
         reference) providing technical assistance related to portions of the Permit Streamlining
         Act (Gov. Code, § 65943, subd. (a)), Housing Accountability Act (Gov. Code, § 65589.5),
         and “AB 2162” By-Right Supportive Housing Law (Gov. Code, §§ 65650-65656).
         Additionally, HCD and City staff met on November 15, 2022. HCD continues to have
         significant concerns about proper implementation of the required by-right ministerial
         process for the Project pursuant to AB 2162. Therefore, the purpose of this letter is to
         reiterate the City’s obligation for review of AB 2162 supportive housing projects and to
         provide notice about which provisions of the statute the City could risk violating based on
         HCD’s observations of the City’s review to date.

         “AB 2162,” By-Right Supportive Housing Law (Gov. Code, §§ 65650-65656)

         Supportive Housing as A “Use By-Right”

         AB 2162 provides that eligible permanent supportive housing projects must be considered
         a “use by right.” Pursuant to Government Code section 65650, subdivision (d) of the
         statute, “use by-right” has the same meaning as it does in Government Code section
         65583.2, subdivision (i) (a part of State Housing Element Law) and provides the following
         as it relates to the meaning of “use by right”:

             For purposes of this section and Section 65583, the phrase “use by right” shall
             mean that the local government’s review of the owner-occupied or multifamily
             residential use may not require a conditional use permit, planned unit development
             permit, or other discretionary local government review or approval that would
             constitute a “project” for purposes of Division 13 (commencing with Section 21000)
             of the Public Resources Code. (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd. (i). Emphasis added.)
      Accordingly, local agencies cannot impose CEQA review or any discretionary process or
      decision making on AB 2162-compliant housing projects. HCD continues to observe
      comments from the City that treat the Project as though it is undergoing discretionary
      evaluation. Some of these comments were identified in HCD’s previous letter to the City
      and others have resulted from the City’s third review letter. This includes, but is not limited
      to, the City’s comment regarding possible CEQA review 1; imposition of a traffic study
      requirement for which the implications on the Project are not clear2; and potential
      imposition of General Plan policies that are not objective 3. Such comments invite
      discretionary evaluations of the Project and are not permitted under AB 2162.
      Written, Objective Development Standards and Policies
      In a similar vein, local agencies can require supportive housing projects to comply with
      applicable written, objective development standards and policies but shall only require
      such projects to comply with objective standards and policies that apply to other
      multifamily projects in the same zone (Gov. Code, § 65651, subd. (b)(1).) The statute
      further provides that “The local government’s review of a supportive housing development
      to determine whether the development complies with objective development standards,
      including objective design review standards … shall be conducted consistent with the
      requirements of subdivision (f) of Section 65589.5.” (Gov. Code, § 65651, subd. (b)(2)).
      This is in reference to the Housing Accountability Act, which states:
             Except as provided in subdivision (o), nothing in this section shall be construed to
             prohibit a local agency from requiring the housing development project to comply
             with objective, quantifiable, written development standards, conditions, and policies
             appropriate to, and consistent with, meeting the jurisdiction’s share of the regional
             housing need pursuant to Section 65584. (Gov. Code, § 65589.5, subd. (f)(1).)
      The statute is explicit that a local agency’s standard of review is narrow, in that it may
      only review for compliance with written objective standards. HCD has already pointed out

      Supportive Housing Eligibility Documentation
      AB 2162 establishes specific requirements for what a project sponsor is required to
      provide to a local agency in order to establish eligibility for by-right processing. This
      includes:
         •   Compliance with project affordability criteria (Gov. Code, § 65651, subds. (a)(1)
             and (2)).
         •   Compliance with minimum threshold for percent of supportive service units within
             the project (Gov. Code, § 65651, subd. (a)(3)).
         •   Submittal of a supportive services plan demonstrating that supportive services will
             be provided onsite to residents in the project. The plan must include the name of
             the proposed entity or entities that will provide the services, the proposed funding
             sources, and the proposed staffing levels (Gov. Code, § 65652, subds. (a)(b)(c)).
         •   Compliance with criteria for minimum nonresidential floor area requirement for
             provision of on-site supportive services (Gov. Code, § 65651, subd. (a)(5)).
         •   Compliance with unit requirements (Gov. Code, § 65651, subd. (a)(7)).
         •   Compliance with replacement housing provisions (Gov. Code, § 65651, subd.
             (a)(6)).
      Although the City is not precluded from undertaking its own additional analysis, the
      statute does not authorize local agencies to seek information about the project’s
      supportive services component beyond this scope. On this point, the City has sought
      additional documentation regarding the supportive services employees and has
      questioned the adequacy of the supportive services plan 4.
      Summary of Potential Violations of Government Code sections 65650-65656

      In sum, any of the following actions could constitute a violation of AB 2162, By-Right
      Supportive Housing Law:
         •   Imposing discretionary review on the Project, including but not limited to requiring
             CEQA review (other than issuance of a statutory exemption) (Gov. Code, § 65650,
             subd. (d)).
         •   Imposition of any requirements that are not written, objective development
             standards or policies that apply to other multifamily projects in the same zone
             (Gov. Code, § 65651, subd. (b)(1)).
         •   Imposition of any requirements relating to the provision of supportive housing

     Pending 6th Cycle Housing Element

     As the City is aware, HCD is reviewing the City’s first draft of its 6th Cycle Housing
     Element (submitted on November 21, 2022). Housing elements are required to contain
     analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints on the development of housing
     for all income levels. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(5)). This includes, but is not limited
     to, analysis of land use controls, local permit processes and procedures, and locally
     adopted ordinances that directly impact the cost and supply of residential development.
     After identifying governmental constraints, the City must implement programs to remove
     those governmental constraints to the development of housing where legally possible.
     (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).) HCD’s observations of the City’s processing of the
     Fillmore Terrace Project has alerted HCD to potential constraints associated with the
     City’s review process more broadly, particularly for ministerial projects mandated by state
     law such as AB 2162. HCD will be providing the City with a formal review and response to
     the draft 6th Cycle Housing Element on or before February 17, 2023.
     Conclusion
     HCD urges the City to remain mindful of its obligations under AB 2162 and the other state
     housing laws previously reviewed with the City. As a reminder, HCD has enforcement
     authority over AB 2162 By-Right Supportive Housing Law, State Density Bonus Law,
     Housing Accountability Act, portions of the Permit Streamlining act, and Housing
     Elements, among other state housing laws. Accordingly, HCD may review local
     government actions and inactions to determine consistency with these laws. If HCD finds
     that a city’s actions do not comply with state law, HCD may notify the California Office of
     the Attorney General that the local government is in violation of state law. (Gov. Code, §
     65585, subd. (j).) If you have questions or need additional information, please contact
     Lisa Frank, of our staff, at lisa.frank@hcd.ca.gov.
