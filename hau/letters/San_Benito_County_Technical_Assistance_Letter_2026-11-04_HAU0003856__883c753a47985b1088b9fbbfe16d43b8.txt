          The purpose of this letter is for the California Department of Housing and Community
          Development (HCD) to express support for the proposed 100 percent affordable
          housing project at 340 Bridgevale Road, known as Baler Place (Project), and to provide
          technical assistance to the County of San Benito (County) regarding its obligations
          under state housing law with respect to this Project, as well as the County’s overall
          compliance with state housing laws, most notably Housing Element Law.

          Background

          CRP Affordable Housing and Community Development (Applicant) has proposed a 100
          percent affordable 53-unit housing project at 340 Bridgevale Road with a mix of one-,
          two, and three-bedroom units. The Project application was submitted in late 2023.

          On January 17, 2024, Abraham Prado, Director of Planning, Building, and Code
          Enforcement, signed a document titled “Verification of Environmental Review and Land
          Use Entitlements” for inclusion in the Applicant’s application for Low Income Housing
          Tax Credits (LIHTCs). In that document, Director Prado stated, “Property is zoned by
          right for multifamily and the project as proposed is not subject to discretionary review”
          (emphasis added).

          On January 19, 2024, Director Prado signed a document titled “Local Approvals and
          Environmental Review Verification” for inclusion in the Applicant’s application for
          Multifamily Super NOFA funds from HCD. In that document, Director Prado again
          confirmed, “The project is zoned by right for multifamily and the project as proposed is
          not subject to discretionary review.”

On March 12, 2024, the County sent the Applicant a letter titled “Complete – Planning
Development Review.” In this letter, the County stated, “This letter is to inform you that
planning has reviewed your proposed submittal and has found it to be consistent with
County ordinances and development standards and can be submitted to the building

family development by right (as a permitted use), with design review at an
administrative level by the County of San Benito.”

On March 13, 2024, Director Prado signed a document titled “Verification of Zoning and
Other Required Local Approvals” for inclusion in the Applicant’s application for HOME
program funds from HCD. In this document, Director Prado again reiterated, “The
property is zoned by right for multifamily and the project as prosed [sic] is not subject to
discretionary review.”

On March 18, 2025, the County sent the Applicant a second letter titled “Complete –
Planning Design Review of Entitlement Set Dated 01/15/2025.” In this letter, the County
stated, “This letter is to inform you that planning has reviewed your proposed submittal
for design review, as well as routed the design to other Agencies, and has deemed the
design review complete. Please note the additional items that need to be addressed
prior to building permit issuance.” The County also noted again that the project site has
a zoning designation of Residential Multifamily (RM) and stated, “The RM District allows
multi-family development by right (as a permitted use), with design review at an
administrative level by the County of San Benito.”

HCD understands that, following that March 18, 2025 letter, the Project’s entitlements
were fully approved. The Applicant then proceeded to begin completing items on the
County’s building permit checklist in reliance on the County’s final determination and
approvals.

On April 28, 2026, the County Board of Supervisors (Board) held a hearing under the
Tax and Equity Fiscal Responsibility Act (TEFRA) in connection with the proposed
issuance of revenue bonds by the California Municipal Finance Authority (the “CMFA”)
to finance the Project. Bond repayment is the sole responsibility of the Applicant and the
CMFA; the County is not responsible for the debts, liabilities, and obligations incurred
through this bond financing. At that hearing, the Board failed to approve the TEFRA
financing, against staff recommendation.

      Analysis

      HCD is extremely concerned by the County’s actions. The County previously approved
      the Project—noting repeatedly, in multiple communications to the Applicant,
      memorialized in writing, that the Project was by right and not subject to discretionary
      review. Indeed, more than one year after the County’s ministerial approval of the
      Project, the County now asserts the Project is subject to a Major Development Plan
      review, including a Planning Commission hearing. Likewise, HCD is extremely
      concerned that the County did not approve the Project’s TEFRA financing. These
      actions are inconsistent with state housing laws and with the County’s adopted 6th cycle
      Housing Element.

      Inconsistency Between General Plan and County Code

      The County’s 6th cycle housing element was determined by HCD to be in substantial
      compliance with Housing Element Law on February 3, 2026. Prior to that date, the
      County’s 5th cycle housing element remained in effect. Therefore, the entire processing
      of the Project from application submission in late 2023 through Planning Design Review
      completion in March 2025 was completed under the 5th cycle housing element.

      The County’s 5th cycle housing element clearly states that projects in the RM district are
      subject to ministerial approval: “San Benito County ministerially allows multifamily rental
      housing in its highest-density residential zoning district, Residential Multiple (RM).” 1

      Furthermore, the 5th cycle housing element does not include any reference to Major
      Development Plan Review for multi-family residential developments containing five or
      more dwelling units. In fact, the housing element explicitly notes that multi-family
      projects larger than fourplexes are ministerially allowed: “RM ministerially allows
      duplexes, fourplexes, condominiums, apartments, and other higher-density housing,
      plus the residential types allowed in R1. … In this district, development requests for
      multifamily dwellings are ministerial and only require a building permit.” 2

      The housing element is one of several elements of the County’s general plan. The
      general plan is the County’s guiding document, and County zoning ordinances must be
      consistent with the general plan. 3 In its initial processing of the Project, the County
      appropriately applied the ministerial process outlined in the governing general plan
      document (specifically, in the housing element of the general plan). The County’s
      antithetical June 22, 2026 letter that the Project is now subject to Major Development
      Plan review process is inconsistent with the County’s general plan, and its approved
      housing element applicable to this Project.

      Required Approval Timelines

      The County should not take further action to disapprove the Project at this time. A public
      agency that is the lead agency for a development project shall approve or disapprove
      the project within 60 days from the date of receipt of a complete application if the project
      is subject to ministerial review by the public agency. 4 While the County did not provide a
      letter to the Applicant expressly determining that a specific version of the application
      was complete, an application must be complete before a jurisdiction can undertake a
      consistency review. Because the County’s March 12, 2024 letter finds the project
      consistent with County ordinances and development standards, the application must
      therefore have been determined to be complete on or before March 12, 2024. As such,
      the County had 60 days – or until May 11, 2024 at the latest – to approve or disapprove
      the Project. That date has long since passed. Therefore, the County should no longer
      take further action to disapprove the Project.

      HCD understands that the County’s position is that the determination that the project
      was subject to ministerial review was made in error, which would mean that the
      approval timeline for projects subject to ministerial review does not apply. However,
      even if that were true, a 60-day approval period would still apply.

      Even if the project was not subject to ministerial review but was instead eligible for a
      CEQA exemption, the lead agency for a development project shall approve or
      disapprove the project within 60 days from the determination by the lead agency that
      the project is exempt from CEQA. 5 Given that the CEQA exemption determination was
      first made on January 17, 2024, the County would have had 60 days – or until
      March 17, 2024 – to approve or disapprove the Project. That date has also passed.
      Accordingly, the County should no longer take further action to disapprove the Project.

      Inconsistencies with Housing Element

      The County’s requirement that the Project undergo Major Development Review even
      after the Project has already been approved, and the County’s failure to approve the
      Project’s TEFRA resolution, are actions inconsistent with several of the policies and
      programs in the County’s adopted 6th cycle housing element, 6 including but not limited
      to the following:

          •   Policy 2.2: Encourage development of affordable housing by non-profit
              organizations primarily engaged in housing construction or management.

          •   Program 1-1: Affordable Housing Developers. “The County recognizes that
              affordable housing cannot be supplied solely by market-rate developers, so the
              County will continue to work with affordable housing developers to expand the
              supply of lower-income units, including extremely low-income units.”

          •   Program 1-7: Federal and State Housing Funding Sources. “The County shall
              seek financial assistance to provide financing to assist housing construction of
              extremely low-, very low-, low-, and moderate-income units that serve families
              and special needs groups using CDBG, USDA, HOME, CalHome, and other
              federal and state funding sources.”

          •   Program 1-15: Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) Program. “Work with
              local non-profit affordable and market-rate housing developers to utilize LIHTC
              funds to provide newly constructed affordable housing rental units within the
              County.”

      HCD shall review any action or failure to act by the County that it determines is
      inconsistent with an adopted housing element, including any failure to implement any
      program actions included in the housing element. If HCD finds that the action or failure
      to act by the County does not substantially comply with Housing Element Law, HCD
      may revoke its findings that the County’s housing element is in compliance with Housing
      Element Law until it determines that the County has come into compliance with state
      law. 7

      Furthermore, the County’s Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA) for the 6th cycle
      is 754 units, including 444 low- and very low-income units. This project is listed as a
      pipeline project in the County’s 6th cycle housing element. This project would provide 53
      low-income units to count towards the County’s RHNA. According to the 2025 Annual
      Progress Report the County has only met 2.4 percent of its RHNA for lower-income
      units. As you are aware, the Project has been awarded funding in the forms of LIHTCs,
      Infill Infrastructure Grant funds, and HOME funds, among others. By requiring additional
      review after the Project has already received its approvals, the County puts the Project
      at risk of losing its awarded funding and further frustrates the County’s ability to achieve
      it RHNA.

      Conclusion
      Based on the foregoing, the County should rescind its requirement that the Project
      undergo Major Development Review, affirm its previous and existing approvals, and
      approve the Project’s TEFRA resolution expeditiously.

      HCD has enforcement authority over Housing Element Law, among other state housing
      laws. Accordingly, HCD may review local government actions to determine consistency
      with these laws. If HCD finds that a jurisdiction is in violation of state law, HCD may
      notify the California Office of the Attorney General. 8 Please be advised that HCD will be
      monitoring the County’s actions on this Project.
