         RE: City of Oceanside 6th Cycle Housing Element Programs – Letter of Inquiry

         Dear Leilani Hines:

         The purpose of this letter is to inquire about the status of the City of Oceanside’s (City)
         implementation of 6th Cycle Housing Element Programs pursuant to Government Code
         section 65585, subdivision (i). The California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) sent the City a letter on November 14, 2023, finding the City’s
         housing element in substantial compliance with Housing Element Law. 1 This was based
         on, among other reasons, the City’s commitment to implement Programs according to
         specific timelines.

         As the commitment date has passed for the programs listed below, HCD requests the
         following information:

         •    Program 6 (Farm Worker and Employee Housing) was due by December 31, 2022.
              The Annual Progress Report (APR) from 2024 states that the City is in the process
              of implementing this program. However, the City should provide an update on the
              status and timeline for completion.
         •    Program 7 (Adequate Residential Sites to Accommodate the Regional Housing
              Needs Allocation (RHNA) and No Net Loss) was due by December 31, 2024. The
              program commits to develop actions and incentives to encourage lot consolidation,
              especially within the Corridors Specific Plan area. The APR from 2024 does not
              address the status of this program action. The City should provide an update on the
              status and timeline for completion.
         •    Program 9 (Smart and Sustainable Corridors Plan) was due by April 30, 2024. The
              program commits to adopt the Smart and Sustainable Corridors Plan and seek
              available grant funding to facilitate TOD and smart growth throughout the planning
              period. The APR from 2024 states that the City is in the process of implementing this

•   Program 10 (Constraints to Development) was due by January 1, 2023. The

    program commits to amend the zoning code to facilitate zoning for emergency
    shelters, low-barrier navigation centers, transitional and supportive housing, as well
    as remove constraints on the development of residential care facilities for more than
    6 persons. The APR from 2024 indicates that the City revised the emergency shelter
    ordinance in 2021 but should still provide relevant documentation. In addition, the
    City should provide an update on the status and timeline for completion of the other
    program commitments.
•   Program 15 (Acquisition/Rehabilitation) was due on an annual basis. The program
    commits to pursue acquisition and rehabilitation of multifamily and single-family
    housing by partnering with nonprofit developers and promoting the availability of
    housing assistance funds to lower-income households. The City should provide an
    update on the status and timeline for completion for this program.
•   Program 19 (Preservation of Affordable Housing Units) was due on an annual basis.
    The program commits to annual monitoring and working with interested nonprofits to
    preserve at-risk projects at least one year prior to expiration of affordability contracts
    to preserve the long-term affordability of 264 units of assisted housing at-risk of
    converting to market rate uses. The element indicates that affordability terms for
    North River Club Apartments expire in 2026. HCD records indicate affordability
    terms for Tremont Street Apartments also expires in 2026. The City should indicate if
    the City is working with nonprofits to preserve at-risk projects.

Consequences of Failure to Implement Programs

Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i), grants HCD authority to review any
action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is inconsistent with an
adopted housing element or Housing Element Law. This includes failure to implement
program actions included in the housing element. Failure to implement actions in the
programs listed above by the statutory deadline may result in HCD revoking its finding
of substantial compliance with Housing Element Law.

       Various consequences may apply if the City does not have a housing element in
       compliance with Housing Element Law, including ineligibility or delay in receiving certain
       state funds, referral to the California Office of the Attorney General, court-imposed
       financial penalties, the loss of local land use authority to a court-appointed agent, and
       the application of the “builder’s remedy.” 2

       Conclusion

       Housing elements are essential to developing a blueprint for growth and are a vital tool
       to address California’s prolonged housing crisis. Accordingly, state law has established
       clear disincentives for local jurisdictions that fail to comply with Housing Element Law.
       To ensure the City continues to meet the 6th cycle update requirements for a
       substantially compliant housing element, the City must submit any adopted ordinances,
       resolutions or documentation that demonstrates that housing element program
       commitments have been met to HCD for review. 3

       HCD understands the City has many commitments and recognizes the challenges of
       implementation. HCD will consider any written response before taking further action
       authorized by Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i), including issuance of a
       Corrective Action Letter and removal of HCD’s finding of housing element compliance.

       Please provide a written response to this inquiry by April 24, 2026. If you have any

       Mao.Lee@hcd.ca.gov.
