          1. Section 20.18.070 B. – Unclear Language and Concurrent Processing – the
             ordinance states: “An urban lot split may be approved without a concurrently
             submitted application for building permits for a two (2) unit residential
             development pursuant to Cal. Gov’t Code § 65852.21 (emphasis added).” SB 9
             allows for a standalone urban lot split application, or concurrent processing of
             units and urban lot splits permitted pursuant to the law. 2 The City must process
             an urban lot split application if it is not accompanied by an application of units
             pursuant to SB 9. The word “may” is unclear3 and, in its plain meaning, opens the
             possibility for the City to reject the application. The provision must be amended to
             state that the City shall process an application for a compliant urban lot split
             application.

          2. Section 20.18.070 N. and 21.49.110 J.2. – Ellis Act – These provisions state that
             “The owner (shall not have/has not) withdrawn rent or price-controlled
             accommodations from rent or lease on this parcel within 15 years prior to the
             date of application for the proposed project. However, Government Code Section
             66411.7, subdivision (a)(3)(D) and Section 65852.21, subdivision (a)(4) both
             state that: “(A/The) parcel… on which an owner of residential real property has
             exercised the owner’s rights under Chapter 12.75 (commencing with Section
             7060) of Division 7 of Title 1 to withdraw accommodations from rent or lease
             within 15 years before the date that the development proponent submits an
             application.” It is important that the language in the ordinance accurately reflects
             SB 9. The ordinance(s) must specify that parcels are ineligible if “an owner of
             residential real property has exercised the owner’s rights under Chapter 12.75
             (commencing with Section 7060) of Division 7 of Title 1 to withdraw

          9. Sections 21.17.030 – Conformance with State Law – Section 21.17.035 states:
             Following adoption of this Chapter by ordinance by the City Council, and upon
             each subsequent amendment to this Chapter, it shall be submitted to HCD for
             review… Following notification by HCD that the ordinance complies with the
             requirements of state law, this Chapter shall be considered in conformance with
             state law… Publication by HCD of a new or updated SB 9 Guidebook or similar
             publication, or receipt of a letter of technical assistance from HCD by the City or
             by another local agency or jurisdiction, or other actions taken by HCD… in the
             absence of state legislative action…, shall not render this Chapter out of
             conformance.” In other words, the ordinance states that (1) it will be submitted to
             (and subsequently reviewed by) HCD pursuant to an uncited section of the
             Government Code, then (2) any HCD technical assistance following that review
             “shall not render (the ordinance) out of conformance (with state law).”
             Section 21.17.035 further states: “following attainment of conforming status as
             described in Section 21.17.030, the City’s administration of this Chapter shall be
             presumed to be consistent with state law, and the City shall owe no deference to
             further statutory interpretation analysis by HCD on the administration of this
             Chapter.”

             Although HCD appreciates the City’s willingness to engage with HCD during
             ordinance development, these provisions should be removed because they
             conflict with state law. SB 9 does not require jurisdictions to submit implementing
             ordinances to HCD, nor does it authorize HCD to issue binding or final
             compliance determinations. In addition, local ordinances cannot insulate
             themselves from subsequent statutory amendments or judicial decisions, which
             control irrespective of any prior communication between HCD and the City.
             Language suggesting that the ordinance will continue to be deemed “in
             conformance” unless the Legislature amends SB 9 encourages misalignment
             with state law and may create confusion regarding the City’s ongoing obligations
             under SB 9.

             Despite any ordinance to the contrary, Government Code Section 65585,
             subdivision (j), requires HCD to notify the City – and may require HCD to notify
             the Office of the Attorney General – when a local action is in violation of state
             housing law, including SB 9, and to take enforcement action as appropriate.

             Accordingly, these ordinance provisions do not serve a practical purpose in
             implementing SB 9 and should be removed to avoid potential conflict with state
             law.

          10. Sections 20.18.020 and 21.49.030 – Application submittal timelines and
              processing – These two sections contain the procedure and timelines with
              processing an application for an SB 9 units and urban lot splits. Broadly
              speaking, HCD lauds the City’s attempts to toll timelines to allow an applicant to
              correct deficiencies (Sections 20.18.020 G. and 21.49.130 C.1.). However, SB 9
              states that applications “shall be considered and approved or denied within 60
              days from the date the local agency receives a completed application. If the local
              agency has not approved or denied the completed application within 60 days, the
              application shall be deemed approved.”13 Under the current ordinance scheme, it
              is possible for an application to be deemed approved by operation of law while
              the City’s ordinance considers the deadline not yet passed. The ordinance must
              be amended to reflect (i.e. be no less permissive or restrictive) than the
              application processing timeline and procedure in SB 9. The ordinance must be
              amended to state that the City will approve or deny an application within 60 days
              from the date the local agency receives a completed application.

             Additionally, the ordinance’s completeness determination references the
             procedure laid out in LBMC “Code (Title 18, LBMC) or Section 21.21.201, as
             applicable” (Section 21.49.130 C.1.). The City must also comply with the
             completeness determination process provided in the Permit Streamlining Act

      Analysis

      The City must ensure that housing development projects that utilize SB9 and/or ADU
      law are not misclassified as a student housing development, student housing facility,
      shared housing, shared housing unit, or other locally defined land use in a way that
      denies applicants the benefits and protections of these state housing laws. As stated in
      the July 29, 2025 letter of technical assistance, such a fact pattern arose in the case of
      a project located at 1411 Iroquois Avenue regarding an SB 9-ADU residential
      development project. Thus, the City must amend its draft ordinance to clarify that units
      lawfully permitted pursuant to SB 9 and ADU law 22 are not considered group housing,
      student a student housing facility, or student housing development as defined locally
      and are thus improperly considered unpermitted or conditionally permitted uses.

      HCD provides the following example edit that would make clear that SB 9 and ADUs are
      not improperly considered under the above locally defined land uses:

         •   21.15.2473 – Shared housing – "Shared housing" means a residence providing
             long-term housing (more than 30 days tenancy) with a shared a kitchen or dining
             area. Shared housing includes but is not limited to communal housing,
             boardinghouse, lodginghouse, dormitory, student housing facility. fraternity or
             sorority house, religious housing, employee housing, barracks or military
             housing, shared housing building, and shared housing units. “Shared housing”
             does not include a residential unit permitted pursuant to Government Code §
             65852.21 or §§ 66310 through 66342.

         •   21.15.2975 – Student housing facility – "Student Housing Facility" is a residence
             exclusively housing students of higher education institutions. Student Housing
             Facility includes dormitories, fraternity and sorority houses, and Student Housing
             Development. Student Housing Facility is a type of Shared Housing. “Student
             Housing Facility” does not include a residential unit permitted pursuant to
             Government Code § 65852.21 or §§ 66310 through 66342.

         •   21.15.2976 – Student housing development – "Student housing development"
             means a residence exclusively housing seven (7) or more students of a higher
             education institution, and which is owned or administered by a private
             organization, not affiliated with an educational institution. Student Housing
             Development is a type of Shared Housing and shall be subject to the conditional
             use standards for a Student Housing Facility. “Student housing development”
             does not include a residential unit permitted pursuant to Government Code §
             65852.21 or §§ 66310 through 66342.

      Constitutional Right to Privacy

      Notwithstanding the unambiguous inability of a local agency to categorize units
      permitted pursuant to SB 9 and ADU Law as a locally defined land use and
      subsequently subject such units to discretionary review or consider them unpermitted,
      HCD expresses concerns with the above locally defined land uses as well as other
      practices that target specific occupants of housing, particularly when housing is offered
      to the general public and is not restricted to any class or category of occupant. Such
      policies may serve to discriminate and impose specific restrictions on housing
      developments based on the City’s presumption of future occupants of housing intended
      to be made available to the general public. HCD encourages the City to review the draft
      definitions of “shared housing” and “shared housing unit” (LBMC §§ 21.15.2473,
      21.15.2477) to ensure alignment with California’s fair-housing and constitutional privacy
      protections. Because the draft ordinance categorizes a variety of communal living
      arrangements – including religious housing – as a separate land-use type distinct from
      dwelling units, this may raise questions under the State’s Anti-Discrimination in Land
      Use Law (Gov. Code § 65008) and Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH) Law
      (Gov. Code § 8899.50), as discussed in HCD’s July 29, 2025, technical assistance
      letter.

      Separately, the California Supreme Court has long held that local governments may not
      restrict household living arrangements solely based on the number of unrelated adults
      living together (City of Santa Barbara v. Adamson), nor impose additional permitting,
      spacing, or operational requirements on communal homes without a legitimate land-use
      nexus (City of Chula Vista v. Pagard). These cases apply the California Constitution’s
      right-to-privacy standard, under which local regulations affecting communal living
      arrangements must be supported by a compelling governmental interest and drawn to
      address legitimate land-use impacts. The Attorney General’s June 3, 2026, legal alert
      reiterates that these constitutional principles – as articulated in Adamson and Pagard –
      “inform and reinforce” the specific protections required under FEHA, the
      Anti-Discrimination in Land Use Law, the AFFH Law, and Housing Element Law, and
      may be useful for the City as it refines its shared housing definitions. 23

      These considerations do not prevent the City from regulating safety or objective
      development standards, but they do suggest that the City may wish to evaluate whether
      “shared housing,” as drafted, could inadvertently differentiate between household types
      in a way that implicates constitutional or fair-housing concerns.
