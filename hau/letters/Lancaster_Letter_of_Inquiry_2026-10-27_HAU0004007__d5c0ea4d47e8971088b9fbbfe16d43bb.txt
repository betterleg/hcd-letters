         RE: City of Lancaster 6th Cycle Housing Element Programs – Letter of Inquiry

         Dear Trolis Niebla:

         The purpose of this letter is to inquire about the status of the City of Lancaster (City)
         implementation of 6th Cycle Housing Element Programs pursuant to Government Code
         section 65585, subdivision (i). The California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) sent the City a letter on February 10, 2023, finding the City’s
         housing element in substantial compliance with Housing Element Law. 1 This was based
         on, among other reasons, the City's commitment to implement Programs according to
         specific timelines.

         As the commitment date has passed for the programs listed below, HCD requests the
         following information:

         •    Program H-1.2 No Net Loss: The Housing Element requires the City to monitor its
              sites inventory and ensure sufficient capacity remains to accommodate its Regional
              Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA) by income category. The 2025 Annual Progress
              Report (APR) indicates that the City reviews development proposals for consistency
              with the sites inventory and reports that no projects pursued lower-density
              development than assumed. Please describe the City’s monitoring procedures and
              provide documentation of any capacity assessments or related actions undertaken
              during the reporting period.

         •    Program H-2.3 Extremely-Low-Income (ELI) and Special Needs Housing: The
              Housing Element commits the City to expand housing opportunities and incentives
              for ELI households and special-needs populations, including seniors and persons
              with disabilities, and to promote the incorporation of universal design features in new
              housing. The 2025 APR indicates that, in 2023, the City revised its zoning ordinance
              to enhance incentives for special-needs housing, including reductions in parking
              requirements. Please provide documentation of these ordinance amendments and
              identify any additional actions the City has undertaken to implement the program
              commitments.

•   Program H-3.1 Monitor and Preserve Affordable Housing and At-Risk Units: The
    Housing Element commits the City to monitor affordable housing units, preserve
    affordability, and support funding efforts to maintain at-risk units, with a goal of
    preserving 661 affordable units. The 2025 APR indicates that the City is actively
    monitoring 661 affordable multifamily units with expiring affordability covenants.
    Please provide an updated inventory of these units, identify any covenant
    approaching expiration, and describe the City’s ongoing efforts to preserve or
    replace at-risk affordable housing.

•   Program H-4.3 Development Process Streamlining: The Housing Element commits
    the City to streamline residential development review, evaluate procedures to reduce
    processing times, and make informational materials about SB 35 streamlining
    provisions available within one year of Housing Element adoption. The 2025 APR
    reports that the City implemented an online land-use management database for
    digital submittals and is evaluating additional ways to reduce review times. Please
    describe any additional streamlining measures under consideration, including
    implementation status and anticipated timeline.

•   Program H-4.8 Nongovernmental Constraints: The Housing Element commits the
    City to monitoring and evaluating development standards, construction methods and
    to revising regulations or procedures as necessary to address nongovernmental
    constraints to housing development. The 2025 APR indicates that the City continues
    to assess its development standards and revised its zoning ordinance in 2023.
    Please identify the specific constraints evaluated, describe how the 2023
    amendments were designated to address those constraints, and outline any
    additional actions taken or planned to further implement the program commitments.

•   Program H-5.2: Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH): The Housing Element
    commits the City to promoting fair housing opportunities, disseminating public
    information on fair housing laws and available resources, referring housing
    discrimination complaints to appropriate agencies, ensuring that development
    applications are reviewed impartially, and implementing the recommendations
    identified in its Analysis of Impediments to Fair Housing. The 2025 APR notes that
    the City updated its Analysis of Impediments and identified a developer for
    approximately 35 acres of Housing Authority property (HNR-3), which will require a
    zone change and General Plan amendment to allow higher-density mixed-use
    development. Please provide an update on the HNR-3 project, including the status
    of the required applications and the anticipated timeline, and describe any additional
    AFFH related actions undertaken during the reporting period to implement the
    program’s commitments.

       Consequences of Failure to Implement Programs

       Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i), grants HCD authority to review any
       action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is inconsistent with an
       adopted housing element or Housing Element Law. This includes failure to implement
       program actions included in the housing element. Failure to implement program actions
       by the statutory deadline means the City’s housing element will no longer substantially
       comply with Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial
       compliance.

       Various consequences may apply if the City does not have a housing element in
       compliance with Housing Element Law, including ineligibility or delay in receiving certain
       state funds, referral to the California Office of the Attorney General, court-imposed
       financial penalties, the loss of local land use authority to a court-appointed agent, and
       the application of the “builder’s remedy.” 2

       Conclusion

       Housing elements are essential to developing a blueprint for growth and are a vital tool
       to address California’s prolonged housing crisis. Accordingly, state law has established
       clear disincentives for local jurisdictions that fail to comply with Housing Element Law.
       To ensure the City continues to meet the 6th cycle update requirements for a
       substantially compliant housing element, the City must submit any adopted ordinances,
       resolutions or documentation that demonstrates that housing element program
       commitments have been met to HCD for review. 3

       HCD understands the City has many commitments and recognizes the challenges of
       implementation. HCD will consider any written response before taking further action
       authorized by Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i), including issuance of a
       Corrective Action Letter and removal of HCD’s finding of housing element compliance.

       Please provide a written response to this inquiry by November 25, 2026. If you have any
