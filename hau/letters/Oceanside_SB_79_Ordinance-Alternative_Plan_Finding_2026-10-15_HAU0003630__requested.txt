          Thank you for submitting the City of Oceanside’s (City) enacted Senate Bill (SB) 79
          Ordinance and supporting documents, adopted under Uncodified Ordinance
          No. 26-0R0414-1 (Ordinance) to:

               •    Adopt and implement SB 79 per Government Code section 65912.160,

               •    Exempt sites per Government Code section 65912.160, subdivision (e)(1); and
               •    Temporarily exclude certain sites per Government Code section 65912.161,
                    subdivisions (b)(1)(A), (b)(1)(B)(ii), and (b)(1)(F).

          This letter consists of the California Department of Housing and Community
          Development’s (HCD) findings per Government Code section 65912.160, subdivision
          (d)(3)(A) that the Ordinance is not in substantial compliance with SB 79 as of the
          date of this letter. The standards and provisions of Government Code section
          65912.157 apply in the City unless and until the City adopts and enacts an amended
          Ordinance and zoning map to comply with HCD's findings. 1

          Findings

          HCD reviewed the documents submitted by the City (see Attachment A – Table 1)2 and
          finds that revisions will be necessary to substantially comply with the requirements of
          SB 79. Specifically:

       1. SB 79 Applicability Map. TOD Zone Map. The San Diego Association of
          Governments (SANDAG) SB 79 map differs from the City’s SB 79 Applicability Map
          for the Oceanside Transit Center and the Coast Highway Sprinter Stations. In
          addition, the City’s SB 79 map is missing pedestrian access points for the El Camino
          Real Station, Rancho Del Oro Station, and College Boulevard Station. The City must
          modify its SB 79 map and capacity calculations to be at least as inclusive as
          SANDAG’s map.

           HCD understands that SANDAG had not published a map or provided the map to
           staff at the time the City’s Ordinance was drafted and the City prepared its map
           based on anticipated eligible TOD zones. As the City is aware, SANDAG
           subsequently published its TOD map pursuant to Government Code section
           65912.160, subdivision (f), which shall have “a rebuttal presumption of validity for
           use by project applicants and local governments.”

       2. SB 79 Applicability Map. Excluded – Zoning. Under the “Excluded – Zoning”

          not covered under Section 65912.157 because they are “Not Residential,

          “zoned for” means principally or conditionally permitted land use within a zone. 4

      3. SB 79 Applicability Map. Excluded - Wetland Buffer and Habitat Preserve. The
         City’s online Draft SB 79 Applicability Map identifies sites that are not covered under
         Section 65912.157 because they are considered “habitat preserve” or “wetland
         buffer”. Based on the City’s Council Staff Report, HCD understands that the City
         considers sites within a habitat preserve, or within 100 feet of an identified wetland,
         as identified in the City’s Vital and Sustainable Resources Element of the general
         plan, ineligible for SB 79. 6 The Staff report states that these sites are, “effectively

              undevelopable given that a project on such a site would likely be ineligible for
              ministerial approval under SB 79 and would be subject to a discretionary review with

              However, Government Code section 65912.157 does not provide exemptions for

       4. SB 79 Applicability Map. Excluded Sites – Rent Control. The City’s online Draft
          SB 79 Applicability Map identifies sites that are not covered under Section
          65912.157 because they are “[e]xcluded per GOV § 65912.157(h)(1)”. However,
          HCD previously issued technical assistance explaining that, for calculating the
          overall SB 79 capacity, sites may not be excluded pursuant to Government Code
          section 65912.157, subdivision (h). 9 The statute does not state that such sites may
          be excluded from the SB 79 capacity calculations, only that SB 79 developments
          that require demolition of such units, or where such units were demolished, may not
          be located on them (emphasis added).

              As in Findings 2 and 3, such excluded parcel land areas must be included in the City’s
              SB 79 capacity calculations to determine whether a site is eligible for temporary
              exclusion pursuant to Government Code section 65912.161, subdivision (b).

       5. Ordinance Section 3. Exempt Sites – Path of Travel. The City’s online Draft
          SB 79 Applicability Map and Section 3 of its Ordinance exempts all sites which have
          a walking path of more than one mile to a TOD stop pursuant to Government Code
          section 65912.161, subdivision (e)(1). The Ordinance defines a walking path as, “a
          publicly accessible, continuous, and unobstructed path of travel with continuous
          paved or improved sidewalk from the closest point of the affected parcel to the
          pedestrian access point of the transit oriented development stop….”

          Government Code section 65912.160, subdivision (e)(1) states (emphasis added),
          “The ordinance may designate areas within one-half mile of a transit-oriented
          development stop as exempt from the provisions of this chapter if: The local
          government makes findings supported by substantial evidence that there exists no
          walking path of less than one mile from that location to the transit-oriented
          development stop.”

          While the City provided a map identifying the sites exempted under subdivision
          (e)(1), HCD did not receive additional documentation providing evidence that the
          sites identified do not have a walking path of less than one mile from the site to the
          transit-oriented development stop.

          Furthermore, sidewalks are not the only form of “walking path.” For example, a
          pedestrian safety resource from the Federal Highway Administration describe
          sidewalks and walkways as, “‘pedestrian lanes’ that provide people with space to
          travel within the public right-of-way that is separated from roadway vehicles,” and
          notes that, “[w]hile sidewalks are typically made of concrete, less expensive
          walkways may be constructed of asphalt, crushed stone, or other materials if they
          are properly maintained and accessible (firm, stable, and slip-resistant).” 10

          Another example from the California Transportation Department Traffic Operations
          Manual glossary similarly defines a ‘walkway’ as a, "[p]assage or path for walking
          such as a sidewalk or pedestrian path,” and defines a ‘pedestrian path’ as a "[p]aved
          walkway along the side of the street that is specifically designed for walking. It is a
          facility that is physically separated from the roadway and intended for pedestrian
          use.” 11

          As a result, HCD is unable to confirm whether the identified sites satisfy the criteria
          of Government Code Section 65912.160, subdivision (e)(1). The City must provide
          evidence that the method used to determine which sites have no walking path
          considers all walking paths, and not merely sidewalks.

      6. Ordinance Section 4.1. Deferred Sites – Sufficient Local Capacity. Pursuant to
         Government Code section 65912.161, subdivision (b)(1)(A), the City’s online Draft
         SB 79 Applicability Map and Section 4.1 of the Ordinance temporarily excludes from
         SB 79 all sites within a TOD zone that permit a density and residential floor area
         ratio at 50 percent of the standards specified by Government Code Section
         65912.157, subdivision (a).

          However, based on the City’s submitted documents and the density and floor area
          ratio standards in the City’s Zoning Ordinance, HCD is unable to confirm that the
          zoning of the identified sites permits the density and residential floor ratio required
          by Government Code section 65912.161, subdivision (b)(1)(A). On July 30, 2026,
          HCD requested a more detailed spreadsheet identifying the sites, either by parcel or
          by zoning district, to verify that this criterion was met, but has not received the
          additional information as of the date of this letter.

      7. Ordinance Section 4.2. Deferred Sites – Low Resource Area With 40 Percent
         Capacity. The City’s online Draft SB 79 Applicability Map and Section 4.2 of the
         Ordinance temporarily exclude sites within the Oceanside Transit Center TOD zone,
         pursuant to Government Code section 65912.161, subdivision (b)(1)(B)(ii). This
         provision establishes that a city may, by ordinance, exclude sites in a TOD zone that
         is primarily comprised of a low-resource area and allows at least 40 percent of the
         aggregate density allowed under Section 65912.157, subdivision (a).

          However, based on the City’s submitted documents, and related to Findings 2
          through 4 above, HCD understands the City did not assess the densities as
          specified in Section 65912.157, subdivision (a). This issue applies to sites in several
          zones that principally or conditionally permit residential or commercial development,
          within a wetland buffer or a designated habitat preserve, or that contain more than
          two units subject to rent or price control. Therefore, HCD is unable to determine
          whether the sites would meet the criteria required by Government Code section
          65912.161, subdivision (b)(1)(B)(ii).

      Conclusion and Next Steps

      The City has until Monday, December 14, 2026 (60 days from this letter), to respond to
      these written findings. 12 Pursuant to Government Code section 65912.160, subdivision
      (d)(5), the City must consider the findings provided here and must either amend the
      ordinance to comply with HCD’s findings or enact the ordinance without changes. If the
      City chooses to enact the ordinance without changes, it must include findings in its
      resolution adopting the ordinance that explain the reasons the City believes the
      ordinance complies with Government Code section 65912.160 despite the findings
      provided by HCD. 13

      Please note, per Government Code section 65912.161, subdivision (b)(2), the City’s
      public zoning map must be updated to reflect any amendments to the sites or transit-
      oriented development zones that are and are not covered by Section 65912.157.
