      Methodology for Calculating Base Density

      The SDBL defines “maximum allowable residential density” or “base density” as the
      greatest number of units allowed under the zoning ordinance, specific plan, or land use
      element of the general plan, or if a range of density is permitted, the greatest number of
      units allowed by a specific zoning range, specific plan, or land use element of the
      general plan applicable to the project. 4 Where planning documents provide conflicting
      base densities, the statute addresses these variations by applying the highest density
      available in any one of the three competing documents.

      General Plan Land Use: The “Residential 14-22” land use designation provides for a
      maximum density of 22 dwelling units per net acre of land. 5 The City’s July 1995
      adopted general plan includes a density calculation methodology which uses a “net”
      acreage that is assumed to be approximately 80 percent of gross acreage to account for
      dedications and easements typically required with development projects. For purposes
      of “net” density, the City’s assumes a “net” acre is 35,000 square feet as opposed to a
      true acre of 43,560 square feet. 6

      Zoning Ordinance: 7 For the RMF 22 district, the City’s 1996 zoning ordinance for High
      Density Residential Districts identifies a maximum density of 22 dwellings per net acre
      of land. “Net acreage” in the City’s zoning is defined as: “[t]he total area of a site or lot
      minus any and all required dedications for public streets and rights-of-way, public parks,
      school sites, and other facilities.” 8

      Although the density expressed in units per acre appear to be equivalent (22 units per
      net acre of land), the calculation of net land area is not the same between the general
      plan land use (i.e., approximately 80 percent of gross land area) and the zoning (i.e.,

      Calculating Base Density for the Project
      The SDBL requires the calculation of base density to use “gross” acreage to yield the
      maximum number of total housing units, which includes all portions of a property owned in fee
      simple. 9
      General Plan Land Use. As explained in the methodology section above, the general
      plan density equates to 22 units per net 35,000 square feet, which scales up to 27.4
      units per gross 43,560 square feet.

      Zoning Ordinance: The net area for the project site, after subtracting areas used for
      public rights-of-way and facilities, equates to 517,912 square feet or 11.89 acres. At a
      density of 22 units per net acres, the base density allowed by zoning is 262 units. As a
      result, the zoned density equates to 262 units per 11.89 acres, or 20.3 units per gross
      43,560 square feet. 10

      Conclusion
      HCD appreciates the opportunity to provide technical assistance to implement the
      provisions of the SDBL, particularly those that inform the calculation of base density for
