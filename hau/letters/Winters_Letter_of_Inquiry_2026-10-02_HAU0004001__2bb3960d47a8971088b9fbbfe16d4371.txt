          RE: City of Winters 6th Cycle Housing Element Programs – Letter of Inquiry

          Dear Jeremy Craig:

          The purpose of this letter is to inquire about the status of the City of Winters (City)
          implementation of 6th Cycle Housing Element Programs pursuant to Government Code
          section 65585, subdivision (i). The California Department of Housing and Community
          Development (HCD) sent the City a letter on May 11, 2022, finding the City’s housing
          element in substantial compliance with Housing Element Law. 1 This was based on,
          among other reasons, the City’s commitment to implement Programs according to
          specific timelines.

          As the commitment date has passed for the programs listed below, HCD requests the
          following information:

          •    Program II.10 Zoning Ordinance Amendments - Housing Constraints Programs was
               due by December 2022. The Housing Element includes an objective to
               accommodate specialized housing types and update the City's policies and
               procedures regarding, streamlined and ministerial review for eligible affordable
               housing projects consistent with Senate Bill (SB) 35 and SB 330, ADUs, residential
               care facilities, low barrier navigation centers, transitional and supportive housing,
               manufactured homes, emergency shelters, employee housing, agricultural worker
               housing, and group homes of 7 or more people to affirmatively further fair housing
               (AFFH) in the City. While the City provided documentation to HCD regarding its
               accessory dwelling unit (ADU) ordinance, pre-approved ADU plans, and a Request
               for Proposal (RFP) for an affordable housing project, it remains unclear whether the
               City has completed the required zoning amendments or established procedures to
               accommodate specialized housing types and to streamline and provide ministerial
               review of qualifying projects consistent with the program commitments.

   The commitments under this program are detailed below:

   o Residential Care Facilities: Update the Zoning Ordinance to allow residential
     care facilities serving six or fewer persons to be treated as a residential use
     subject to the same permit requirements as a residence of the same type in all
     zoning districts, define residential care facilities serving more than six persons,
     and ensure that applicable conditions are limited to objective standards that do
     not create barriers to housing for seniors, persons with disabilities, or other
     special-needs populations.
   o Low-Barrier Navigation Centers: Define and permit low-barrier navigation centers
     consistent with Government Code Sections 65660 through 65668, including
     allowing such facilities by right in areas zoned for mixed use and nonresidential
     zones permitting multifamily uses.
   o Agricultural Worker Housing: Define agricultural worker housing; allow qualifying
     agricultural worker housing consisting of no more than 36 beds or 12
     units/spaces as an agricultural land use and permit it in the same manner as
     agricultural uses, including in the Open Space (OS) and Rural Residential (R-R)
     districts, and provide streamlined, ministerial approval for qualifying agricultural
     worker housing consistent with Health and Safety Code Section 17021.8.
   o Employee Housing: Define employee housing separately from agricultural worker
     housing and provide that employee housing serving six or fewer employees is
     deemed a single-family structure and subject to the same standards as a single-
     family residence in the same zone.
   o Transitional and Supportive Housing: Revise the Zoning Ordinance to allow
     transitional and supportive housing subject to the same standards as a residence
     of the same type and allow eligible supportive housing by-right in zones where
     multifamily and mixed uses are permitted.
   o Manufactured Homes: Remove restrictions on manufactured housing and
     factory-built homes that conflict with Government Code Section 65583.
   o Emergency Shelters: Revise parking requirements for emergency shelters to
     require sufficient parking to accommodate all staff working at the shelter,
     provided that the standards do not require more parking than other residential or
     commercial uses within the same zone.
   o Design Review: Revise design review criteria to address subjective terminology,
     including requirements concerning compatibility with the setting of the property
     and visual character of the neighborhood, through objective definitions or
     illustrations demonstrating how such terminology will be applied.
   o Height Limits and Development Standards: Review development standards in the
     R-3, R-4, C-1, C-2, D-A, and D-B zones to ensure development can occur at
     maximum densities and intensities, and revise standards that constrain
     development at maximum allowed densities. The City committed to removing or
     reducing the additional one-foot setback requirement for each foot of building

•   Program II.28 Reasonable Accommodation Program was due by September 2022:
    The Program commits the City to review its reasonable accommodation procedures
    and update procedures and findings to remove constraints consistent with the
    requirements of state law. The City previously indicated that it would review its
    zoning ordinance to identify potential constraints and address them through a
    reasonable accommodation request process. The City also noted that this program
    would be presented at an upcoming Planning Commission meeting. Please provide
    an update on the implementation of this procedure and provide relevant
    documentation including any adopted ordinance amendments, written procedures,
    resolutions, or other actions establishing or implementing the City’s reasonable
    accommodation process.

•   Program II.29 AFFH - The Housing Element identifies an ongoing program to
    facilitate equal and fair housing opportunities by implementing actions to AFFH
    services and opportunities for all persons. The City recently reported that it has
    compiled information on newly constructed homes and distributed a survey to gather
    additional data on household characteristics. The City also indicated that it intends to
    rely on the Sacramento Area Council of Governments (SACOG) for its AFFH
    analysis. Please identify the specific AFFH actions it has undertaken pursuant to this
    program and provide documentation demonstrating how these actions fulfill the
    program’s commitment. If the City intends to rely on SACOG’s AFFH analysis,
    please explain how that analysis will be used to carry out the actions identified in the
    adopted Housing Element and provide any available supporting documentation.

•   Program II.32 Zoning for a Variety of Housing Types - The Housing Element sets an
    objective to encourage qualified farmworker housing developers to pursue
    development of farmworker housing and to monitor state funding opportunities that
    support such projects. In its communication with HCD the City indicated that this
    program has not yet been started. Please provide an update on the steps the City
    has taken, or plans to take, to implement this program, including documentation of
    any efforts to identify or promote applicable state funding opportunities, as well as
    any outreach or coordination with qualified farmworker housing developers. If the
    program remains unimplemented, please provide a timeline for completion of the
    committed actions.

Consequences of Failure to Implement Programs

Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i), grants HCD authority to review any
action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is inconsistent with an
adopted housing element or Housing Element Law. This includes failure to implement
program actions included in the housing element. Failure to implement program actions

       by the statutory deadline means the City’s housing element will no longer substantially
       comply with Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial
       compliance.

       Various consequences may apply if the City does not have a housing element in
       compliance with Housing Element Law, including ineligibility or delay in receiving certain
       state funds, referral to the California Office of the Attorney General, court-imposed
       financial penalties, the loss of local land use authority to a court-appointed agent, and
       the application of the “builder’s remedy.” 2

       Conclusion

       Housing elements are essential to developing a blueprint for growth and are a vital tool
       to address California’s prolonged housing crisis. Accordingly, state law has established
       clear disincentives for local jurisdictions that fail to comply with Housing Element Law.
       To ensure the City continues to meet the 6th cycle update requirements for a
       substantially compliant housing element, the City must submit any adopted ordinances,
       resolutions or documentation that demonstrates that housing element program
       commitments have been met to HCD for review. 3

       HCD understands the City has many commitments and recognizes the challenges of
       implementation. HCD will consider any written response before taking further action
       authorized by Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i), including issuance of a
       Corrective Action Letter and removal of HCD’s finding of housing element compliance.

       Please provide a written response to this inquiry by November 2, 2026. If you have any
