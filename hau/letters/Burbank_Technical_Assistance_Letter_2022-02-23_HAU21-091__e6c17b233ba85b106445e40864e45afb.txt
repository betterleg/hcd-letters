         RE: Pickwick Gardens Housing Project – Letter of Technical Assistance

         Dear Senator Anthony Portantino:

         The purpose of this letter is to provide technical assistance regarding the Pickwick
         Gardens Housing Project (“Project”), which is proposed within the City of Burbank (“City”).
         This assistance is based in part upon conversations with you and with Emily J. Gabel.
         The California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) understands
         that there are questions regarding the eligibility of the Project for the Streamlined
         Ministerial Approval Process created by Senate Bill (SB) 35 (Chapter 366, Statutes of
         2017) and codified in Government Code section 65913.4. This letter of technical
         assistance provides HCD’s interpretation regarding the Project’s eligibility for a
         streamlined, ministerial approval process under the statute. In short, HCD concludes that
         the Project is eligible.

         In July 2021, a preliminary application for the Project, prepared pursuant to Government
         Code section 65941.1, was submitted to the City of Burbank. The Project is proposed
         on an approximately 8.44-acre site located at 921-1001 W. Riverside Drive in Burbank,
         California. In the City’s General Plan, the land use designation for that site is Rancho
         Commercial; in the City’s Municipal Code, the site is zoned Commercial Recreation.
         Existing on-site uses consist of a bowling alley, restaurant, banquet rooms, garden
         wedding venue, ice rink, and parking lots. The proposed housing development project
         includes 98 dwelling units, of which ten units are proposed for lower-income households
         (80 percent of area median income), on 5.1 acres of the 8.44-acre site. The ice rink will
         remain on site, and presumably occupies the remainder of the acreage.

         While the land use designation for the site under the General Plan’s Land Use Element
         is called “Rancho Commercial,” the primary use described for that designation allows for
         residential with some incidental commercial. The operative provisions of the General
         Plan recognize that the properties would be single-family residential, some lower
         intensity multifamily, and commercial uses. The General Plan provides:

Some have argued that the uses stated in the Land Use Element are not allowed under
the General Plan because certain densities allowed in this area require discretionary
approval — “20 units per acre with discretionary approval” — and that this language
creates the presumption that a General Plan amendment is required to allow these
activities. This view of the terms is not consistent with ordinary practice. Ordinary
practice would be either to list uses that are not allowed or to remain silent as to such
uses. Further, while the General Plan does not define “discretionary approval” per se, it
does provide that all discretionary proposals are subject to the California Environmental
Quality Act (CEQA) under Program LU-7, as well as sustainability review and noise
studies where there is excessive ambient noise. (Burbank General Plan, p. 8-8.) Given
that General Plan amendments are not a normal or typical component of discretionary
approval, if a General Plan amendment were contemplated, it seems likely that such a
requirement would have been mentioned.

While the General Plan does not define discretionary approval, the zoning code does.
Although this might not technically be controlling since the zoning code does not restrict
the General Plan — rather, the reverse would be the case — the definitions in the
zoning code are consistent with ordinary practice and are evidence of the general
understanding in the city of the meaning of “discretionary approval.” The zoning code
provides:

      DISCRETIONARY APPROVAL: Means any entitlement or approval as in
      Title 10 of the Municipal Code, including but not limited to a Conditional Use
      Permit, Variance, and subdivision map. Housing Development means one
      (1) or more groups of projects for residential units constructed or to be
      constructed in the City for sale or for rent. For the purposes of this Division,
      “HOUSING DEVELOPMENT” also includes a subdivision, planned unit
      development, or Condominium Project, the substantial rehabilitation and

       conversion of an existing commercial building to residential use, and the

(Burbank Mun. Code, § 10-1-633.) Thus, any number of approvals might be needed as

Under the statute, a qualifying project is only subject to applicable “objective standards”
in the general plan and zoning code. (See, e.g., Gov. Code, § 65913.4, subd. (a).)
Where the objective standards in the general plan and zoning ordinance conflict,
Government Code section 65913.4, subdivision (a)(5)(B), provides that the project is
deemed consistent if it is consistent with objective standards in the general plan. Here,
the standards in the General Plan allow housing (as noted above) and the standards in
the zoning code do not. HCD understands that the property is zoned Commercial-
Recreational (C-R). Burbank Municipal Code section 10-1-501 defines what uses are
allowed in various zones. It provides expressly that “uses in all zones are only allowed
as described in the use table comprising Section 10-1-502.” The Table in section 10-1-
502 clearly indicates that residential is not a permitted or discretionary use in C-R zone.
In order for the zoning to be consistent with the General Plan, the site would have to be
subject to a rezone directly or through a planned development (Burbank Mun. Code, §
10-1-19121 [discussing regulations and standards for the “PD” zone]) to facilitate
housing. This seems to present a direct conflict. Thus, the General Plan standards
control. The Project appears to be consistent with the objective standards in the
General Plan and thus would, by operation of law, be deemed consistent with applicable
objective General Plan standards.

In sum:

   •   The General Plan “allows” residential uses on property with the Rancho
       Commercial designation, although discretionary approval is required for a
       maximum density and intensity of 0.60 FAR, 20 units per acre. (Burbank General
       Plan, p. 3-16.) The zoning code does not. (See, e.g., Burbank Mun. Code, §§ 10-
       1-501 [“uses in all zones are only allowed as described in the use table
       comprising Section 10-1-502”], 10-1-502 [residential is not an allowed use in C-R
       zone].)
   •   State law allows projects pursuant to densities allowed in the general plan
       regardless of underlying zoning, because where the two conflict (as here), the
       objective standards of the general plan prevail. (Gov. Code, § 65913.4, subd.
       (a)(5)(B).)
   •   The term “discretionary approval” does not refer to legislative decisions like
       general plan amendments, but to CEQA review, which is mentioned in the
       General Plan (Burbank General Plan, p. 8-8), and potentially to things like

       conditional use permits (CUP), which are mentioned in the zoning code.
       (Burbank Mun. Code, § 10-1-633.) In other words, the term applies to actions
       that may be taken in an adjudicatory fashion with the appropriate findings. Where
       a use is not allowed without a general plan amendment, a general plan would, as
       here, be silent as to the use, as is common practice.
   •   The purpose of SB 35 was to allow local jurisdictions to control housing
       development through objective standards, but to eliminate discretionary
       processes that delay or de facto prohibit housing during the housing crisis. (See,
       e.g., Gov. Code, § 65913.4, subds. (a)(5), (b).) Neither the statute nor the
       Streamlined Ministerial Approval Process Guidelines are intended to be so
       narrowly construed as to apply only to where uses are permitted outright or with
       a CUP. Rather, SB 35 was intended to streamline discretionary review processes
       generally.
   •   It is the policy of the State per Government Code section 65913.4, subdivision
       (n), that the statute “be interpreted and implemented in a manner to afford the
       fullest possible weight to the interest of, and the approval and provision of,
       increased housing supply.”

It is HCD’s determination based upon the statute and referenced in HCD’s Updated
Streamlined Ministerial Approval Process Guidelines, that the streamlined ministerial
process applies to the Pickwick Gardens Housing Project.
