         RE: Glendale - Senate Bill 9 - Notice of Violation

         On February 10, 2026, the California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) sent a Letter of Technical Assistance to the City of Glendale (City)
         regarding the number of units permitted on a parcel for purposes of Senate Bill (SB) 9.
         In the letter, HCD advised the City that, absent a lot split, a property owner may
         construct up to two primary dwellings in addition to the maximum number of Accessory
         Dwelling Units (ADU) permitted under ADU law. HCD also clarified that the term “units”
         as used in Government Code section 65852.21 refers only to primary dwellings. Primary
         units and ADUs are separate and distinct from each other.

         On March 9, 2026, the City responded via email, informing HCD that the City’s position
         remains unchanged. The City states, “We understand that HCD interprets the SB9
         housing development state statute … and the SB9 lot split state statute … differently
         than the City does with respect to the legality of defining “units” as ADUs and JADUs,
         however, the City cannot approve a development that violates its municipal code.” The
         City’s response indicated no change in its view of the 1316 North Pacific Avenue
         application’s eligibility for SB 9. Lastly, the City’s response also did not indicate that the
         City has any plans to amend the relevant SB 9 regulations in their municipal code as
         requested by HCD.

         HCD has reviewed the City’s position and reiterates that regardless of what the City’s code
         may currently state, pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (j), the City’s
         actions must comply with Government Code sections 65852.21 and 66411.7.

         Additionally, HCD finds that for sites that utilize SB 9, the city’s code also limits the
         development of ADUs in conflict with state ADU law 1. The City’s SB 9 regulations state,
         “A SB 9 housing development is permitted to have a maximum of two units on a parcel

      in a single-family residential zone…”. 2 The City’s SB 9 regulations further define “unit” to
      include SB 9 units, ADUs and Junior ADUs (JADU). 3 Therefore, the City’s regulations
      not only prevent the development of two SB 9 units (i.e., primary units) on a single-
      family lot where an ADU has already been built, they also limit the development of
      ADUs and JADUs when a site is developed with one or two SB 9 units.

      Background

      On March 28, 2025, the property owner attempted to submit an SB 9 application
      proposing to add a single-family dwelling to the parcel which currently contains one
      single-family dwelling and one ADU. The City refused to accept the application for
      approval or denial (as required by Government Code section 65852.21, subdivision
      (h)(1)) and made a determination that the property was not eligible for additional SB 9
      units or ADUs/JADUs. In an email from the City to the Applicant dated April 11, 2025,
      the City explained its refusal to accept the application for approval or denial: “Per
      Glendale Municipal Code (GMC) 30.34.110(D)(2), A SB 9 housing development is
      permitted to have a maximum of two units on a parcel in a single-family residential
      zone... Further, per GMC 30.34.110(C), a "Unit" includes a primary dwelling, an
      accessory dwelling unit, or a junior accessory dwelling unit. Based on available records,
      the subject property is already developed with two units (a primary dwelling and an
      accessory dwelling unit). If an additional unit for the existing property is desired, then a
      concurrent SB 9 lot split application is required to be submitted in addition to PSB9-
      004777-2025.”

      On August 29, 2025, HCD provided verbal technical assistance to the City that absent a
      lot split, under SB 9 the City is required to allow two primary dwellings and however
      many ADUs are allowed under ADU law. HCD noted that a local agency shall not be
      required to permit more than two units on a parcel only if an SB 9 urban lot split has
      also occurred. While SB 9 does not define a unit, it does distinguish between
      “residential units” and “accessory dwelling units.” Separate standards applicable to ADU
      development demonstrate that the term “units” used in Government Code section
      65852.21 is intended to mean primary dwellings unless specifically identified as ADUs.

      Conclusion

      The City has indicated to HCD that it intends to continue implementing the City’s
      adopted SB 9 regulations which limit the number of units on a parcel that was not
      created through an urban lot split. Additionally, the City has not indicated any plans to
      amend the adopted regulations. By continuing to limit the number of units on a parcel
      not created through an SB 9 lot split to two, the City’s actions are in violation of SB 9
      and ADU law.

Under Government Code section 65585, HCD must notify a local government when that
local government takes actions that violate SB 9 and may notify the California Office of
the Attorney General.

The City has until October 21, 2026 to provide a written response to this letter. In its
response, the City should include a specific plan and timeline for corrective action,
including the amendment of the City’s municipal code to align with state law. HCD will
consider any written response before taking further action authorized by Government
Code section 65585, subdivision (j), including, but not limited to, referral to the California
Office of the Attorney General.

Additionally, HCD reminds the City that Assembly Bill 712 (Chapter 496, Statutes of
2025) went into effect on January 1, 2026. Among other provisions, the new law
increases penalties that a court may impose on a local government if the local
government was advised in writing by the Attorney General or HCD prior to the
commencement of a lawsuit that the local government’s decision, action, or inaction
would represent a violation of specified housing laws.
