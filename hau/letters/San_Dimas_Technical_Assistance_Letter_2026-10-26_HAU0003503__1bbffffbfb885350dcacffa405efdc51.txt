         The California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) received a
         request for technical assistance from the applicant of the project located at 736 N Walnut
         Avenue (“Project”). The applicant would like to apply the guidance provided by a prior
         HCD technical assistance letter sent to the City of San Diego 1, which states that an
         existing unit may be “sectioned off” as a remainder parcel under a Starter Home
         Revitalization Act (SHRA) 2 application, and the rest of the parcel should then be
         considered vacant for the purposes of the SHRA. HCD understands that the City of
         San Dimas (“City”) does not agree with this guidance but also had other questions for how
         to process applications that propose a remainder parcel, including whether future
         development can occur on remainder parcels and if local standards may be applied to
         remainder parcels. This letter serves to re-state HCD’s prior guidance while also
         addressing the City’s questions.

         Background

         The Project proposes to construct ten units on ten lots in addition to a remainder parcel.
         The remainder parcel contains an existing single-family home. The project site is in a
         single-family zone and is 1.1 acres in size. Of the 1.1 acres, 0.5 will be dedicated to the
         ten SHRA units, while the other 0.6 will remain with the existing residence as the
         remainder parcel. The applicant has also inquired about potentially using SB 9 to create
         additional units on the remainder parcel after the completion of the SHRA project.

         HCD understands that the City considers the lot ineligible under the SHRA, stating that the
         lot does not meet the definition of “vacant” under Government Code section

         Question 1: Can an applicant designate an existing single-family residence or
         structure as a “remainder parcel” such that the rest of the site is considered
         “vacant” and therefore eligible for the provisions under the SHRA?

         As stated in prior referenced HCD guidance, a proposed subdivision may designate a
         remainder parcel that includes an existing single-family residence. Assembly Bill (AB) 130,
         signed on June 25, 2025, establishes that such a remainder parcel must “retain[ ] existing
         land uses or structures, . . . not contain any new residential units, and . . . not [be]
         exclusively dedicated to serving the housing development project.” 3

         The vacancy requirement established under the SHRA was intended to prevent the
         demolition of viable housing. By allowing the designation of a remainder parcel to retain an
         existing residence or other use, the statute provides a clear mechanism to preserve
         existing housing stock while still enabling subdivision and the production of new housing
         opportunities. The statutory language governing remainder parcels is written broadly to
         expressly allow the retention of “existing land uses or structures”, including an existing
         single-family residence. Importantly, the remainder parcel is excluded from the ten-parcel
         cap as well as density calculations, 4 demonstrating the intent for remainder parcels to be
         treated distinctly from the proposed subdivision and development. Accordingly, once a
         remainder parcel is designated to retain an existing use, the site area outside of the
         remainder parcel may be considered “vacant” for purposes of eligibility under the SHRA.
         The site area for the proposed subdivision and development is still required to meet all
         criteria under the SHRA, including minimum lot sizes.

         Question 2: Can development occur on a remainder parcel created by a SHRA
         project?

         Yes, in most cases. After completion of the SHRA project, the remainder parcel is eligible
         for development in the same manner as any other parcel. This is true whether the
         remainder parcel is vacant or has an existing unit (e.g., allowing an ADU). The exception
         being that the remainder parcel cannot be further divided through SHRA or SB 9. 5

         Question 3: Can a parcel be developed as a SHRA development if the parcel has
         been previously developed or entitled pursuant to an SB 9 Two-Unit development
         with no lot split?

         Yes, as long as there was no lot split under the SB 9 project, the parcel is eligible under
         SHRA. The SB 9 development may be sectioned off as a remainder parcel.

         Question 4: Can the City require the remainder parcel created as part of a SHRA
         development to comply with existing development standards (i.e., minimum lot
         size, length and width, setbacks, lot coverage) required by the underlying zone?

         The remainder parcel and existing structures are not subject to the applicable
         development standards required by the underlying zone at the time of the SHRA
         subdivision. However, a local government may deny the issuance of a parcel map,
         tentative map, or final map so long as it makes certain written findings that the project will
         create an adverse impact to public health and safety that cannot be mitigated (e.g.,
         emergency access, building/fire code, etc.). 6

         Once the SHRA development is complete, any future development on the remainder
         parcel would be subject to all development standards required by the underlying zone. In
         other words, SHRA standards only apply at the time of the SHRA subdivision. Following
         the subdivision, the remainder parcel is treated like any other parcel in its zone.

         Conclusion

         The City’s position is inconsistent with the provisions of the SHRA because the statute
         allows a site with an existing dwelling to be sectioned off through a remainder parcel to
         qualify the rest of the parcel as “vacant”. Existing structures on the remainder parcel are
         subject to SHRA standards at the time of the SHRA project. However, all future
         development on the remainder parcel is subject to local development standards
         associated with the underlying zone.

         HCD reminds the City of its enforcement authority over the SHRA, among other state
         housing laws. 7 Accordingly, HCD may review local government actions to determine
         consistency with these laws. If HCD finds that a jurisdiction’s actions do not comply with
         state law, HCD may notify the California Office of the Attorney General that the local
         government is in violation of state law.
