         Dear Zach Rehm:

         The purpose of this letter is to provide technical assistance regarding the
         implementation of Program 1 (Provide Adequate Sites and Monitoring for No Net Loss)
         to rezone sites to accommodate the Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA)
         pursuant to Government Code section 65583, Subdivision (c)(1)(A).

         On March 17, 2025 the California Department of Housing and Community Development
         (HCD) sent a Letter of Inquiry to the City of San Clemente (City) regarding the
         completion of Program 1 and Program 2A (By-Right Approval for Projects with 20
         Percent Affordable Units). The City responded with documentation for HCDs review on
         March 18, 2025, and provided supplemental documentation per HCD’s request on
         October 16, 2025. Based on the documentation provided by the City, HCD has
         determined that while the City has taken steps to plan for adequate capacity, additional
         actions are needed to ensure compliance with Government Code section 65583.2,
         subdivisions (c), (h) and (i).

         Specifically, while it appears that more than 50 percent of the City’s identified RHNA
         shortfall is met on mixed-use sites and the ordinance does state that mixed-use sites
         permit 100 percent residential development, it does not appear to require a minimum of
         50 percent of the square footage in a mixed-use development to be residential. The
         ordinance also does not indicate if the City permits owner-occupied and rental
         multifamily uses by-right for developments in which 20 percent or more of the units are
         affordable to lower-income households. By-right means local government review must
         not require a conditional use permit, planned unit development permit, or other
         discretionary review or approval that that would constitute a “project” for purposes of
         Division 13 (commencing with Section 21000) of the Public Resources Code. 1 The
         submitted ordinances must be revised to allow for by-right development and clearly
         include the definition of ‘by-right’ in its revisions.

         Additionally, the rezone documentation submitted by the City appears to be missing the
         required minimum densities for sites being used to meet the lower-income RHNA
         shortfall. While the sites in the City’s Housing Overlay currently allow projects to
         develop at a maximum density of over 20 dwelling units per acre, there is no minimum
         density requirement to ensure compliance with Government Code section 65583.2,
         subdivision (h). The submitted ordinances must be revised to ensure all sites being
         rezoned to satisfy the City’s lower- income RHNA shortfall permit a minimum of 20
         dwelling units per acre by right.

         Furthermore, please be aware the California appellate decision in Martinez v. City of
         Clovis found that while overlays can be used in a rezone, when the base zone allows
         residential development, both the base zone and the overlay zone must comply with the
         minimum density requirements of Government Code section 65583.2, subdivision (h). 2
         In addition, the City should also consult with legal counsel on the applicability of New
         New Commune DTLA LLC v. City of Redondo Beach decision related to the
         establishment of minimum densities in base zones. 3

         HCD requests that the City revise its zoning ordinance or provide written documentation
         clearly demonstrating compliance with these statutory requirements. If needed, a copy
         of the revised ordinance should be transmitted to HCD. HCD will review the
         documentation and issue correspondence before taking any action authorized by
         Government Code section 65585, subdivisions (i) and (j). Such action may include
         issuance of written findings (Corrective Action Letter) and revoking HCD’s finding that
         the City’s housing element is in substantial compliance with Housing Element Law
         and/or referral to the California Office of the Attorney General.

         HCD provides the City until May 29, 2026 to provide a written response to these
         findings. If you have questions or need additional information, please contact
         Dexter Egleston at Dexter.Egleston@hcd.ca.gov.
