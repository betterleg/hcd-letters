          RE: City of Los Angeles – AB 2011 Letter of Technical Assistance

          This letter provides the City of Los Angeles (City) with technical assistance regarding
          the Affordable Housing and High Road Jobs Act (AB 2011). 1 The California Department
          of Housing and Community Development (HCD) understands that the City of Los
          Angeles recently denied a housing development application that was seeking
          streamlined ministerial approval under AB 2011. HCD has reviewed the City’s “Final
          Determination of Ineligibility for Ministerial Processing,” dated August 20, 2026, as well
          as the Planning Memorandum prepared by the City’s Environmental Specialist, dated
          August 19, 2026, which serves as the basis for that determination. In its final
          determination, the City claims there is “substantial evidence of habitat for protected
          species” under Government Code section 65913.4, subdivision (a)(6)(J). This rationale
          appears to improperly expand the statutory standard, effectively undermining the intent
          of AB 2011, and is not supported by the law.

          approximately 19.9-acre site zoned “Agriculture” (A1-1XL)2 and currently operating as
          a private golf course. The applicant is seeking streamlined ministerial approval under
          AB 2011 and has submitted a complete development application to the City, including
          two biological assessments prepared by qualified professionals. These assessments
          evaluated environmental and biological resources across the site, including the

      Site Requirements Pursuant to Government Code Section 65913.4

      Under AB 2011, local agencies must provide a by-right, ministerial approval process for
      qualifying multifamily affordable and mixed-income housing developments within zones
      where office, retail, or parking are principally permitted uses. 3 These provisions apply
      only to parcels that satisfy the requirements in Government Code section 65913.4, 4
      including the requirement that the “development is not located on a site that is …
      [h]abitat for protected species identified as candidate, sensitive, or specific of special
      statute by state or federal agencies....” 5 The City of Los Angeles determined that the
      proposed project site is exempt from AB 2011 based on the City-issued report that the
      site “provides habitat functions relevant to the Southern California mountain lion,” a
      candidate species under the California Endangered Species Act.

      However, the City’s determination is based on the report’s identification of potential or
      context-dependent ecological functions, not evidence that mountain lions actually use or
      occupy the property. The report concedes the lack of any affirmative evidence of
      mountain lion presence but argues that the absence of telemetry data “does not show
      that no mountain lion ever entered or used the property.” While that is true, the
      observation does not establish the opposite, that mountain lions do use the site or that it
      does serve as a habitat. Likewise, the report further acknowledges that the site “is a
      modified and actively managed urban landscape; it is not equivalent to intact natural
      communities” found elsewhere in the Santa Monica Mountains. Habitat is land that
      provides food, shelter, and other resources necessary to support the species. 6 By
      describing the site as a significantly altered urban landscape with no demonstrated
      mountain lion presence, the report undermines its own conclusions. 7

      Further, the statute says, “is … habitat,” not “may provide functions relevant to …
      habitat.” 8 The exemption applies only to parcels that are actually serving as habitat, not
      parcels that are merely relevant to, might serve, or theoretically provide ecological
      functions. Yet the report on which the City’s determination is based defines “habitat” in
      terms of potential capability rather than actual use. The author explicitly frames their
      analysis on whether the site “provides resources, conditions, or functions relevant to”
      mountain lions, which is an assessment of theoretical capacity and not evidence of
      actual habitat utilization, and therefore is not a valid basis to deem the project ineligible.
      Under Government Code section 65913.4, subdivision (a)(6)(J), conjectural potential is
      insufficient. The City must demonstrate that the site actually serves as a habitat, for
      which the report offers no such evidence.

      Standard of Review Pursuant to Government Code Section 65912.124

      AB 2011 establishes a clear and predictable evidentiary framework governing
      consistency determinations. Government Code section 65912.124, subdivision (b)(1) 9
      provides that a development is consistent with objective planning standards when the
      application contains “substantial evidence that would allow a reasonable person to
      conclude that the development is consistent with objective planning standards.”

      Here, the applicant has met that standard. As part of the full application submittal, the
      applicant provided two biological assessments prepared by qualified professionals,
      including a “Biological Assessment for AB 2011” dated July 17, 2026, completed by
      South Environmental, a firm on the City’s own list of Qualified Biological Consultants. 10
      These assessments rely on recognized scientific methodologies and provide well-
      supported conclusions that the site does not constitute a mountain lion habitat. The
      City’s own statement acknowledges the key facts established in those materials: “the
      property’s management as a golf course, the absence of documented mountain lion
      GPS locations on the property, and the absence of identified continuous intact wildlife
      corridor across it.”11 Under the reasonable person standard found in AB 2011, this
      serves as substantial evidence demonstrating that the site does not function as a
      mountain lion habitat.

      AB 2011 requires substantial evidence of actual habitat conditions. The applicant’s
      assessments clearly established the absence of such conditions—including the lack of
      documented mountain lion use, the developed nature of the landscape, and the limited
      cover capable of supporting mountain lion behavior—that would allow a reasonable

AB 712 Enforcement Actions—Fines and Penalties

Additionally, HCD would like to remind the City that AB 712 (Chapter 496, Statutes of
2025) went into effect on January 1, 2026, under Government Code section 65914.2.
Among other provisions, this law increases the penalties that a court may impose on a
local government if, prior to the commencement of a lawsuit alleging a violation of state
housing laws such as AB 2011, the local government was advised in writing by the
Attorney General or HCD that the local government’s decision, action, or inaction would
represent a violation of those laws. This letter serves to inform the City that the City’s
determination of the Project’s ineligibility under AB 2011, as set forth above, is a
violation of AB 2011.

Conclusion and Next Steps

Under Government Code section 65585, subdivision (j), HCD must notify local
government when that local government takes actions that violate AB 2011 and other
laws that HCD enforces and may notify the California Office of Attorney General of
these and other violations of state housing laws. As outlined above, HCD has concluded
that the City’s determination that the proposed project is ineligible for ministerial
processing under AB 2011 appears to be in violation of state housing law.

HCD requests that the City review this correspondence and provide a written response
no later than November 25, 2026. HCD will review and consider the City’s written
response before taking any action authorized by Government Code section 65585,
subdivisions (i) and (j). As noted above, such action could include referral to the Office
of Attorney General.
