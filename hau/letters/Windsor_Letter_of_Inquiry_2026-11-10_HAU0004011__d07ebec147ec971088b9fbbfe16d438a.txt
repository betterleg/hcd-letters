         RE: Town of Windsor 6th Cycle Housing Element Programs – Letter of Inquiry

         Dear Patrick N. Streeter:

         The purpose of this letter is to inquire about the status of the Town of Windsor’s (Town)
         implementation of 6th Cycle Housing Element Programs pursuant to Government Code
         section 65585, subdivision (i). The California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) sent the Town a letter on August 31, 2023, finding the Town’s
         housing element in substantial compliance with Housing Element Law. 1 This was based
         on, among other reasons, the Town’s commitment to implement Programs according to
         specific timelines.

         As the commitment date has passed for the programs listed below, HCD requests the
         following information:

         •    Program H-3 (Lot Consolidation and Small Site Development) commits the Town to
              facilitate the consolidation of small lots for the development of mixed-use and
              multifamily developments, particularly for affordable housing, by the end of 2024.
              Based on the Town’s 2025 Annual Progress Report (APR) and discussions with
              HCD, the Town has not yet completed this commitment. Please provide HCD with a
              specific and detailed timeline for implementing the remaining commitments of this
              program, including but not limited to, adoption of lot consolidation incentives to
              facilitate mixed use development.

         •    Program H-5 (Inclusionary Housing Ordinance Update) commits the Town to
              conduct a nexus study by December 2024 and determine the appropriate in-lieu fees
              and adopt amendments by July 2025. Based on the Town’s 2025 APR and
              discussions with HCD, the Town has not yet completed this commitment. Please
              provide HCD with a specific and detailed timeline for implementing the remaining
              commitments of this program, including but not limited to, adoption of amendments
              to the Inclusionary Housing Ordinance.

•   Program H-7 (ADU Production) commits the Town to determine if production
    estimates of Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU’s) and Junior Accessory Dwelling Units
    (JADU’s) are meeting production estimates by 2026. Based on the Town’s 2025
    Annual Progress Report (APR) and discussions with HCD, the Town has completed
    outreach and demonstrated measurable ADU/JADU development activity. However,
    it remains unclear whether the Town has completed the commitment to evaluate
    whether actual ADU/JADU production is meeting projected production levels. Please
    provide HCD with a specific and detailed timeline for implementing the remaining
    commitments of this program, including but not limited to, determining if production
    estimates of ADU’s and JADU’s are meeting production estimates, particularly with
    respect to affordability to lower- or moderate-income households.

•   Program H-9 (Special Needs Housing) commits the Town to revise the Zoning Code
    to accommodate inclusion of units for lower-income and special needs groups in
    new development by providing the necessary zoning incentives by June 2026.
    Based on the Town’s 2025 APR and discussions with HCD, the Town has not yet
    completed this commitment. Please provide HCD with a specific and detailed
    timeline for implementing the remaining commitments of this program, including but
    not limited to, amending the Zoning Code to incentivize the development of housing
    units affordable to lower-income households and to address the housing needs of
    special needs population in new residential development.

•   Program H-22 (Shiloh Road Village Vision Plan Update) commits the Town to sunset
    the Shiloh Road Village Vision Plan and amend the General Plan concurrent with the
    adoption of the Housing Element on August 16, 2023. Based on the Town’s 2025
    APR and discussions with HCD, the Town has not yet completed these
    commitments. Please provide HCD with a specific and detailed timeline for
    completing the remaining commitments under this program, including, but not limited
    to, formally sunsetting the Shiloh Road Village Vision Plan and amending the
    General Plan accordingly.

•   Program H-31 (Transitional Housing, Supportive Housing, and Residential Care
    Homes) commits the Town to regularly apply to notices of funding availability for
    State and federal sources to support developers of transitional and supportive
    housing facilities to house at least 25 households in predominantly single-family
    neighborhoods (i.e., Michael Hall Park and Foothills Regional Park neighborhoods).
    Based on the Town’s 2025 APR and discussions with HCD, the Town has
    successfully supported the development of transitional and supportive housing
    facilities during the planning period. Please provide HCD with a specific and detailed
    timeline for fulfilling the ongoing commitments under this program, including, but not
    limited to, continuing to pursue applicable State and federal funding opportunities
    and supporting the development of transitional and supportive housing facilities in
    the Michael Hall Park and Foothills Regional Park neighborhoods.

•   Program H-32 (Farmworker Housing) commits the Town to regularly apply to notices
    of funding availability for State and federal sources to support developers of
    farmworker projects. Based on the Town’s 2025 APR and discussions with HCD, the
    Town has not yet completed this commitment. Please provide HCD with a specific
    and detailed timeline for implementing the remaining commitments of this program,
    including but not limited to, supporting the development of farmworker housing
    projects through the regular pursuit of applicable State and federal funding
    opportunities.

•   Program H-37 (Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing) commits the Town to develop
    an Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH) Plan to support affordable housing
    development for special-needs groups throughout the Town by annually conducting
    outreach events and meeting with relevant regional agencies to close gaps in
    services to extremely low-income households. Based on the Town’s 2025 APR and
    discussions with HCD, the Town has demonstrated ongoing regional collaboration,
    including coordination with the Windsor Unified School District. However, it remains
    unclear whether the Town has fully completed all commitments under this program,
    including development and implementation of an AFFH Plan and outreach efforts
    specifically intended to support affordable housing development for special-needs
    groups. Please provide HCD with a specific and detailed timeline for implementing
    the remaining commitments under this program to support affordable housing
    development for special-needs groups.

•   Program H-41 (Multifamily Development Fee Review) commits the Town to analyze
    development fees for multifamily housing by August 2025 to ensure such fees are
    reasonable and do not unduly constrain development while protecting the quality,
    health, and public safety of the community. Based on the Town’s 2025 APR and
    discussions with HCD, the Town has adopted a time-limited development impact fee
    pilot program for multifamily developments. However, it remains unclear whether the
    Town has completed the required analysis of development fees and identified any
    long-term actions necessary to ensure fees do not constrain multifamily housing
    development. Please provide HCD with a specific and detailed timeline for
    implementing the remaining commitments of this program, including but not limited
    to, identifying any permanent or ongoing measures, such as extending the
    development impact fee program or identifying an alternative fee waiver strategy to
    encourage the development of multifamily projects.

Consequences of Failure to Implement Programs

Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i), grants HCD authority to review any
action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is inconsistent with an
adopted housing element or Housing Element Law. This includes failure to implement
program actions included in the housing element. Failure to implement program actions
by the statutory deadline means the Town’s housing element will no longer substantially

       comply with Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial
       compliance.

       Various consequences may apply if the Town does not have a housing element in
       compliance with Housing Element Law, including ineligibility or delay in receiving certain
       state funds, referral to the California Office of the Attorney General, court-imposed
       financial penalties, the loss of local land use authority to a court-appointed agent, and
       the application of the “builder’s remedy.” 2

       Conclusion

       Housing elements are essential to developing a blueprint for growth and are a vital tool
       to address California’s prolonged housing crisis. Accordingly, state law has established
       clear disincentives for local jurisdictions that fail to comply with Housing Element Law.
       To ensure the Town continues to meet the 6th cycle update requirements for a
       substantially compliant housing element, the Town must submit any adopted
       ordinances, resolutions or documentation that demonstrates that housing element
       program commitments have been met to HCD for review. 3

       HCD understands the Town has many commitments and recognizes the challenges of
       implementation. HCD will consider any written response before taking further action
       authorized by Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i), including issuance of a
       Corrective Action Letter and removal of HCD’s finding of housing element compliance.
