           RE: City of Goleta’s Initiation of a General Plan Amendment for 625 Dara Road
               and Housing Element Requirements – Letter of Technical Assistance
           HCD has become aware of an application to initiate a general plan amendment for
           property located at 625 Dara Road and the landowner’s desire to develop senior
           housing on the site1. HCD works to maintain and expand technical assistance on state
           laws and strategies that promote zoning for a variety of housing types. The purpose of
           this correspondence is to provide technical assistance to decision-makers that
           describes how approval of the project would align with multiple requirements of State
           Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code) as well as implement several of
           Goleta’s (City) housing element policies and programs.
           Housing Supply for Special Needs
           Currently there is an inadequate supply and production of affordable homes available to
           lower-income households and homes for persons in protected classes, including
           housing for seniors. State Housing Element Law contains multiple requirements related
           to the development of housing for seniors including:

               •    An analysis of special needs populations, including those of the elderly
                    population (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(7));
               •    An analysis and provision of zoning that accommodates a variety of housing
                    types (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd. (c));
               •    An analysis of any potential governmental constraints to the development of
                    housing (Gov. Code § 65583, subd. (a)(5)); and
               •    Inclusion of program actions that:
                        o Encourage a variety of housing types (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1));
                        o Assist in the development of housing (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2));
                        o Remove governmental constraints (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3)); and
                        o Affirmatively further fair housing (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(10)).

           Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing

           Assembly Bill (AB) 686 (Chapter 958, Statutes of 2018) amended Government Code
           section 8899.50 to strengthen California’s commitment to fair housing and access to
           opportunity by mandating that all public agencies must affirmatively further fair housing
           through their housing and community development programs. Government Code
           section 8899.50, subdivision (a)(1), defines, “affirmatively furthering fair housing” as
           taking meaningful actions, in addition to combating discrimination, that overcome
           patterns of segregation and foster inclusive communities free from barriers that restrict
           access to opportunity based on protected characteristics. The duty to affirmatively
           further fair housing extends to all of a public agency’s activities and programs relating to
           housing and community development. Public agencies are required to take meaningful
           actions to affirmatively further fair housing. While HUD has since suspended the rule at
           the federal level, Government Code section 8899.50 maintains this obligation for entities
           in California with this language: “[s]ubsequent amendment, suspension, or revocation of
           this Final Rule or its accompanying commentary by the federal government shall not
           impact the interpretation of this section.” The development of senior housing is an
           important strategy for creating an inclusive community and affirmatively furthering fair
           housing.

It is important to note that the City reports through its 2019 Annual Progress Report
(APR) that it has permitted housing for only 2.1% of its Regional Housing Need
Allocation (RHNA) for very-low income households; only 51.6% of its RHNA for low-
income households; and only 9.2% of its RHNA for moderate-income households while
permitting 287.7% of its RHNA for above-moderate income households. The APR
demonstrates a strong preference for building market-rate and single family residences
over meeting the needs of its lower-income and special needs residents. This pattern
fails to demonstrate the City meeting its obligations to implement its housing element
and to affirmatively further fair housing.

The development of senior housing is an important strategy for creating an inclusive
community, achieving housing element goals, implementing housing element programs,
and affirmatively furthering fair housing. HCD supports actions to initiate the general
plan amendment for 625 Dara Road and encourages the City to take action in support
of the goals, policies, and programs contained in its adopted housing element.

Application Processing

In addition, HCD is concerned with the processes and procedures required by the City
for this project. Under common practice, a housing project application is submitted to a
jurisdiction and a general plan amendment, if required, is processed as part of the
housing project approval. In the case of 625 Dara Road, the senior housing project is
described in the Staff Report and is cited as the impetus for the request to implement a
general plan amendment. However, approval of the general plan amendment is an
action that is separate and apart from approval of the senior housing project.

City Council’s deliberation of item B-1 at the July 21, 2020 City Council meeting was not
limited to consideration of initiating the general plan amendment, but also included
discussion and consideration of the senior housing project as well as the potential effect
on the City’s 6th cycle RHNA and upcoming housing element update.

As structured, the process subjects the senior housing project to multiple reviews, thus
affording the City multiple opportunities to deny the housing project at varying points in
the process. This creates uncertainty for the landowner, lengthens the time for approval,
adds costs to development, and potentially demonstrates bad faith on the part of the
City. HCD supports the actions to initiate the general plan amendment for 625 Dara
Road and encourages the City to expedite and approve any subsequent application to
develop senior housing on the site.
