         RE: City of Turlock’s 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Adopted Housing Element and Rezone

         Thank you for submitting the City of Turlock’s (City) housing element, which was
         adopted August 26, 2026, and received for review on August 27, 2026. The California
         Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) also received Resolution
         Number 2026-110, Ordinance Numbers 1351-CS and 1352-CS and other planning
         documents (Zoning Documents) related to rezoning intended to accommodate the
         regional housing need allocation (RHNA). Pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, HCD is reporting the results of its review.

         On July 3, 2026, HCD found the revised draft housing element met statutory
         requirements of State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq). The adopted
         element was found to be substantially the same as the revised draft element that HCD’s
         July 3, 2026 review determined met statutory requirements and continues to meet
         statutory requirements. However, the housing element cannot be found in substantial
         compliance until the City had completed necessary rezones (Programs 1-B – Rezoning
         to Accommodate RHNA Shortfall and 2-E - By Right Rezoning of Sites from Prior
         Inventories) to accommodate the regional housing needs allocation (RHNA). The
         Zoning Documents are intended to meet these rezoning requirements and do meet
         most requirements such as permitting eligible developments without discretionary action
         on sites identified in prior planning periods. However, the Zoning Documents do not
         meet all statutory requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65583.2,
         subdivisions (h) and (i). Specifically, the Zoning Documents must be revised, as follows:

              •   Non-residential Uses: Pursuant to Government Code section 65583.2,
                  subdivision (h), given the City’s mixed-use approach, sites rezoned to
                  accommodate the lower-income RHNA must require that residential uses occupy
                  50 percent of the total floor area of a mixed-use development. However,
                  Ordinance Number 1351-CS appears to permit 100 percent non-residential uses.

       The Zoning Documents should be revised to not permit or allow 100 percent non-
       residential uses.

   •   Shortfall Sites and Streamlined Ministerial Approval Processes (SMAP):
       Currently, the Zoning Documents conflate sites intended to accommodate the
       RHNA (Shortfall Sites - Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i)) and
       developments that are proposed pursuant to Government Code section 65913.4
       (SMAP Sites). However, these processes differ and eligible developments should
       not be required to meet both processes. For example, residential performance
       standards are a minimum of 50 percent for Shortfall Sites and are two-thirds for
       SMAP Sites. The Zoning Documents should be clarified or revised to de-couple
       the different processes.

Further, allowable heights in the Commercial-Residential Mixed Use Zones (CRMU) of
2 stories up to 30 feet in specified areas appear to be constraints on development and
the ability to achieve maximum allowable densities. The City should evaluate the
impacts of these heights on the ability to facilitate maximum densities and make
adjustments as appropriate as part amendments to the associated zoning documents.
For example, increasing heights by one story would facilitate achieving maximum
allowable densities.

Once the City has amended the Zoning Documents to address the above requirements,
the City should submit all pertinent documentation for approval. HCD will review the
documentation and issue correspondence identifying the updated status of the City’s
housing element compliance.

Please be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD authority
to review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
inconsistent with an adopted housing element or Housing Element Law. This includes
failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke
housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state
law.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

HCD appreciates the hard work and commitment of the City in taking significant steps
toward a compliant housing element and is committed to assisting the City in
addressing all statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law. If you have any
questions or need additional technical assistance, please contact Andrea Grant, of our
staff, at Andrea.Grant@hcd.ca.gov.
