         RE: County of Tehama’s 6th Cycle (2024-2029) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the County of Tehama’s (County) revised draft housing
         element received for review on April 10, 2025. Pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is
         reporting the results of its review.

         The revised draft element addresses most statutory requirements described in HCD’s
         March 17, 2025, review; however, revisions will be necessary to comply with State
         Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq), as follows:

              1. Review the previous element to evaluate the appropriateness, effectiveness, and
                 progress in implementation, and reflect the results of this review in the revised
                 element. (Gov. Code, § 65588 (a) and (b).)

                  By-Right Requirements: As noted in the prior review, the element must
                  demonstrate by-right requirements were met for all rezoning or replacement
                  capacity or at least adequate to accommodate the shortfall of lower-income
                  Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA) pursuant to Government Code
                  section 65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i). In response, the element now modifies
                  Program HE-3.A (Housing and Vacant Land Inventory) to review the rezones and
                  make adjustments as needed. However, the element should review the rezones
                  for compliance with by-right requirements as part of this housing element update
                  and revise analysis and programs, as necessary, including actions with specific
                  commitment and discrete timing. Please see HCD’s prior review for additional
                  information.

              2. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including
                 vacant sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for
                 redevelopment during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for

      a designated income level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and

      Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning
      period with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and

      Realistic Capacity: As noted in the prior review, the element must support
      assumptions for calculating realistic residential capacity. In response, the
      element mentions multifamily development has been limited and one two-unit
      development was permitted at maximum allowable densities, but the element
      should still list recent developments at least from the broader region to support
      capacity assumptions. The listing should address number of units, zone,
      allowable density, built density and affordability.

      Availability of Infrastructure: As stated in HCD’s prior letter, the element
      acknowledges that there are infrastructure limitations in the County and suggests
      that multifamily development is infeasible given lack of “large-capacity septic
      system or advanced wastewater system” (p. 5-2). However, it must also
      demonstrate sufficient total water and sewer capacity (existing and planned) to
      accommodate the RHNA. The County must add a Program to facilitate the
      development of the remaining RHNA affected by these development constraints
      (e.g., R-4 sites with 215 units). Furthermore, the element suggests that these
      sites will likely require annexation by the City of Red Bluff and service districts.
      Program HE-6.B should be added or revised with specific commitments and
      timelines as needed to address the constraints identified following a complete
      analysis. Please see HCD’s prior review for additional information.

      Annexations: While the element explains the annexation process, it must still
      address status, remaining steps, coordination of zoning and permit procedures
      across jurisdictions, any known barriers and likelihood of development in the
      planning period. Based on the outcomes of this analysis, the element should add
      or modify programs to commit to a specific schedule of actions to facilitate
      development in the planning period, including alternative actions if the sites do
      not become available by a specified date in the planning period.

      Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) and Manufactured Housing: While the element
      may utilize ADUs and manufactured housing toward the RHNA, the calculation
      must be based on factors such as past trends and other relevant factors. The
      element should provide recent trends for ADU construction (e.g., average of past
      three years) and assumptions should be adjusted as needed.

      Electronic Sites Inventory: For your information, pursuant to Government Code
      section 65583.3, the County must submit an electronic sites inventory with its
      adopted housing element. The County must utilize standards, forms, and
      definitions adopted by HCD. Please see HCD’s housing element webpage at
      https://www.hcd.ca.gov/planning-and-community-development/housing-elements
      for a copy of the form and instructions. The County can reach out to HCD at
      sitesinventory@hcd.ca.gov for technical assistance.

      Zoning for a Variety of Housing Types (Emergency Shelters): As noted in the
      prior review, the element should describe the zoning control and/or approval
      processes for the development of emergency shelters between the County and
      the City of Red Bluff and add or modify programs, as appropriate.

      Programs: The element does not include a complete site analysis; therefore, the
      adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the results of a
      complete sites inventory and analysis, the County may need to add or revise
      programs to address a shortfall of sites or zoning available to encourage a
      variety of housing types.

      In addition, Program HE-6.A (Infrastructure Improvements) should include
      specific commitment to support housing development on sites identified with
      infrastructure constraints (e.g., R-4 zone).

   3. An analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints upon the
      maintenance, improvement, or development of housing for all income levels,
      including… …land use controls... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(5).)

      Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
      nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and
      development of housing... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

      Land Use Controls: The element (p. 6-8) now discusses some aspects of the
      combining districts but should also identify the actual standards and analyze
      impacts on housing types, cost and supply and ability to achieve maximum
      allowable densities.

      In addition, the element was revised to clarify that the County applies a 350
      square foot minimum unit size development standard (p. 6-5), however, this
      standard may pose a constraint to the development on smaller units (e.g., SROs,
      group housing) that are more affordable by design. The element should add or
      revise to address identified constraints.

      Finally, based upon the results of a complete analysis, the County should add or
      modify programs to address and remove or mitigate any identified constraints.

The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it
has been revised, adopted, submitted to, and reviewed by HCD to comply with the
above requirements, pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

As a reminder, the County’s 6th cycle housing element was due June 30, 2024. As of
today, the County has not completed the housing element process for the 7th cycle. The
City’s 6th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the County to revise the element as described above, adopt, and submit to
HCD to regain housing element compliance.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021),
as the County did not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
statutory deadline (June 30, 2024), Program HE-3.A (Housing and Vacant Land
Inventory), to make prior identifies sites available, must be completed no later than one
year from the statutory deadline pursuant to Government Code sections 65583,
subdivision (c) and 65583.2, subdivision (c). Please be aware, if the County does not
complete rezoning (HE-3.A -Housing and Vacant Land Inventory) by June 30, 2025, the
element cannot be found in substantial compliance until rezones are completed.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the County should continue to engage the community, including organizations
that represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information
regularly available and considering and incorporating comments where appropriate.
Please be aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local
government’s website and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have
previously requested notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least
seven days before submitting to HCD.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Affordable Housing and Sustainable
Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing Allocation consider
housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to
