         RE: City of Santa Fe Spring’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Dear Raymond Cruz:

         Thank you for submitting the City of Santa Fe Spring’s (City) housing element adopted
         on February 8, 2022 and received for review on March 25, 2022. Pursuant to
         Government Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing
         and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

         The adopted housing element addresses many statutory requirements described in
         HCD’s September 24, 2021 review; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully
         comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code). See enclosed
         Appendix.

         Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
         element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
         process, the City should continue to engage the community, including organizations that
         represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
         available and considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Additionally,
         pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (b) (AB 215, Statutes of
         2021), any subsequent draft revision, the local government must post the draft revisions
         on its website and email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previous
         requested notices related to local governments housing element at least seven days
         before resubmitting to HCD.

         As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due October 15, 2021. As of
         today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
         City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
         encourages the City to revise the element as described above, adopt, and submit to
         HCD to regain housing element compliance.

         For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
         a local government fails to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the

                                        APPENDIX
                                CITY OF SANTA FE SPRINGS

The following changes are necessary to bring the City’s housing element into compliance with
Article 10.6 of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite the
supporting section of the Government Code.

Housing element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-
memos.shtml. Among other resources, the housing element section contains HCD’s latest
technical assistance tool, Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks),
available at http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/index.shtml and
includes the Government Code addressing State Housing Element Law and other resources.


A. Review and Revision

   Review the previous element to evaluate the appropriateness, effectiveness, and progress
   in implementation, and reflect the results of this review in the revised element. (Gov. Code,
   § 65588 (a) and (b).)

   The element was not revised to address this requirement. Please see HCD's prior review.


B. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

   1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
      Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
      the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A).)

      Fair Housing Enforcement and Outreach: While the element now includes information on
      fair housing complaints, it must still address compliance with existing fair housing laws as
      well as any past or current fair housing lawsuits, findings, settlements, judgements, or
      complaints.

      Integration and Segregation: The element now includes some information and analysis
      on familial status and persons with disabilities. However, the element must still evaluate
      patterns within the City related to households and incomes. The City may utilize mapping
      information available on HCD’s website at https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-
      development/affh/index.shtml.

      Disparities in Access to Opportunity: The element was not revised to address this
      requirement. Please see HCD's prior review.

      Disproportionate Housing Needs, Including Displacement Risk: The element was not
      revised to address this requirement. Please see HCD's prior review.

   2. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including vacant
      sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for redevelopment during the
      planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for a designated income level, and an

      Realistic Capacity: The element was revised to include some example projects to justify
      its realistic capacities and describe efforts to create a Mixed-Use TOD zone with a
      maximum density of 60 dwelling units per acre (p. H-69). However, the analysis should
      still discuss the affordability of those projects as well as other projects of similar scale in
      the City. As stated in the prior review, the element should be based on factors such as
      development trends, standards or other relevant factors. The element may be revised, for
      example, to discuss development standards intended in the new Mixed-Use TOD zone.
      The element may also, for example, establish or clarify minimum densities in the R3 and
      mixed-use zones and confirm or describe development standards that facilitate maximum
      densities and residential uses can be accommodated on all sites in the inventory.

      Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: The element was revised to include analysis of specific rezone
      sites including one site's existing uses, the ages of the buildings and the building to land to
      value ratio for sites that will be rezoned to meet the moderate income and above moderate-
      income regional housing needs allocation (RHNA) (p. H-73). However, the element still must
      be revised to include analysis on the potential for additional development on Site U-2 or
      consider other sites if appropriate. The element should also, as stated in the prior review,
      discuss its past experience with converting existing uses to higher density residential
      development the current market demand for the existing use, an analysis of any existing
      leases or other contracts that would perpetuate the existing use or prevent redevelopment of
      the site for additional residential development, development trends, market conditions, and
      regulatory or other incentives or standards to encourage additional residential development
      on these sites.

      In addition, for your information, the element relies on nonvacant sites to accommodate
      50 percent or more of the housing needs for lower-income households, which triggers
      requirements to make findings based on substantial evidence that the existing use is not
      an impediment and will likely discontinue in the planning period. While the resolution of
      adoption includes the appropriate findings, any changes to the analysis should be
      reflected in future re-adoption of the element.

C. Housing Programs

   1. Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period with
      appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities to
      accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing need for
      each income level that could not be accommodated on sites identified in the inventory...
      (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

      As noted in Finding B2, the element does not include a complete site analysis, therefore,
      the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the results of a
      complete sites inventory and analysis, the City may need to add or revise programs to
      address a shortfall of sites or zoning available to encourage a variety of housing types. In
      addition:

         •   Shortfall: The element revised Program 11 to accommodate the needs of lower
             income households, pursuant to Government Code section 65583.2,
             subdivisions (h) and (i). However, the program still must specifically commit to
             acreage, allowable densities, and anticipated units. In addition, the program
             should clarify at least minimum densities and residential performance standards
             consistent with Government Code section 65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i).

         •   City-owned Sites: The element was revised to discuss City owned sites as part
             of Program 20 and describes efforts to conduct outreach and pursue an alternate
             strategy by 2027 if actions do not occur as planned (p. H-93). However, the City
             should also include a date or schedule of actions for all of these efforts. For
             example, the program should commit to a date for requests for proposals for
             both the City-owned and excess state sites.
