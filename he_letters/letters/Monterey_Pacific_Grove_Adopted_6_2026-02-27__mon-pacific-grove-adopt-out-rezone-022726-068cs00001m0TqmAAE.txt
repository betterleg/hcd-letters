         RE: Pacific Grove’s 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Adopted Housing Element and Rezone

         Thank you for submitting the City of Pacific Grove’s (City) housing element, which was
         adopted December 17, 2025 and received for review on December 30, 2025. In
         addition, the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD)
         received Resolution Numbers 25-029, 25-030 and 25-031 and Ordinances 26-002 and
         26-003 (Rezoning Documents). Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, the HCD
         is reporting the results of its review. In addition, HCD considered public comments from
         Jane Haines pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (c).

         On July 10, 2025, HCD found the revised draft housing element, including revisions,
         met statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq).
         The adopted element is substantially the same as the revised draft and as a result, the
         element continues to meet statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law.
         However, as the City did not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
         statutory deadline (December 15, 2023) and rezones were not completed within one
         year, HCD could not find the element in compliance until rezones to make prior
         identified sites available or address a shortfall of sites to accommodate the regional
         housing need allocation (RHNA) are completed and meet all statutory requirements
         pursuant to Government Code sections 65583, subdivision (c)(1)(A) and 65583.2,
         subdivisions (c), (h) and (i). While Program 5 (Adequate Sites for RHNA) actions have
         been adopted and meet most of the statutory requirements, the Rezoning Documents
         do not meet all statutory requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65583.2,
         subdivision (h). Specifically, the Rezoning Documents do not meet minimum density
         requirements for the C-1, C-2 and R-3 zones. As a result, revisions to the Rezoning
         Documents will be necessary to comply with State Housing Element Law.
Jon Biggs, Director

The element will substantially comply with State Housing Element Law once rezones
are modified to meet all statutory requirements and documentation has been submitted
to and reviewed by HCD to comply with the above requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65585. Once the City amends the rezoning to meet all
statutory requirements, a resolution, ordinance and other pertinent documentation
should be transmitted to HCD. HCD will review the documentation and issue
correspondence identifying the updated status of the City’s housing element
compliance.

As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due December 15, 2023. As of
today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. HCD
encourages the City to expeditiously amend its rezoning as described above and submit
documentation to HCD to regain housing element compliance.

Additionally, the City must continue timely and effective implementation of all programs
including but not limited to the following:

    •   Program 3 (Architectural Review)
    •   Program 5 (Adequate Sites for RHNA)
    •   Program 6 (Shopping Centers Redevelopment)
    •   Program 7 (Large Lot Development)
    •   Program 8 (Lot Consolidation)
    •   Program 9 (Accessory Dwelling Units)
    •   Program 10 (Enhanced Housing Mobility Options)
    •   Program 11 (Water Supply)
    •   Program 12 (Sewer System Capacity)
    •   Program 13 (Zoning Code Amendments)
    •   Program 14 (Affordable Housing Development)
    •   Program 15 (Density Bonus)
    •   Program 19 (Resource Conservation in Existing Homes)
    •   Program 21 (Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing)

The City must monitor and report on the results of these and other programs through
the annual progress report, required pursuant to Government Code section 65400.
Please be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD authority
to review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
inconsistent with an adopted housing element or housing element law. This includes
failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke
housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state
law.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Land Use and Climate Innovation at:
https://www.lci.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.

HCD appreciates the hard work and commitment you and City staff provided throughout
the housing element update and review. The City has successfully addressed most
statutory requirements and made tremendous progress toward substantial compliance.
HCD looks forward to expeditiously working with the City to achieve substantial
compliance with State Housing Element Law. If HCD can provide technical assistance
in implementing the housing element, please contact Mao Lee of our staff at
Mao.Lee@hcd.ca.gov.
