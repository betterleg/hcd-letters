          RE: City of Indio’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

          Thank you for submitting the City of Indio’s (City) housing element adopted
          January 5, 2022 and received for review on January 21, 2022, along with revisions
          received on March 16, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision
          (h), the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is
          reporting the results of its review. Our review was facilitated by a conversation on
          March 1, 2022 with City’s consultants, Erika Van Sickel and
          Elizabeth Dickson.

          The adopted housing element along with draft revisions address the findings in HCD’s
          October 12, 2021 review letter. However, the revisions must be adopted for the
          element to comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Government
          Code). Therefore, the element will comply with State Housing Element Law when it is
          re-adopted to incorporate the March 16, 2022 revisions and submitted to HCD for
          review, pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivisions (b) and (g).

          For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021),
          as the City failed to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the statutory
          deadline (October 15, 2021), Program 33 (Sites Inventory Zoning Update Program) to
          rezone 2,286 units to accommodate the regional housing needs allocation (RHNA) for
          lower-income must be completed no later than one year from the statutory deadline.
          Otherwise, the local government’s housing element will no longer comply with State
          Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial compliance
          pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i).

          Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
          compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
          (SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
