             Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Kingsburg’s adopted amendments to the housing
         element. The amendment was adopted October 15, 2025 and received for review on
         October 17, 2025. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, the California
         Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of
         its review.

         On September 16, 2024, HCD determined that the City’s housing element was in
         substantial compliance with State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq).
         On August 19, 2025, HCD reviewed the draft amendment to the housing element and
         found that the amendments did not impact the September 16, 2024 finding of
         substantial compliance. For example, the amendments to the housing elements
         demonstrate adequate sites and rezoning is no longer necessary to accommodate the
         regional housing need allocation (RHNA). Because the adopted amendments are
         substantially the same as draft amendments reviewed on August 19, 2025, the adopted
         housing element and its amendments continue to meet the requirements of State
         Housing Element Law. This finding of substantial compliance has remained unchanged
         since HCD’s September 16, 2024 review.

Please be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD
authority to review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines
is inconsistent with an adopted housing element or Housing Element Law. This
includes failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD
may revoke housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not
comply with state law.

As indicated on page 1H-57 (Table 1H-6), the element identifies a minimal surplus of
sites to accommodate the RHNA. For your information, pursuant to Government Code
section 65863 (No Net Loss Law), among many other provisions, local governments
shall ensure that identified sites can accommodate, at all times throughout the planning
period, its remaining unmet share of the RHNA. Further, if the approval of a
development project results in fewer units by income category than identified in the
housing element and the jurisdiction does not find that the remaining sites in the
housing element are adequate to accommodate the jurisdiction’s share of the regional
housing need by income level, the local government shall within 180 days identify and
make available additional adequate sites to accommodate the jurisdiction’s share of
the regional housing need by income level. HCD encourages the City to diligently
implement these provisions and Program 4 (Provision of Adequate Sites) and reminds
the City that HCD may review, among other actions, and notify the Attorney General of
any action in violation of No Net Loss Law.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Affordable Housing and Sustainable
Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing Allocation consider
housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing element, the City now
