       RE: County of Fresno’s 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Revised Draft Housing Element

       Thank you for submitting the County of Fresno’s (County) revised draft housing element
       update received for review on October 24, 2024. Pursuant to Government Code section
       65585, the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is
       reporting the results of its review. In addition, HCD considered comments from
       Leadership Counsel for Justice and Accountability, pursuant to Government Code section
       65585, subdivision (c).

       The revised draft element addresses most statutory requirements that were described in
       HCD’s March 15, 2024 review; however, revisions will be necessary to substantially
       comply with State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq.), as follows:

            1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing
               with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair
               housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A).)

                 Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
                 throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,
                 religion, sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or
                 disability, and other characteristics... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subds. (c)(5).)

                 Enforcement and Outreach: On August 5, 2024, HCD provided technical
                 assistance to the County regarding withdraw of the County’s application to HCD’s
                 No Place Like Home (NPLH) program in connection with the approved 86-unit
                 housing project called Libre Commons. As noted in that correspondence, the
                 element should specifically analyze impacts on affirmatively furthering fair housing
                 (AFFH), including compliance with fair housing and related laws (e.g., Government
                 Code section 8899.50), and add or modify programs based on a complete
                 analysis. For more information, please see HCD’s August 5, 2024 correspondence.

      Identified Sites and AFFH: The element now includes some discussion of the
      location of sites. However, to evaluate the cumulative impact of identified sites on
      fair housing conditions, the element should incorporate all identified sites, including
      pipeline projects and vacant sites with existing zoning (Table A) then evaluate
      whether the location of sites, by all income groups, improves or exacerbates
      current fair housing conditions.

      Programs: Based on the outcomes of these analyses, the element must add or
      modify programs. Programs to AFFH should go beyond status quo actions and
      should be significant and meaningful to promote inclusive and equitable
      communities. Programs should include specific commitment, timing, geographic
      targeting and metrics or numerical targets and should, as appropriate, address
      housing mobility, encourage new housing choices in relatively higher resource and
      income areas, improve place-based strategies toward community revitalization and
      protect existing residents from displacement. The element should, for example,
      commit to additional programs to promote housing mobility (not limited to the
      regional housing need allocation (RHNA)), address displacement risk and enhance
      place-based strategies toward community revitalization.

   2. Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period
      with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and
      facilities... Sites shall be identified as needed to facilitate and encourage the
      development of a variety of types of housing for all income levels… (Gov. Code,
      § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

      Program 14 (Adequate Sites): The Program should specifically commit to rezone
      sites and comply with all requirements pursuant to Government Code sections
      65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i), including permitting housing developments with
      20 percent affordability without discretionary action.

      For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of
      2021), if a local government does not adopt a compliant housing element within
      120 days of the statutory deadline (December 31, 2023), then any rezoning to
      make prior identified sites available or accommodate the RHNA pursuant to
      Government Code sections 65583, subdivision (c) and 65583.2, subdivision (c),
      shall be completed no later than one year from the statutory deadline. Please be
      aware, if the County does not adopt a compliant housing element within one year
      from the statutory deadline, the element cannot be found in substantial compliance
      until all necessary rezones (Program 14 – Adequate Sites) are completed.

      Program 20 (Zoning Ordinance Amendments): Regarding permanent supportive
      housing, while the Program commits to permit supportive housing similar to
      multifamily development in nonresidential zones, it should specifically commit to
      amend zoning to permit multifamily development in all zones allowing multifamily
      without discretionary action and comply with various other provisions pursuant to
      Government Code section 65651.

      Electronic Site Inventory: For your information, pursuant to Government Code
      section 65583.3, the County must submit an electronic sites inventory with its
      adopted housing element. The County must utilize standards, forms, and
      definitions adopted by HCD. Please see HCD’s housing element webpage at
      https://www.hcd.ca.gov/planning-and-community-development/housing-elements
      for a copy of the form and instructions. The County can reach out to HCD at
      sitesinventory@hcd.ca.gov for technical assistance.

   3. Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
      nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and development
      of housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for persons with
      disabilities... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

      Program 33 (Improving Housing Options): While the Program includes several
      actions to improve housing options, it should specifically commit to address
      constraints related to multifamily heights and housing for persons with disabilities,
      as follows:

         •   Multifamily Heights: As noted in the prior review, one story height limits in
             multifamily zones are a constraint. The element should recognize the
             constraint and add or modify programs to specifically address the constraint,
             regardless of the RHNA and local support for increasing heights in
             multifamily zones.

         •   Housing for Persons with Disabilities: The Program commits to evaluate
             parking and conditional use permit (CUP) requirements as potential
             constraints on group homes for seven or more persons and modify
             requirements if constraints are determined by the County. However, the
             purpose of the housing element update is to analyze constraints, not commit
             to analyze constraints as part of a program. In the absence of analysis, the
             Program should be revised to specifically commit to address parking and
             the CUP as constraints on housing for persons with disabilities.

   4. The Housing Element shall contain programs which assist in the development of
      adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and
      moderate-income households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

      Program 35 (Housing for Farmworkers): The Program should specifically commit to
      annually identify specific development opportunities and pursue strategies to
      integrate housing for farmworkers into affordable housing.


The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once the
element has been revised, adopted, submitted to, and reviewed by HCD to comply with
the above requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

As a reminder, the County’s 6th cycle housing element was due December 31, 2023. As
of today, the County has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle.
The County’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the County to revise the element as described above, adopt if needed, and
submit to HCD to regain housing element compliance.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the County must continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households; by making information regularly
available while considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. For example,
HCD received comments on this revised draft and the County should consider these
comments and make adjustments, as appropriate. Please be aware, any revisions to the
element must be posted on the local government’s website and to email a link to all
individuals and organizations that have previously requested notices relating to the local
government’s housing element at least seven days before submitting to HCD.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill SB1
Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s Affordable
Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing
Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements
pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing element, the
County will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the County to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.

HCD appreciates the hard work and dedication you, your staff, and your consultants
