          RE: Ontario’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Revised Adopted Housing Element

          Thank you for submitting the City of Ontario’s housing element adopted August 16, 2022
          and received for review on August 23, 2022 in addition to correspondence received on
          September 26, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the
          California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the
          results of its review.

          HCD is pleased to find the adopted housing element in full compliance with State
          Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code). The adopted element addresses
          the statutory requirements described in HCD’s May 10, 2022 review by providing a
          complete analysis of large sites in the Ontario Mills Mall and Great Park Corridor areas;
          including a complete constraints analysis of the Design Review Advisory Board (DAB)
          process, and by including programs that encourage an facilitate housing at all income
          levels on applicable sites in the inventory. HCD also understands that on March 1,
          2022, the City completed the land use changes as part of the Ontario Plan and
          updated the Plan on August 16, 2022. As a result all rezones required under Program
          13 to accommodate the shortfall of sites for the lower-income RHNA have been
          completed.

          Additionally, the City must continue timely and effective implementation of all programs
          including but not limited to the following:

               •   Program 8 (Downtown Plan): Program 8 commits to the adoption of the
                   Downtown Specific Plan, which will facilitate the development of 634 housing
                   units at all income levels during the planning period. While the specific plan itself
                   has already been adopted, its successful implementation is critical to the
                   success of the element and the City’s ability to meet its RHNA goals.

   •   Program 11 (Ontario Ranch): Program 11 commits to hosting annual roundtable
       discussions with developers to proactively discuss integrating affordable units
       into development plans, and to using the preapplication process as a time to
       discuss integrating affordable units into development plans.
   •   Program 16 (Incentives): As part of this program, in order to facilitate the
       subdivision of larger sites (larger than 10 acres), the City will waive map review
       or lot line adjustment fees when a project commits to including affordable
       housing consistent with the Affordable Housing Overlay.
   •   Program 13 (2021-2029 Regional Housing Need): This program identifies
       adequate sites to accommodate an overall shortfall of 17,716 units by
       committing to needed rezones and adopting an Affordable Housing Overlay
       zone by February 12, 2025. Among other things, the program commits to zoning
       with densities of 20-30 units per acre and permit owner-occupied and rental
       multifamily uses by right for developments in which 20 percent or more of the
       units are affordable to lower income households.
   •   Program 6 (Neighborhood Stabilization) and Program 20 (Development Code
       Amendments): These programs commit to removing “crime free multi-housing”
       meetings (Program 6), and “crime free lease addendum” requirements (Program
       20). While the element does reference these requirements elsewhere,
       correspondence from the City received on September 26, 2022 confirmed these
       activities will be discontinued, and that any language saying they would be
       continued into the current planning cycle was included in error.

The City must monitor and report on the results of this and other programs through the
annual progress report (APR), required pursuant to Government Code section 65400.
Please be aware, Government Code section 65585(i) grants HCD authority to review
any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is inconsistent with
an adopted housing element or housing element law. This includes failure to implement
program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke housing element
compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state law.

Public participation in the implementation of the housing element is essential to effective
housing planning. Throughout the housing element implementation process and as part of
the APR submitted to your City Council, the City must continue to engage the community,
including organizations that represent lower-income and special needs households and
neighborhoods with higher concentrations of lower-income and special needs households,
by making information regularly available while considering and incorporating feedback as
necessary.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
