         RE: City of Huntington Park’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Huntington Park’s (City) draft housing element
         received for review on November 18, 2021. Pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, subdivision (b), the California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. Our review was facilitated by
         a telephone conversation on January 11, 2022, with you and City Attorney Araceli
         Almazan. In addition, HCD considered comments from Communities for a Better
         Environment (CBE), Disability Rights California, and the Public Interest Law Project
         (PILP) pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (c).

         The draft element addresses some statutory requirements; however, revisions will be
         necessary to comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 (commencing with.
         Section 65580) of Chapter 3 of the Government Code). The enclosed Appendix A
         describes revisions needed to comply with State Housing Element Law.

         Given the change in staff working on the housing element at the City, HCD considers it
         advisable to remind the City of its commitments under the November 8, 2021
         Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) signed between the City and HCD. A copy of that
         MOU is attached as Appendix B. What is most important about the MOU, and the
         discussions leading up to the MOU, is the City commitment to certain milestones and
         deadlines for the express purpose of producing a legally compliant housing element by
         February 12, 2022. An adopted housing element that does not comply with statutory
         obligations is insufficient. This intent was repeated throughout the MOU.

         The City’s 6th cycle housing element was due October 15, 2021, and City has not
         completed the housing element process for the 6th Cycle. Under the MOU, the City must
         revise the element, adopt, and submit to HCD on or before February 12, 2022.
         Should the City fail to meet its compliance milestones and submit a substantively
         compliant housing element by that date, the City may be deemed in material breach

of the MOU. In which case, HCD may notify the California Office of the Attorney General,
which may bring suit for violations of State Housing Element Law. Further, statute provides
for court-imposed penalties for persistent non-compliance, including financial penalties.
Government Code section 65585, subdivision (l)(1), establishes a minimum fine of
$10,000 per month and up to $100,000 per month. If a jurisdiction continues to remain
noncompliant, a court can multiply the penalties up to a factor of six. Other potential
ramifications could include the loss of local land use authority to a court-appointed agent.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
a local government fails to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
statutory deadline (October 15, 2021), then any rezoning to accommodate the regional
housing needs allocation, including for lower-income households, shall be completed no
later than one year from the statutory deadline. Otherwise, the local government’s
housing element will no longer comply with State Housing Element Law, and HCD may
revoke its finding of substantial compliance pursuant to Government Code section
65585, subdivision (i).

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the City should continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
available and considering and incorporating comments where appropriate.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criterion. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs, and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

HCD is committed to assist the City in addressing all statutory requirements of State
Housing Element Law. If you have any questions or need additional technical assistance,
please contact Colin Cross, of our staff, at colin.cross@hcd.ca.gov.

   A. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

      1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
         Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
         the jurisdiction (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A))

          Regional Patterns and Trends: For all categories of analysis (segregation and
          Integration; racially and ethnically concentrated areas of poverty; access to opportunity.
          and disproportionate housing needs, including displacement) the element addresses
          many requirements to analyze local patterns and trends. However, the element must
          also analyze regional patterns and trends for each category and subcategory of
          analysis. A regional analysis should compare conditions at the local level to the rest of
          the region. This analysis could compare the locality to the County, a Council of
          Governments, or other subregional geography.

          Disproportionate Housing Needs: The element is revised to include some information
          about substandard housing, but the analysis is incomplete. A full analysis should
          identify local patterns and trends and could address, for example, the actual number
          and percentage of households with one or more of the four housing problems,
          comparing findings throughout the jurisdiction. The element must also address
          homelessness and displacement risk; currently, the draft includes maps of sensitive
          communities, but does not describe or contextualize them, or analyze any findings.

          Site Inventory: The analysis still does not fully identify whether sites improve or
          exacerbate conditions or whether sites are isolated by income group. Revisions
          provide a table summarizing the Regional Housing Needs Assessment (RHNA) targets
          for the Downtown Specific Plan (DTSP) and a statement that the DTSP area “provides
          an adequate amount of various income categories” (p. II-91). This is not adequate to
          satisfy this requirement. The element should fully address the impact of the decision to
          select only sites that are within the DTSP area and should analyze identified sites with
          respect to all categories of analysis (segregation and integration; R/ECAPs; access to
          opportunity; and disproportionate housing needs, including displacement). Depending
          on the results of a complete analysis, the element should add programs as appropriate.

          5th Cycle RHNA Carryover: As stated in the prior review, the City failed to demonstrate
          the availability of adequate sites in the 5th cycle housing element and therefore must
          demonstrate consistency with Government Code section 65584.09. To address this
          finding, the element was amended to state that the sites identified in the current 6th
          cycle housing element were available in the 5th cycle and concludes the City does not
          have an unaccommodated 5th cycle carryover (p. B-2). However, no further analysis
          was given to support this assertion. The element must demonstrate that sites were
          appropriately zoned and available for housing development from the beginning of the
          5th cycle, meeting statutory requirements for suitability otherwise, the City must rezone
          sufficient sites to accommodate the unaccommodated need within one year of the
          planning period. Please be aware, should the element successfully demonstrate that
          there is no carry-over obligation because of the availability of these sites, and
          continues to use the sites in their 6th cycle housing element towards the lower-income
          need, these sites will be considered to be “reuse” sites which trigger the by-right
          requirements under Gov. Code 65583.2(c).

          Realistic Capacity: The element is revised to describe the suitability of the DTSP but
          does not include further explanation of the trends and other factors that led to the City’s
          capacity assumption of 80 percent of total allowable density. Please refer to HCD’s
          prior review.

          For sites zoned for nonresidential uses such as the DTSP area, the element is revised
          to assume that 50 percent of identified sites in Districts A and B will develop as
          commercial uses and 25 percent will develop as mixed-use. The element should relate
          these assumptions to development trends and other factors. The element must also
          describe the underlying zoning and whether it allows entirely nonresidential
          development to occur and adjust for the likelihood of such development.

          Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: The element must include an analysis demonstrating the
          potential for redevelopment of nonvacant sites. The description of existing uses in the
          revised element remains minimal; for example, several sites are labeled with text such
          as “Most buildings in this site are more than 40 years old…” (p. B-35). This general
          description is not adequate. The element must be revised to describe existing uses in
          sufficient detail to facilitate an analysis demonstrating the potential for additional
          development in the planning period.

          In addition, the element must analyze the extent to which existing uses impede
          additional residential development and should describe and support (through
          development trends) the factors and thresholds used to identify sites for
          redevelopment. The revised element notes that the City considered age and condition
          of structures on nonvacant sites to identify sites for the inventory. This alone is not
          adequate to satisfy this requirement. A full analysis should consider additional
          indicators such as property owner interest, developer interest, market conditions, past
          development trends, presence of expiring leases, low improvement to land value ratio,
          and other factors. For more information, please refer to HCD’s prior review.

          Substantial Evidence: The element does not address this finding. Please refer to
          HCD’s prior review.

          Small Sites: The revised element includes a program to encourage lot consolidation for
          the small parcels identified in the inventory, but still does not analyze the City’s
          capacity for aggregating small parcels. The analysis could describe the City’s role or
          track record in facilitating small-lot consolidation, policies or incentives offered or
          proposed to encourage and facilitate lot consolidation, conditions rendering parcels
          suitable and ready for redevelopment, recent trends of lot consolidation, and/or
          information on the owners of each aggregated site. For more information, please refer
          to HCD’s prior review.

          Suitability and Availability of Infrastructure: Currently, the draft notes that “resources

          review.

          Processing and Permit Procedures: The element includes additional information
          regarding the City’s discretionary development permit for all multifamily development,
          but still does not analyze the permit or the process as a potential constraint. For
          example, the revised element claims that the “purpose of the Development Review is
          to ‘protect the integrity and character of the residential, commercial, and industrial
          areas of the City…’” (p. IV-21); such language seeks to justify the development permit
          without discussing its impact on development. A full analysis should describe the
          process in detail; assess its impact on approval certainty, supply, affordability, timing,
          and other relevant factors; discuss the City’s recent track record of multifamily
          development; and more fully analyze the subjective design standards noted in HCD’s
          prior review. This is especially important given the impact this process may have on the
          site inventory. The inventory envisions compact multifamily development throughout
          the DTSP area, but a requirement for a discretionary review for all multifamily projects
          could greatly hinder the viability of the identified sites. The element should add or
          modify programs to mitigate or remove identified constraints.

          Design Review: Revisions to the element claim that the subjective design review
          process is necessary because of the age of the City’s buildings, and somewhat
          describe the process’ impact on timing. This is not adequate to satisfy this requirement.
          A complete analysis should describe the process and any approval findings in detail;
          thoroughly assess the impact on approval certainty, supply, affordability, timing, and
          other relevant factors; and more fully analyze the City’s design standards and/or
          guidelines. The element must demonstrate this process is not a constraint or it must
          include a program to address this permitting requirement, as appropriate. For more
          information, please refer to HCD’s prior review.

          Housing for Persons with Disabilities: The element includes additional language
          confirming the City’s fee for reasonable accommodation, but generally does not
          address this finding. Please refer to HCD’s prior review. The City’s fee for reasonable
          accommodation requests is a constraint to housing for persons with disabilities and the
          element should commit to removing this constraint via a program action.

          Regarding residential care facilities for seven or more persons, the element is revised
          simply to state that the City’s Conditional Use Permit exists “to regulate provisions such
          as adequate housing, amenities, and staffing” and that it “does not pose as a
          constraint” (p. IV-13). This is not adequate to satisfy the requirement. The element
          should analyze the process as a potential constraint on housing for persons with
          disabilities and add or modify programs as appropriate to ensure zoning permits group
          homes objectively with approval certainty.

          Local Ordinances: The element is not revised to address this finding. Please refer to
          HCD’s prior review.

   C. Public Participation

      Local governments shall make a diligent effort to achieve public participation of all
      economic segments of the community in the development of the Housing Element, and the
      element shall describe this effort. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.(c)(8).)

      The element does not describe a diligent effort to include all economic segments of the
      community in the development of the housing element. The element is revised to state that
      the City’s farmer’s market booths constituted outreach to lower-income households. In
      addition, HCD’s prior review indicated that the element should provide more information on
      nongovernmental organizations and other parties that were notified. The element is
      revised to include nearly 50 pages of email addresses, without context to explain which
      organizations were proactively contacted and whom those organizations may represent.
      Moving forward, the City should employ additional methods for public outreach efforts,
      particularly to include lower-income and special needs households and neighborhoods
      with higher concentrations of lower-income households.

      Third-party comments suggest that the revised draft was not made available to the public
      prior to submission to HCD. The City must proactively make future revisions available to
      the public, including any commenters, with sufficient time to comment and prior to

      submitting any revisions to HCD. The element should also diligently consider and address
      comments, including revising the document where appropriate. For example, HCD

      suggestions that do not appear to have been considered. The element should summarize
      future public comments and describe how comments were considered and incorporated
