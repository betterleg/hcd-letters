A. Review and Revision

   Review the previous element to evaluate the appropriateness, effectiveness, and progress
   in implementation, and reflect the results of this review in the revised element. (Gov. Code,
   § 65588 (a) and (b).)

   General: While the element includes some information on the progress in implementation, it
   must evaluate the effectiveness of programs and reflect the results in the current element.
   Instead, in some cases, the element describes progress; but the progress does not appear
   related to the program’s objectives or does not evaluate the effectiveness of the program in
   achieving the program objectives. Further, in other cases, the element mentions the
   program will be modified and continued as appropriate but does not appear to be modified
   in response to the analysis. For example, Program 1.4 (Community Plan Updates) was
   intended to ensure adequate land for housing. However, the element does not discuss
   effectiveness of the community plans that were adopted or reflect the accomplishments of
   these plans in the current program. For another example, Program 1.8 (Planning for Large
   Sites) was intended to encourage housing development. The element mentions the County
   had meetings with developers but does not expand on any other actions or strategies to
   encourage development for large sites. To address this requirement, the element should
   examine the objectives of past programs, effectiveness of implementation in achieving the
   objectives and modify current programs as appropriate for Programs 1.2, 1.4, 1.5, 1.6, 1.8,
   1.10, 2.1, 2.2, 2.5, 3.3, 3.4, and 4.1.

   Special Housing Needs: As part of the evaluation of programs in the past cycle, the
   element must also provide an explanation of the cumulative effectiveness of goals, policies,
   and related actions in meeting the housing needs of special needs populations (e.g.,
   elderly, persons with disabilities, large households, female-headed households,
   farmworkers, and persons experiencing homelessness). While the element briefly
   describes the goals of a few past programs for housing for persons with special needs (p.
   A1-1), a complete analysis must provide an evaluation of the cumulative effectiveness of
   these strategies in meeting the housing needs of all special-needs populations. Programs
   should be revised as appropriate to reflect the results of this evaluation.

B. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

   1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
      Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
      the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A).)

      Enforcement and Outreach: The element describes capacity to provide education and
      outreach and a summary of fair housing complaints. However, the element must also
      include a discussion of any findings, lawsuits, consent decrees, judgments, and any
      other legal matters regarding fair housing. The analysis should also discuss how the
      County complies with federal, state and local fair housing laws and regulations. For
      additional information, please see pages 28-30 on HCD’s Affirmatively Furthering Fair
      Housing (AFFH) Guidance Memo at https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-
      development/affh/docs/AFFH_Document_Final_4-27-2021.pdf.

      Local Patterns: The element includes some mapping and reporting of socio-economic
      characteristics but should also address patterns and trends of socio-economic
      characteristics within each of the communities (comparing neighborhoods or areas).
      This analysis may aggregate some community areas such as planning areas. The
      analysis should address all components of the AFFH assessment, including segregation
      and integration, racially/ethnically concentrated areas of poverty and affluence,
      disparities in access to opportunity and disproportionate housing needs, including
      displacement risk. The assessment should also incorporate local date and knowledge
      and other relevant factors, particularly in smaller rural areas, to better evaluate patterns
      of disparities in access to opportunities and prioritize contributing factors to fair housing
      issues.

      Identified Sites and AFFH: This analysis should discuss patterns of identified sites by
      income group within CDPs or geographic sub-areas. The analysis must identify whether
      sites are isolated by income group and should be conducted by a geography smaller
      than census tract. The element should also discuss whether the distribution of sites
      improves or exacerbates conditions. If sites exacerbate conditions, the element should
      identify further program actions that will be taken to promote equitable quality of life
      throughout the community (e.g., anti-displacement and place-based community
      revitalization strategies).

D. Quantified Objectives

   Establish the number of housing units, by income level, that can be constructed,
   rehabilitated, and conserved over a five-year time frame. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (b)(1
   & 2).)

   The element must include quantified objectives to establish an estimate of housing units by
   income category that can be constructed, rehabilitated, and conserved over the planning
   period. While the element includes these objectives by income group for new construction,
   the element must also include objectives for rehabilitation and conservation units by income
   category. The conservation objectives should not be limited to units at-risk of conversion to
   market rate uses during the planning period and may include broader efforts to conserve
   the existing housing stock such as code enforcement, housing choice vouchers and
   weatherization.
