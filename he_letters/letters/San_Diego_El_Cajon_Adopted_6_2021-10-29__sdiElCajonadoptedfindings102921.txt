          RE: El Cajon’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element Update

          Thank you for submitting the City of El Cajon’s (City) housing element, which was
          adopted on July 27, 2021 and received for review on August 2, 2021. Pursuant to
          Government Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing
          and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.
          The adopted housing element addresses most statutory requirements described in
          HCD’s May 17, 2021; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully comply with
          State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code):
                   1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15
                      (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
                      assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
                      (c)(10)(A).)

                        Enforcement and Outreach: The element did not address this finding. While the
                        housing element includes a summary of fair housing enforcement actions,
                        complaints, and outreach capacity (p. D-1), the analysis must still describe
                        compliance with existing fair housing laws and regulations.
                        Racial/Ethnic Areas of Concentration of Poverty and Affluence (R/ECAP and
                        RCAA): While the element now includes some data on R/ECAPs and RCAA, the
                        element must analyze the data for patterns and trends. Additionally, the element
                        identified two areas considered R/ECAPs and should include specific analysis such
                        as trends, past and current efforts, including investment and their effectiveness,
                        local data and knowledge and other relevant factors (as noted below). The analysis
                        should also consider any overlapping fair housing issues with other components of
                        the assessment of fair housing (e.g., access to opportunity and disproportionate
                        housing needs, including displacement).
                        Local Knowledge and Data: The element should incorporate local data and
                        knowledge of the jurisdiction into the assessment of fair housing. To assist in
                        meeting this requirement, the element should provide local data not captured in

                regional, state, or federal data analysis such as information on resident’s lived
                experiences, and comments from community, neighborhood and stakeholder

                Other Relevant Factors: The element must include other relevant factors that could
                have contributed to fair housing issues in the jurisdiction. For instance, the element
                can analyze historical land use and investment practices, demographic trends, or
                other information.

                Sites Inventory: While the element does include an analysis of sites relative to
                several fair housing components, it also needs to analyze sites related to
                disproportionate housing needs. Also, the element should analyze and conclude
                whether the identified sites improve or exacerbate eachof the fair housing issue
                area.

                Goals, Priorities, Metrics, and Milestones: While the element included
                additional actions to address Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH),
                several of these actions have either been recycled from the last housing
                element, need additional information, or are inadequate in meaningfully
                addressing the City’s fair housing issues and overcoming identified patterns
                and trends of segregation. Additionally, programs must include descriptions of
                how each action will be implemented, milestones and metrics to target
                outcomes and measure progress. For example, to address concentration of
                lower-income households in low resourced areas, the element stated that it
                will prioritize improvements and programs to address the needs of residents
                in these communities. However, the element must detail steps in how this
                action will be implemented, timelines for implementation, and metrics to target
                outcomes and measure the results of this program. Additionally, given that
                the City is generally a lower-resourced area with census tracts that are
                considered R/ECAP and high segregation and poverty, and have several
                overlapping fair housing issues, the City should focus on strong place-based
                strategies for community preservation and revitalization, displacement
                protection, and enhancing housing mobility.

                Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: The element must still include analysis to
                demonstrate the potential for additional development on nonvacant sites, as
                follows:

                   •   Existing Uses: The element was not revised to address the findings
                       noted in the May 17, 2021 letter and must still describe existing uses
                       sufficiently to facilitate an analysis of the suitability of nonvacant sites
                       for additional development.
                   •   Factors: While the revised element did include the list of actual values
                       for the factors used on each of site (e.g., improvement to land ratio,
                       existing FAR, building age), it must include this information for sites
                       listed in Table C-1 of the element. Additionally, the element stated that
                       it removed sites that had a land-to-improvement (LIV) ratio of greater
                       than 1.0, the age of the building was less than 40 years old and sites
                       that had more than 0.45-0.60 floor area ratio (p. 74-75). However, the
                       inventory does not reflect that criteria. For example, several sites list
                       values “00” and “90” under the “year built” column. The element should
                       clarify if those values indicate how many years ago the existing use
                       was built or the year that the use was built. Additionally, the element
                       lists several sites with LIV ratio ranging from 1.35 – 10.58.
                       The element also stated that the methodology considered local
                       knowledge (staff knowledge of existing conditions, owners’ interest)
                       and objective criteria (quantified factors) and the inventory indicated
                       which sites were identified by local knowledge or objective criteria.
                       However, the element should clarify if the City relied on both local
                       knowledge and objective criteria for each site or utilized one or the
                       other. As stated in the previous letter, the element included few
                       examples of projects that redeveloped from commercial uses into
                       residential or mixed-use (p. 76), however the element should discuss
                       the characteristics of these examples (e.g., age of structure, FAR,
                       improvement to land ratios) to support the assumptions.

                   •   Adopted Resolution Findings: As noted in the previous letter, the
                       element relies on nonvacant sites for more than 50% of the Regional
                       Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA) for lower-income (p.80), and must
                       make findings of substantial evidence that the existing use does not
                       constitute as an impediment to development, and that the existing use
                       is likely to discontinue during the planning period. The resolution for
                       the adopted element did not include those findings. The element will
                       meet this requirement once it is re-adopted with findings on nonvacant
                       sites.

                  Electronic Sites Inventory: Pursuant to Government Code section 65583.3,
                  subdivision (b), the City must utilize standards, forms, and definitions adopted by
                  HCD when preparing the sites inventory. Additionally, upon adoption of the housing
                  element, the City must submit an electronic version of the sites inventory with its
                  adopted housing element to sitesinventory@hcd.ca.gov. The City has not yet
                  submitted an electronic sites inventory to HCD. Please see HCD’s housing
                  element webpage at element/index.shtml#element for a copy of the form and
                  instructions. The City can reach out to HCD at sitesinventory@hcd.ca.gov for
                  technical assistance.
                  Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete site analysis,
                  therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the
                  results of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City mayneed to add or
                  revise programs to address a shortfall of sites.

       The element will comply once the element has been revised and re-adopted to address
       the above findings.
       As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due April 15, 2021. As of today,
       the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The City’s 5th
       cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD encourages the
       City to make revisions to the element as described in this letter, adopt, and submit to
       HCD to regain housing element compliance. For more information on housing element
       adoption requirements, please visit HCD’s website at:
       http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-
       memos/docs/sb375_final100413.pdf.

       For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
       element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
       the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
       Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
       http://opr.ca.gov/docs/OPR_Appendix_C_final.pdf and
       http://opr.ca.gov/docs/Final_6.26.15.pdf.

       Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
       compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
       (SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
       Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
       Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
       requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
       element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
       sources.
