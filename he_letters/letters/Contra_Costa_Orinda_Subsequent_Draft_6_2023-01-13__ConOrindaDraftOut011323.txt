         RE: City of Orinda’s 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Revised Draft Housing Element Update

         Thank you for submitting the City of Orinda’s (City) revised draft housing element
         update received for review on November 16, 2022, along with revisions received on
         January 12, 2023. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (b), the
         California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the
         results of its review. Our review was facilitated by a telephone conversation on
         January 3, 2023 with yourself, Winnie Mui, David Biggs, and consultants Cynthia Walsh
         and Jennifer Gastelum. In addition, HCD considered comments from East Bay for
         Everyone, Inclusive Lafayette, YIMBY Law, Andrew Collins, Chris Chelberg,
         Ross Bernet, Steve Spiker, Will Finch, and Rohit Sarathy, pursuant to Government
         Code section 65585, subdivision (c). In addition, pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, subdivision (c), HCD must consider third party comments in the preparation of
         its findings. However, HCD was unable to fully examine the information and consider
         comments received on January 12, 2022 from East Bay for Everyone, SRVCC,
         Greenbelt Alliance, and 350 Contra Costa Action and comments received by YIMBY
         Law as part of this review. Consequently, HCD will retain the comments for full
         consideration in the next review of the housing element. Prior to adoption of the
         element, the City should review these comments and incorporate feedback were
         appropriate.

         The draft housing element addresses most statutory requirements described in HCD’s
         August 12, 2022 review; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully comply
         with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code), as follows:

             1. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including
                vacant sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for
                redevelopment during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for
                a designated income level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and
                public facilities and services to these sites. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

            Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning
            period with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and
            facilities to accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the
            regional housing need for each income level that could not be accommodated on

            Suitability of Nonvacant Sites and Small Sites: While the element was revised to
            include examples of development on sites smaller than 0.5 acres to support
            assumptions in the sites inventory, it must include analysis to demonstrate the
            suitability of these sites to facilitate development affordable to lower income
            households. The element should provide specific examples with the densities
            and affordability. For sites expected to be aggregated, the element must describe
            circumstances leading to consolidation, such as common ownership, the City’s
            role or track record in facilitating small lot consolidation, policies or incentives
            offered or proposed to encourage and facilitate lot consolidation, specific
            examples of projects that were built for lower-income households on similarly
            sized sites, densities and affordability and relate those examples back to the
            sites inventory.

            CalTrans Site: The element was revised to include estimated timing and the
            process for decertification on the CalTrans site. HCD also received additional
            revisions to Action 1.C (Caltrans Gateway Site Development); however as these
            changes were not made available to the public pursuant to Government Code
            section 65585, subdivision (b), HCD could not fully consider them as part of this
            review. In addition, HCD had received several third-party comments related to
            the suitability of this site for development. In order to demonstrate the availability
            of this site for residential development, the element should include evidence from
            CalTrans to determine that the site will be available for development within the
            planning period, and demonstrate that decertification of this site by Caltrans will
            happen within two years from the start of the planning period or that if this does
            not occur by that timeframe, the City will identify additional sites in the inventory
            to accommodate the lower-income housing need by January 31, 2026. The
            element must include a clear steps to analyze whether decertification will occur
            by January 2025 and when the site will be available for residential development
            or identify additional sites to meet by-right shortfall requirements pursuant to
            Government Code section 65583.2, subdivision (h) and (i).

            Suitability of Sites: Please note, that the East Bay for Everyone comment letter
            notes several sites which may be inaccurately depicted. The City should verify
            the status of the sites and amend the sites inventory and analysis accordingly.

            Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete site
            analysis. Depending upon the results of that analysis, the City may need to add
            or revise programs.

         2. An analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints upon the
            maintenance, improvement, or development of housing for all income levels,
            including… …land use controls… …fees and other exactions required of
            developers, and local processing and permit procedures... (Gov. Code, § 65583,
            subd. (a)(5).)

            Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
            nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and
            development of housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for
            persons with disabilities... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

            Land Use Controls: While the element was revised to analyze lot width and
            depths in the RM and SH overlay as a constraint, analysis is needed regarding
            heights limited to 27 feet. Heights that limit development to two stories in
            multifamily zones are a constraint on development and a program must be added
            to allow at least three stories in zones allowing multifamily developments,
            including but not limited to the RH-25 zone. Based on the outcome of the
            analysis, the element should add or modify programs to address the constraint.

            Program 4.G (Monitor Development Fees): While the element was revised to add
            an analysis of multifamily development fees, it demonstrated that multifamily
            development fees are significantly higher than single family fees as total
            development cost. The City must revise program 4.G to commit to modifying
            standards and reducing fees based on conversations with developers and
            applicants.

      The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it
      has been revised and adopted to comply with the above requirements.

      For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
      a local government fails to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
      statutory deadline (January 31, 2023), then any rezoning to make prior identified sites
      available or accommodate the regional housing needs allocation (RHNA) shall be
      completed no later than one year from the statutory deadline pursuant to Government
      Code sections 65583, subdivision (c) and 65583.2, subdivision (c). Otherwise, the local
      government’s housing element will no longer comply with State Housing Element Law,
      and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial compliance pursuant to Government
      Code section 65585, subdivision (i). Please be aware, if the City fails to adopt a
      compliant housing element within one year from the statutory deadline, the element
      cannot be found in substantial compliance until these rezones are completed.

      For your information, if the housing element relies upon nonvacant sites to
      accommodate more than 50 percent of the RHNA for lower-income households, the
      housing element must demonstrate existing uses, based on substantial evidence, are
      not an impediment to additional residential development in the planning period and will
      likely discontinue during the planning period (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd. (g)(2).).

      Absent findings (e.g., adoption resolution) based on substantial evidence, the existing
      uses will be presumed to impede additional residential development and will not be
      utilized toward demonstrating adequate sites to accommodate the RHNA. The City must
      make these findings as part of its adoption resolution.

      Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
      element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
      process, the City must continue to engage the community, including organizations that
      represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
      available while considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be
      aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local government’s website
      and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested
      notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
      submitting to HCD.

      For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
      element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
      the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
      Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
      https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.

      Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
      compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
      (SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
      Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
      Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
      requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
      element, the City meets housing element requirements for these and other funding
      sources.
