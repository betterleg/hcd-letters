         RE: City of Bishop’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Bishop’s (City) draft housing element received for
         review on February 4, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision
         (b), the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is
         reporting the results of its review. Our review was facilitated by a conversation on
         March 23, 2022 with Associate Planner Elaine Kabala and consultant Sandra Bauer.

         The draft element addresses many of the statutory requirements listed in HCD’s
         November 24, 2021 review; however, revisions will be necessary to comply with State
         Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code), as described below.

             1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15
                (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
                assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
                (c)(10)(A).)

                  Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
                  throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,
                  religion, sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or
                  disability, and other characteristics... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

                  Regional Patterns and Trends: The element is revised to include a general
                  description of certain fair housing conditions in Inyo County. However, this
                  analysis is limited, and does not compare most fair housing conditions at the
                  local level to the rest of the region. The element must be revised to make this
                  comparison through data and analysis for all categories of the Assessment of
                  Fair Housing (segregation and integration; racially/ethnic concentrated areas of
                  poverty (R/ECAPs); access to opportunity; and disproportionate housing needs,
                  including displacement). Please see HCD’s prior review for additional
                  information. HCD will provide sample tables and analysis under separate cover.

      Site Inventory: The element is revised to state that all sites are located in high- or
      moderate-resource areas, but otherwise does not address this requirement. The
      analysis must identify whether sites improve or exacerbate fair housing
      conditions and whether sites are isolated by income category. A full analysis
      should address the income categories of identified sites with respect to location
      and how that affects existing patterns for all components of the Assessment of
      Fair Housing (segregation and integration; R/ECAPs; access to opportunity; and
      disproportionate housing needs, including displacement). HCD will provide a
      sample table and analysis under separate cover.

      Goals, Actions, Metrics and Milestones: Goals and actions must significantly
      seek to overcome contributing factors to fair housing issues. New program
      actions include pursuing grant funding and exploring a truck bypass outside of
      the city’s residential areas. This is not adequate to satisfy the requirement for
      specific and meaningful actions. For more information, please see HCD’s prior
      review.

      Additionally, the element is revised to state that metrics and milestones will be
      captured in the City’s Annual Progress Reports (APRs). This is not adequate to
      meet this requirement. Programs must include metrics and milestones for
      targeting meaningful affirmatively furthering fair housing (AFFH) outcomes and
      evaluating progress and effectiveness of programs on fair housing results. HCD
      will provide samples under separate cover.

   2. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including
      vacant sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for
      redevelopment during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for
      a designated income level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and
      public facilities and services to these sites. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

      Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning
      period with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and
      facilities to accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the
      regional housing need for each income level that could not be accommodated on
      sites identified in the inventory... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

      Adequate Sites for Accommodating the Regional Housing Needs Allocation
      (RHNA): The current draft of the inventory does not meet the City’s RHNA by
      income category, being short 2 units for the lower-income RHNA category (p.
      69). The element must be revised to include adequate sites to meet the City’s
      RHNA by income category.

      Realistic Capacity: The element is revised to adjust its capacity assumptions for
      several sites downward by 25 percent to account for infrastructure and

      circulation, and assumes 50 percent of the allowable density on one site.
      However, the element must also demonstrate how the City arrived at these

      Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete site
      analysis; therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning was not established. Based

   3. The Housing Element shall contain programs which assist in the development of
      adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and
      moderate-income households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

      The element is not revised to include programs to (1) assist in the development
      of housing for extremely low-income (ELI) households and households with
      special needs and (2) conserve and improve the condition of the existing stock.
      Please see HCD’s prior review for additional information.

   4. Establish the number of housing units, by income level, that can be constructed,
      rehabilitated, and conserved over a five-year time frame. (Gov. Code, § 65583,
      subd. (b)(1 & 2).)

      The element is revised to include quantified objectives for new construction, but it
      must also provide these objectives for rehabilitation and conservation.

The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it
has been revised and re-adopted to comply with the above requirements and re-
adopted.

As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due April 30, 2021. As of
today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the City to revise the element as described above, adopt, and submit to
HCD to regain housing element compliance.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
a local government fails to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
statutory deadline (October 15, 2021), then any rezoning to accommodate the RHNA,
including for lower-income households, shall be completed no later than one year from
the statutory deadline. Otherwise, the local government’s housing element will no longer
comply with State Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial
compliance pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i).

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the City should continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
available and considering and incorporating comments where appropriate.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
http://opr.ca.gov/docs/OPR_Appendix_C_final.pdf and
http://opr.ca.gov/docs/Final_6.26.15.pdf.
