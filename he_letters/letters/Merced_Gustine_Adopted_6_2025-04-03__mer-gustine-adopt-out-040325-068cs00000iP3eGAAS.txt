      Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: The element must include an analysis
      demonstrating the potential for redevelopment of nonvacant sites. While the
      element now generally explains that nonvacant sites are similar to other
      nonvacant sites that have been redeveloped, it should also analyze the extent
      existing uses impede additional development and current market demand for the
      existing use. Based on our meeting, current uses are nonoperating and fallow.
      The element should explain these circumstances and conditions to better
      demonstrate the potential for redevelopment in the planning period.

      Additionally, the housing element relies upon nonvacant sites to accommodate
      more than 50 percent of the regional housing needs allocation (RHNA) for lower-
      income households. Absent findings (e.g., adoption resolution) based on
      substantial evidence, the existing uses will be presumed to impede additional
      residential development and will not be utilized toward demonstrating adequate
      sites to accommodate the RHNA. The City should make these findings as part of
      future readoption of the element. For a sample finding, please see HCD’s
      Housing Element Site Inventory Guidebook (p. 27) at
      https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-
      element-memos/docs/sites_inventory_memo_final06102020.pdf.

      Local Processing and Permit Procedures: The element (p. D-4-32) now explains
      the Planned Development (PD) process is required for all projects in the PD zone
      and sites are mapped with the PD zone without fixed development standards.
      The lack of fixed standards impacts approval certainty, timing and cost of
      development. The element should analyze these impacts and add or modify
      actions to address the constraint. For example, a program could commit to create
      default development standards within the PD zone or replace PD zoned sites
      with an alternative zone that contains development standards.

      In addition, the element should address public comments on the prior submittal
      and discuss compliance with the Permit Streamlining Act and intersections with
      CEQA and timing requirements, including streamlining determinations and add or
      modify programs as appropriate.

      On/Off-Site Improvements: The element now lists various requirements for on
      and off-site improvements but must still analyze the cost impact of on and off-
      site improvements on total development costs. The City may include estimates of
      this data if there is no other specific information available about cost impacts.

The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once
rezones have been completed to meet all requirements (See Below) and the element
has been revised, readopted, submitted to, and reviewed by HCD to comply with the
above requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

A jurisdiction that did not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days from the
statutory deadline must rezone sites within one year of the statutory deadline and meet
requirements pursuant to Government Code sections 65583, subdivision (c)(1)(A) and
65583.2, subdivision (c). As this year has passed and Program 2 (Adequate Sites for
RHNA and Monitoring of No Net Loss) has not been completed, the housing element is
out of compliance and will remain out of compliance until the rezoning has been
completed. Once the City completes the rezone, a copy of the resolution or ordinance
should be transmitted to HCD. HCD will review the documentation and issue
correspondence identifying the updated status of the City housing element compliance.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. During the housing element revision
process, the City must continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
available while considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be
aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local government’s website
and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested
notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
submitting to HCD.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. The City currently meets
housing element requirements for the Community Development Block Grant Program
and with a substantially compliant housing element, the City will meet housing element
requirements for other funding sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Land Use and Climate Innovation at:
https://www.lci.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.
