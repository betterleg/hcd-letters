         Thank you for submitting the Town of Los Altos Hills’ (Town) draft amendment to the
         housing element update that was received for review on August 11, 2025. Pursuant to
         Government Code section 65585, the California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. In addition, HCD considered
         comments from California Housing Defense Fund, The League of Women Voters of Los
         Altos-Mountain View Area, Kevin Ma, Harsukhdeep and Sara Ratia, Silicon Valley is
         Home, pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (c). In addition, HCD
         considered the Town’s October 3, 2025 response to commenters.

         On May 30, 2023, HCD found the Town’s housing element in substantial compliance
         with State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq) and as of this writing, the
         Town continues to comply with State Housing Element Law. This draft amendment
         continues to meet most statutory requirements. For example, the draft amendment
         demonstrates the suitability of large sites (greater than ten acre) to accommodate the
         lower-income regional housing need allocation (RHNA), including programs to facilitate
         developments affordable to lower-income households. However, this draft amendment
         may impact HCD’s prior finding of substantial compliance and should be revised, as
         follows:

   Nonvacant Sites: The existing adopted housing element largely relies on interest
   from developers or property owners on identified sites to demonstrate the potential
   for redevelopment on nonvacant sites proposed to be rezoned to accommodate the
   RHNA. The draft amendment appears to continue this assumption that developers
   or property owners are interested in developing identified nonvacant sites. However,
   conditions and circumstances have changed. For example, all sites for the Twin
   Oaks Court/Arastradero Road sites are no longer available and the remaining site
   acreage appears to be more influenced by existing uses. Further, the allowable
   density has been reduced from 30 units per acre to 24 units per acre; potentially
   affecting financial feasibility. For these reasons, the element should evaluate
   whether developers or property owners continue to be interested in redevelopment
   on all nonvacant sites proposed to be rezoned to accommodate the RHNA (e.g.,
   Twin Oaks, Foothill College, Saint Nicholas). If developers or property owners
   continue to be interested in redevelopment, then no additional analysis is necessary.
   If developers or property owners are no longer interested in redevelopment, then the
   element must demonstrate the potential for redevelopment on all nonvacant sites
   proposed to be rezoned to accommodate the RHNA. The analysis must address the
   extent to which existing uses may constitute an impediment to additional residential
   development, past experience with converting existing uses to higher density
   residential development, the current market demand for the existing use, an analysis
   of any existing leases, other contracts or other conditions that would perpetuate the
   existing use or prevent redevelopment of the site for additional residential
   development, development trends, market conditions, and regulatory or other
   incentives or standards to encourage additional residential development on these
   sites. If the analysis concludes that sites do not have potential for redevelopment,
   the element should identify and analyze the suitability of other sites. Based on the
   outcomes of a complete analysis, the element should add or modify programs to
   make sites available to address a shortfall of sites or zoning available to encourage
   a variety of housing types and accommodate the RHNA.

   Electronic Site Inventory: Pursuant to Government Code section 65583.3,
   subdivision (b), the Town must utilize standards, forms, and definitions adopted by
   HCD when preparing the sites inventory and submit an electronic version of the sites
   inventory. While the Town has submitted an electronic version of the sites inventory,
   since changes will occur, future re-adopted versions of the element must also submit
   the electronic version of the sites inventory.

The draft amendment will not negatively impact HCD’s finding of substantial compliance
with State Housing Element Law once the amendment has been revised to address the
above requirements, adopted, submitted, and reviewed by HCD to comply with the
above requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

Additionally, Programs A-1 (Adequate Sites) and A-4 (By Right Approvals) must still be
completed by January 31, 2026. Program A-1 (Adequate Sites) commits to rezoning
sites in excess of the RHNA to make adequate sites available to accommodate the
RHNA and promote housing mobility beyond the RHNA, including housing choices and
affordability, to affirmatively further fair housing (AFFH). The Program will also conduct
a mid-cycle review of the effectiveness of sites and zoning, including progress toward
development in the planning period and identify additional incentives or complete
rezoning, if necessary, within one year. This program commits to rezone at least 19
acres to permit multifamily developments with 20 percent affordability without
discretionary action and require a minimum density of 20 units per acre to address the
identified shortfall of 249 units, 92 of which are units affordable to lower income. Please
be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD authority to
review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
inconsistent with an adopted housing element or housing element law. This includes
failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke
housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state
law.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the Town must continue to engage the community, including commenters on
this draft amendment and organizations that represent lower-income and special needs
households, by making information regularly available while considering and
incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be aware, any revisions to the
element must be posted on the local government’s website and to email a link to all
individuals and organizations that have previously requested notices relating to the local
government’s housing element at least seven days before submitting to HCD.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the Town continues to meet housing element requirements for these and other
funding sources.
