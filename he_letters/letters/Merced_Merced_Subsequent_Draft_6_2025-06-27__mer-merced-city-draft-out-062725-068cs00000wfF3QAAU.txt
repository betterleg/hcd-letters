         RE: City of Merced’s 6th Cycle (2024-2032) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Merced’s (City) revised draft housing element
         received for review on April 30, 2025. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, the
         California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the
         results of its review. In addition, HCD considered comments from Leadership Counsel
         for Justice and Accountability pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision
         (c).

         The revised draft element addresses many statutory requirements described in HCD’s
         November 7, 2024 review; however, additional revisions are necessary to substantially
         comply with State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq), as follows:

         1. Review the previous element to evaluate the appropriateness, effectiveness, and
            progress in implementation, and reflect the results of this review in the revised
            element. (Gov. Code, § 65588 (a) and (b).)

              General: The prior review found that the element must evaluate the progress and
              effectiveness of the programs in meeting the objectives of the prior housing element,
              including a complete analysis of prior objectives versus actual results. While the
              element included some minimal additional information on the progress in
              implementation, it must still address the effectiveness of Programs including but not
              limited to Programs 1.2c and 1.2d. Based on the outcomes of this evaluation,
              programs should be revised as appropriate. Please see HCD’s prior review for
              additional information.

         2. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing
            with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair
            housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A).)

   Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
   throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,
   religion, sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or
   disability, and other characteristics… (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

   Racial/Ethnic Areas of Concentration of Poverty (R/ECAP) and Racially
   Concentrated Areas of Affluence (RCAAs): The element now includes some
   demographics and discusses proximity to transit routes related to R/ECAPs in the
   City, but it should also include a specific analysis to better formulate policies and
   programs. A specific analysis should address comparisons to other neighborhoods
   in terms of quality of life (e.g., infrastructure, housing and neighborhood conditions,
   parks, community amenities, safe routes to school, environmental health) and
   incorporate local data and knowledge and other relevant factors. The element must
   add or modify significant and meaningful programs based on the outcomes of this
   analysis. Additionally, the element must still address RCAAs and include specific
   analysis. Please see HCD’s prior review for additional information.

   Disproportionate Housing Needs Including Displacement Risks: The element
   includes some general information on persons experiencing homelessness but
   should also address access to services and programs. Specifically, the element
   should discuss patterns of need and any gaps in providing services to those areas.
   The element should revise programs accordingly with geographic targeting.

   Contributing Factors: Based on the outcome of a complete analysis, the element
   should reassess and, particularly prioritize contributing factors to fair housing issues
   then formulate appropriate policies and programs.

   Programs: As noted above, the element must complete an analysis of affirmatively
   furthering fair housing (AFFH). Depending on the results of a complete analysis, the
   City may need to revise or add programs.

   In addition, while the element includes some meaningful actions to AFFH, it should
   expand and expand housing mobility actions, place-based strategies toward
   community revitalization and programs to address displacement risk. For example,
   the element should commit to applying for funding (beyond prioritizing the capital
   improvement program) at least every other year toward broader community
   development issues such as infrastructure, neighborhood revitalization, parks, safe
   routes to school, community facilities and pollution burdens. Examples of housing
   mobility actions include promoting more housing choices and affordability in lower-
   density areas, religious institutional sites, city-owned sites, and enhancing accessory
   dwelling units (ADU), junior accessory dwelling units (JADU) or additional
   conversion of existing single-family space, and home sharing strategies.

   Housing Conditions: While the element appears to estimate the number of units in
   need of rehabilitation based on Census data for age and tenure, it should consider
   local data and knowledge to better estimate the number of units in need of
   rehabilitation and replacement. For example, the element could rely on the
   knowledge and expertise of code enforcement officials. Please see HCD’s prior
   review for additional information.

4. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including
   vacant sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for redevelopment
   during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for a designated
   income level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and public facilities and
   services to these sites. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

   Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period
   with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities
   to accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing
   need for each income level that could not be accommodated on sites identified in the
   inventory completed pursuant to paragraph (3) of subdivision (a) without rezoning,
   and to comply with the requirements of Government Code section 65584.09. Sites
   shall be identified as needed to facilitate and encourage the development of a
   variety of types of housing for all income levels, including multifamily rental housing,
   factory-built housing, mobilehomes, housing for agricultural employees, supportive
   housing, single-room occupancy units, emergency shelters, and transitional housing.
   (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

   Progress in Meeting the Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA): To
   demonstrate the availability of pending projects in the planning period, the element
   must still address any remaining steps to completion, anticipated completion, and
   likelihood of development given past trends of approvals not resulting in built units.

   Realistic Capacity: While the element discusses residential development trends in
   nonresidential zones, including sufficient buffer for development, the analysis still
   includes the assumption that 100 percent of sites will develop with residential uses,
   despite the trends and patterns indicating that residential uses occur less than a
   quarter of the time. The element should adjust the calculation of realistic residential
   capacity based on trends in nonresidential zones. See HCD’s prior review for more
   information.

   Electronic Site Inventory: Pursuant to Government Code section 65583.3,
   subdivision (b), upon adoption of the housing element, the City must submit an
   electronic version of the sites inventory with its adopted housing element to
   sitesinventory@hcd.ca.gov. The City must utilize standards, forms, and definitions
   adopted by HCD. While the City has submitted an electronic version of the sites
   inventory, if changes occur, any future adopted versions of the element must also
   submit the electronic version of the sites inventory. The City can reach out to HCD at
   sitesinventory@hcd.ca.gov for technical assistance.

   Sites with Zoning for a Variety of Housing Types (Emergency Shelters): The element
   was not revised to address this finding. Please see HCD’s November 7, 2024 review
   for additional information.

   Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete site analysis;
   therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the
   results of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City may need to add or
   revise programs to address a shortfall of sites or zoning available to encourage a
   variety of housing types. In addition, the element should be revised as follows:

      •   Annexation: The City included Program 2 (Adequate Sites for RHNA and
          Monitoring of No Net Loss) and Program 3 (By-Right Approval) for rezones
          and by-right requirements. However, if any identified sites require annexation,
          the Programs should commit to actions for annexation, including remaining
          steps, discrete timing, alternative actions, and a specified date if the sites
          used to accommodate the RHNA are not annexed early in the planning
          period.
      •   Infrastructure: The City included Program 12 (Water and Sewer Service) for
          water and sewer services within City limits. However, the Program should
          commit to actions for wastewater system and potable water system
          improvements that are consistent with the analysis on page G6-35.
      •   Downtown Zoning: The element includes downtown and urban village zones
          in the analysis but does not include any land currently zoned for these in the
          Zoning Map. The element could either consider a program to apply these
          zones to further encourage infill development or other actions and incentives
          to promote infill development.
      •   Program 6 (Zoning Code Amendments): This Program should be revised to
          address parking requirements for emergency shelters as a constraint and
          correct the statutory cite from Government Code 65621 to Government Code
          65651. Please see HCD’s prior review for additional information.

   use controls, building codes and their enforcement, site improvements, fees and
   other exactions required of developers, and local processing and permit
   procedures... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(5).)

   Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
   nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and development of
   housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for persons with
   disabilities... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

   Land Use Controls: While the element now includes some analysis about the
   cumulative impacts of land use controls as potential constraints on a variety of
   housing types, it should also specifically analyze height requirements in the R-4
   zone. The analysis should address setbacks and development standards related to
   construction not exceeding the plane established by a one-to-one height and
   setback ratio as referenced on page G4-15. Program 6 (Zoning Code Amendments)
   should be modified as appropriate following a complete analysis to address
   identified constraints.

   Local Processing and Permit Procedures: The element was not revised to address
   this finding. Please see HCD’s November 7, 2024 review for additional information.

   On/Off-Site Improvements: The prior review found that the element should list typical
   off-site improvement requirements. The element now states that on and off-site
   improvements can amount to 10 to 45 percent of total development costs then
   concludes on and off-site improvements are not a constraint on development.
   However, 45 percent of total development costs is a significant proportion and the
   element should closely examine whether significant proportions (e.g., greater than
   30 percent) are atypical and, if typical, analyze contributing factors to significant
   proportions and add or modify programs to address identified constraints.

   Programs: As noted above, the element requires a complete analysis of potential
   governmental constraints. Depending upon the results of that analysis, the City may
   need to revise or add programs and address and remove or mitigate any identified
   constraints.

6. The Housing Element shall contain programs which assist in the development of
   adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and
   moderate-income households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

   Program 1 (Regional Collaboration): While the program now includes how often the
   City will collaborate with regional partners, including developers, it should still
   consider efforts to AFFH throughout the planning period.

   Special Housing Needs: The City included Program 10 (Lower-Income and Special
   Needs Housing) to assist in the development of housing to meet the needs of
   extremely low, very low- and low-income households. However, the Program must
   be modified to include actions to address homelessness and persons with
   disabilities and expand actions for farmworkers. Please see HCD’s prior review for
   additional information.

7. The housing program shall preserve for low-income household the assisted housing
   developments identified pursuant to paragraph (9) of subdivision (a)... (Gov. Code, §
   65583, subd. (c)(6).)

   Program 8 (Preservation of At-Risk Housing): This Program should be revised to
   commit to outreach and coordination with qualified entities that can acquire and
   manage at-risk developments and assistance with funding.

8. Establish the number of housing units, by income level, that can be constructed,
   rehabilitated, and conserved over a five-year time frame. (Gov. Code, § 65583,
   subd. (b)(1 & 2).)

   While the element includes conservation objectives in Table G7-1, conservation
   objectives should not be limited to preservation of units at-risk of conversion to
   market rate uses. Conservation objectives may include the variety of strategies
   employed by the City to promote tenant stability and conserve the existing stock
   such as code enforcement, housing vouchers and weatherization.

9. Local governments shall make a diligent effort to achieve public participation of all
   economic segments of the community in the development of the Housing Element,
   and the element shall describe this effort. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.(c)(9).)

   While the element includes information about meetings and hearings that occurred,
   it should still list stakeholders and service providers in Merced that were utilized in
   the public participation process and work to provide materials in all the most
   common languages in the City. The element should also include what efforts were
   made to target lower-income and special needs households and any additional
   methods utilized for outreach. See HCD’s prior review for more information.

The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once
rezones have been completed to meet all requirements (see below) and the element
has been revised, adopted, submitted to, and reviewed by HCD to comply with the
above requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

A jurisdiction that did not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days from the
statutory deadline (January 31, 2024) must rezone sites within one year of the statutory
deadline and meet requirements pursuant to Government Code sections 65583,
subdivision (c)(1)(A) and 65583.2, subdivisions (c), (h) and (i), as applicable. As this
year has passed, the housing element cannot be found in substantial compliance until
rezoning has been completed to meet all statutory requirements. HCD understands the
City has completed rezoning pursuant to Programs 2 (Adequate Sites for RHNA) and 3
(By Right Approval). As a result, the City should submit documentation of rezoning (e.g.,
resolution, ordinance) as part of upcoming submittals of the housing element and HCD
will incorporate the documentation in future review.

As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due January 31, 2024. As of
today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the City to revise the element as described above, adopt, and submit to
HCD to regain housing element compliance.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the City should continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
available and considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be
aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local government’s website
and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested
notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
submitting to HCD.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Affordable Housing and Sustainable
Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing Allocation consider
housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing element, the City will meet
housing element requirements for these and other funding sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Land Use and Climate Innovation at:
https://www.lci.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.

HCD appreciates the continued coordination and support of the housing element update
team to revise the housing element as part of the multi-jurisdictional housing element
process. We are committed to assisting the City in addressing all statutory requirements
of State Housing Element Law. If you have any questions or need additional technical
assistance, please contact Andrea Grant, of our staff, at andrea.grant@hcd.ca.gov.
