         RE: City of Taft’s 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Taft’s (City) draft housing element that was received
         for review on September 5, 2024. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, the
         California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the
         results of its review. In addition, HCD considered comments from Faith in the Valley –
         Kern County and David Kellogg pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

         The draft element addresses many statutory requirements; however, revisions will be
         necessary to substantially comply with State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, §
         65580 et seq), as follows:

             1. Review the previous element to evaluate the appropriateness, effectiveness, and
                progress in implementation, and reflect the results of this review in the revised
                element. (Gov. Code, § 65588 (a) and (b).)

                  Special Housing Needs: As part of the evaluation of programs in the past cycle,
                  the element must also provide an explanation of the cumulative effectiveness of
                  goals, policies, and related actions in meeting the housing needs of special
                  needs populations (e.g., elderly, persons with disabilities, large households,
                  female-headed households, farmworkers, and persons experiencing
                  homelessness). While the element briefly describes the accomplishments of a
                  few past programs for housing for persons with special needs; a complete
                  analysis must provide an evaluation of the cumulative effectiveness of these
                  strategies in meeting the housing needs of all special-needs populations. For
                  example, the element mentions that objectives of the various programs have
                  been met, it could discuss those objectives, provide examples of how the
                  objectives were met and whether the programs were effective or need to be
                  adjusted. Programs should be revised as appropriate to reflect the results of this
                  evaluation.

   2. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including
      vacant sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for
      redevelopment during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for
      a designated income level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and
      public facilities and services to these sites. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

      Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning
      period with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and
      facilities to accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the
      regional housing need for each income level... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
      (c)(1).)

      Realistic Capacity: To calculate realistic capacity, the element assumes 70 percent
      of the maximum allowable densities on residential zoned sites and 50 percent of
      the maximum allowable densities on non-residential zoned sites. However, the
      element should support these assumptions or adjust the assumption as
      appropriate based on trends in the City or broader area. For example, the element
      could list developments in the broader region by zone, allowable density, built
      density and affordability.

      In addition, the element appears to assume residential development on sites with
      zoning that allows 100 percent non-residential uses and must account for the
      likelihood of 100 percent non-residential uses. For example, the element could
      analyze all development activity in these non-residential zones, how often
      residential development occurs and adjust residential capacity calculations,
      policies, and programs accordingly. The analysis may utilize trends in the
      broader region.

      Large Sites: Sites larger than ten acres in size are deemed inadequate to
      accommodate housing for lower-income housing unless it is demonstrated that
      sites of equivalent size and affordability were successfully developed during the
      prior planning period or unless the housing element describes other evidence to
      HCD that the site is adequate to accommodate lower income housing (Gov.
      Code, § 65583.2, subd. (c)(2)(A).). While the element mentions large sites will be
      developed with varying housing types and only categorizes 20 percent of the site
      toward the lower-income regional housing need allocation (RHNA), it should also
      support these assumptions. For example, the element could discuss
      development and affordability trends in the broader region on similarly sized and
      situated sites. The element could also discuss how large sites will be parceled at
      appropriate sizes (e.g., 1-10 acres) to facilitate affordability.

      Public Lands: The element mentions several City-owned sites and that the City
      has worked with developers in the past but should also evaluate the suitability

      and availability of City-Owned sites. Specifically, the analysis should address
      their status (e.g., whether surplused or not), remaining steps to facilitate
      development and any known barriers or conditions that preclude development in
      the planning period.

      Nonvacant Sites: While the element demonstrates the potential for
      redevelopment on Sites A and B, it should still evaluate the extent existing uses
      impede additional development on Sites C and D that currently operate as a
      parking lot. Specifically, the element notes the Sites have operated as a parking
      lot for nearby commercial buildings but should also discuss whether that current
      use impedes additional development such as whether any leases, contracts, title
      conditions or other circumstances preclude development in the planning period.
      The element could also discuss whether parking is available in the area if parking
      discontinues on the identified sites or how the parking could be replaced with
      redevelopment of the property.

      Zoning Appropriate for Lower-Income Households: The element must
      demonstrate densities appropriate to accommodate housing for lower-income
      households. For communities with densities that meet specific standards (at least
      20 units per acre for Taft), no analysis is required. (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd.
      (c)(3).) Otherwise, to utilize the zone toward the lower-income RHNA, an analysis
      must demonstrate appropriate densities based on factors such as market
      demand, financial feasibility and development experience within identified zones.
      Analysis is generally not required to demonstrate the appropriateness of the
      mixed-use zones since allowable densities are 29 units per acre. However, the
      element appears to indicate that the maximum allowable density in the Zone C
      airport compatibility area is 15 units per acre – less than 20 units per acre -
      where at least two sites are utilized toward the lower-income RHNA. The element
      should either explain that maximum densities may exceed 15 units per acre,
      demonstrate the appropriateness of 15 units per acre as described above or
      remove the sites from being used toward the lower-income RHNA.

      Electronic Sites Inventory: For your information, pursuant to Government Code
      section 65583.3, subdivision (b), the City must utilize standards, forms, and
      definitions adopted by HCD when preparing the sites inventory and submit an
      electronic version of the sites inventory. While the City has submitted an
      electronic version of the sites inventory, if changes occur, any future re-adopted
      versions of the element must also submit the electronic version of the sites
      inventory.

      Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete site
      analysis; therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established.
      Based on the results of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City may
      need to add or revise programs to address a shortfall of sites or zoning available

      to encourage a variety of housing types. In addition, the element should be

          •   Program 1.b (Acquisition and Development of City Owned Property):
              While the Program commits to advertise sites and various other actions, it
              should include a specific schedule of actions, including discrete timelines.
              Examples of actions include disposition of sites, release of requests for

              with predevelopment and funding, facilitating entitlements and issuing

              and “consider” additional measures. However, the Program should clearly

              period and commit to when additional measures will be completed, if
              necessary. Finally, additional measures should include identify or rezoning

          •   Program 1.d (Acquisition and Development of Large Lot Properties): The
              Program should clearly commit to appropriate parcel sizes (e.g., 1-10
              acres) and discrete timelines for establishing incentives, coordination with
              property owners and adding incentives if necessary, upon future
              evaluation of program effectiveness.

   3. An analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints upon the
      maintenance, improvement, or development of housing for all income levels,
      including the types of housing identified in paragraph (1) of subdivision (c), and
      for persons with disabilities as identified in the analysis pursuant to paragraph
      (7), including… …local processing and permit procedures... (Gov. Code, §
      65583, subd. (a)(5).)

      Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
      nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and
      development of housing... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

      Processing and Permit Procedures: The element describes the Site Plan Review
      process, including listing approval findings but should also evaluate the approval
      findings for impacts on approval certainty. For example, the element should
      analyze the application of findings such as “…not impair the integrity and
      character of the zone…” and “…site is suitable for the type and intensity of use or
      development proposed.” The analysis should also discuss how the City promotes
      approval certainty as part of its Project Assistance Team (PAT) process. Based
      on the outcomes of a complete analysis, the element should add or modify
      programs to address identified constraints.

   4. The housing element shall contain programs which assist in the development of
      adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and
      moderate-income households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

      Programs should be added or modified to assist in the development of housing
      for lower-income and special needs households, as follows:

          •   Program 9 (Housing Production, Streamlining, and Incentives): The
              Program should commit to discrete timing (e.g., at least every other year,
              with ongoing efforts) for Program 9 (Housing Production, Streamlining,
              and Incentives). This includes discrete timelines for Program 9.a (Multi-
              Family Housing Project), Program 9.c (Self-Help Housing), Program 9.d
              (Senior Housing Project), and Program 9.g (Developmental Disabilities).

          •   Program 9.o (Farmworker Housing): The Program should be expanded
              with specific commitment, including numerical objectives and discrete
              timelines. Examples of actions include proactively identifying development
              or housing opportunities on an annual basis, coordinating with employers
              and other regional entities to enhance farmworker housing strategies and
              funding and pursuing strategies to integrate housing for farmworkers into
              affordable housing.

   5. Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
      throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,
      religion, sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or
      disability, and other characteristics... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

      Programs: The element should add or modify programs with place-based
      strategies for community revitalization. Examples of programs include broader
      neighborhood improvements such as streets, sidewalks, safe routes to school,
      active transportation, streetscapes, parks, community facilities and amenities,
      community programs, public art, infrastructure and air quality mitigation.

      In addition, several programs should geographically target efforts to promote
      more inclusive and equitable communities. Examples include geographically
      targeting rehabilitation efforts in older areas of the City or promoting a variety of
      housing types in newer areas of the City or throughout the City. Programs that
      should be revised with geographic targeting include Programs 2.a (Housing
      Rehabilitation – Funding), 2.b (Housing Rehabilitation – Public Information), 3.b
      (First Time Homebuyer), 4.a (Housing Choice Vouchers), 4.b (Rental
      Assistance), 6.c (Accessory Dwelling Units) and 9.e (Funding for New Low-
      income Development).

   6. Establish the number of housing units, by income level, that can be constructed,
      rehabilitated, and conserved over a five-year time frame. (Gov. Code, § 65583,
      subd. (b)(1 & 2).)

      The element should still consider additional conservation objectives. These

      enforcement, rental assistance and weatherization efforts.

   7. Local governments shall make a diligent effort to achieve public participation of
      all economic segments of the community in the development of the Housing
      Element, and the element shall describe this effort. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
      (c)(9).)

      Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the
      housing element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the
      housing element process, the City must continue to engage the community,
      including organizations that represent lower-income and special needs
      households, by making information regularly available while considering and
      incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be aware, any revisions to
      the element must be posted on the local government’s website and to email a
      link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested notices
      relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
      submitting to HCD.

      In addition, while the element generally describes outreach efforts including key
      takeaways and comments, it should also discuss how those comments were
      incorporated into the element. Finally, HCD urges the City to consider comments.
      Finally, HCD received comments on this revised draft and the City should
      consider these comments and make adjustments, as appropriate. Examples of
      topics include community engagement, programs related to housing stability and
      affordability and methods to engage the community through the implementation
      of the element.


The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it
has been revised, adopted, submitted to, and reviewed by HCD to comply with the
above requirements, pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

As a reminder, the City’s 6th Cycle housing element was due December 31, 2023. As of
today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
City’s 6th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the City to revise the element, adopt, and submit to HCD to regain housing
element compliance.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Affordable Housing and Sustainable
Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing Allocation consider
housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing element, the City will meet
housing element requirements for these and other funding sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.

HCD appreciates the efforts of the City’s housing element team during the housing
element update. We are committed to assisting the City in addressing all statutory
requirements of State Housing Element Law. If you have any questions or need
additional technical assistance, please contact Margaret Scarpa, of our staff, at
Margaret.Scarpa@hcd.ca.gov.
