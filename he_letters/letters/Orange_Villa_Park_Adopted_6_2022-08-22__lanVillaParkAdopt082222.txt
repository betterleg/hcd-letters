         RE: City of Villa Park’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Villa Park’s (City) adopted housing element
         received for review on June 29, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585,
         subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing and Community Development
         (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. In addition, HCD considered comments from
         Kennedy Commission and Bonnie Robinson, pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, subdivision (c).

         The adopted element addresses many statutory requirements to address HCD’s
         January 6, 2022 review; however, revisions will be necessary to comply with State
         Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code), as follows:

             1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing
                with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair
                housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A).)

                  Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
                  throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,
                  religion, sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or
                  disability, and other characteristics... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

                  Enforcement and Outreach Capacity: While the element now describes
                  enforcement and outreach capacity, it must still describe how the City complies
                  with existing state and federal fair housing laws. For example, the element
                  should discuss compliance with the California Fair Employment and Housing Act
                  and Government Code sections 65008, 8899.50 and 11135. In addition, the
                  element should also address any findings, lawsuits, enforcement actions,
                  settlements, or judgments related to fair housing or civil rights. For more
                  information, see pages 28-30 on HCD’s AFFH Guidance Memo at
                  https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/affh/index.shtml.

      Racially Concentrated Areas of Affluence (RCAA): While the element reports
      data on concentrations of race and income and compares some statistics to the
      County, it must also evaluate this data to better formulate policies and programs.
      The element should discuss coincidence across various components (e.g.,
      segregation and integration and disproportionate housing needs) of the
      assessment of fair housing and utilizing outreach, local data and knowledge and
      other relevant factors such as historical zoning practices. This analysis should
      specifically address the disparities within the City and the disparities when the
      City is compared to the broader region. For example, northeastern Orange
      County has a significantly higher number of concentrated areas of affluence
      when compared to the rest of the County. The element should examine this
      relationship to better prioritize contributing factors to fair housing issues and
      formulate appropriate actions such as increasing new opportunities in higher
      opportunity areas (above and beyond the regional housing need allocation
      (RHNA)). HCD recommends the City utilize the new RCAA data available at
      https://www.hcd.ca.gov/affirmatively-furthering-fair-housing.

      Site Inventory and Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH): While the
      element includes some general discussion of the sites inventory and disparities
      in access to opportunity, it must evaluate the identified sites across all
      components of the assessment of fair housing, quantify and analyze impact at
      the locational level (e.g., neighborhood, census tract, etc.) and evaluate any
      isolation of the regional housing need allocation (RHNA).

      Contributing Factors to Fair Housing Issues: Based on the outcomes of a
      complete analysis, the element should re-assess and prioritize contributing factors
      to fair housing issues.

      Goals, Actions, Metrics, and Milestones: Based on the outcomes of a complete
      analysis, the element must be revised to add or modify goals and actions
      accordingly. Goals and actions must specifically respond to the analysis and to
      the identified and prioritized contributing factors to fair housing issues and must
      be significant and meaningful enough to overcome identified patterns and trends.
      For example, the element should have significant actions to promote new
      housing choices and affordability in higher opportunity areas (e.g., throughout the
      City). Actions must have appropriate specific commitment, milestones,
      geographic targeting and metrics.

      facilities to accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional
      housing need for each income level that could not be accommodated on sites
      identified in the inventory... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

      Nonvacant Sites: The element identifies two sites in the Town Center and
      mentions their potential for redevelopment based on the age of the structures and
      the existing floor area ratios and recent trend of one smaller mixed-use
      development in a nearby community. However, the element should discuss the
      extent existing uses impede additional development, how trends relate to
      identified sites and whether the policy framework is sufficient to attract
      redevelopment. Specially, the element should:

         •   Characteristics of Redevelopment: The element should clearly describe
             what kind of redevelopment is anticipated; whether the existing uses are
             anticipated to continue or discontinue (razing the structures). If the
             structures and uses or anticipated to continue, the element should discuss
             how development will occur given development standards, existing
             structures and available space.
         •   City Hall: The element should discuss how City Hall is planned to continue
             operating (e.g., replacement, relocation) and any impacts on the availability
             of the site in the planning period.
         •   Office Uses: While the element discusses an older structure and low floor
             area ratio, it should discuss how the use is anticipated to discontinue in the
             planning period such as interest from the property owner, development
             community, lease expiration or other indicators of turnover.
         •   Recent Trends: The element should include more than one example of
             recent trends and should relate those trends to the identified site and uses.
             For example, the description of trends should address the prior use and
             situation, age of structure, floor area ratio, zoning and allowable densities
             and other relevant factors that led to redevelopment and draw similarities to
             the identified sites or consider a different methodology or identify other
             sites.
         •   Policy Framework: The element should discuss whether the existing policy
             framework (e.g., allowable densities, floor area ratio, heights, coverages) is
             sufficient to encourage the intended future uses and add or modify
             programs as appropriate.

      Electronic Site Inventory: As noted in the prior review, pursuant to Government

      the element must also submit the electronic version of the sites inventory.

   3. An analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints upon the
      maintenance, improvement, or development of housing for all income levels,
      including… … land use controls... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(5).)

      Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
      nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and development
      of housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for persons with
      disabilities... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

      Land Use Controls: The element now states that development standards allow
      two to three story multifamily development of 20 to 24 units per acre without the
      need of a variance or other discretionary exception. The element also notes (p.
      IV-41) that 35 feet may be allowed with “Alternate Development Standard”; an
      apparent exception process that does not mention the number of stories. Program
      1 (Adequate Sites) commits to modify development standards without
      discretionary approval if the proposal is part of a density bonus request. Density
      Bonus law requires modifications to heights without discretionary approval. This
      Program essentially commits to only follow state law. Instead, the element should
      clearly identify whether three stories are allowed without any special process. If
      not, the element should include a program to modify heights to at least three
      stories and depending on the outcomes of the analysis under Findings 1 and 2,
      the program should consider heights of four stories.

      In addition, prior reviews found the lack of a variety of lot sizes can impact
      housing supply, choices and affordability. As a result, the element should include
      analysis of the impacts of an apparent predominant large lot land use pattern and
      include programs as appropriate.

The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it
has been revised and re-adopted to comply with the above requirements.

For your information, pursuant to Senate Bill 197 (Chapter 70, Statutes of 2022), as the
City did not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the statutory deadline
(October 15, 2021), as of this writing, any rezoning must be completed within one year
of the statutory deadline (October 15, 2022). This includes Program 1 (Adequate Sites).
However, if the City’s adopted element is found in compliance by October 15, 2022, the
City may use a rezone deadline of later than October 15, 2022. HCD encourages the
City to revise the element, adopt and expeditiously submit the housing element. If the
element is not found in compliance by October 15, 2022, HCD cannot find the element
in compliance until the rezoning is complete.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.
