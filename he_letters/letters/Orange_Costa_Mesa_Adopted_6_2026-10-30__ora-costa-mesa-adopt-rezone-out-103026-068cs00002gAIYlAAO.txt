         Thank you for submitting the City of Costa Mesa’s (City) amendment to the housing
         element that was adopted on September 1, 2026 and received for HCD’s review on
         September 2, 2026. The California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) also received Resolution Numbers 2026-38, 2026-51 and 2026-52,
         Ordinance Numbers 2026-03 and 2026-08, and various other documents (Zoning
         Documents) related to rezoning to accommodate the regional housing need allocation
         (RHNA). HCD also received and considered public comments from Disability Rights
         California, the Public Interest Law Project, Brancart and Brancart, and the Law Office of
         Autumn Elliott, pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (c). Pursuant
         to Government Code section 65585, HCD is reporting the results of its review.

         On May 9, 2023, HCD found the adopted housing element met the statutory
         requirements of State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq). Further, on
         August 21, 2026, HCD found draft amendments to the housing element did not impact
         HCD’s May 9, 2023 finding that the element meets statutory requirements. However,
         the element could not be found in substantial compliance until the City had adopted the
         draft amendments and completed all necessary rezones, including additional sites and
         the Fairview Developmental Center Specific Plan (FDCSP), to meet all statutory
         requirements and make zoning available to accommodate the RHNA. The Zoning
         Documents are intended to meet the statutory rezoning requirements and do meet most
         requirements, particularly for additional sites. However, residential capacity made
         available in the FDCSP does not meet all statutory requirements pursuant to
         Government Code section 65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i). Specifically, the FDCSP
         must address:

              •   By-right (Without discretionary review): Pursuant to Government Code section
                  65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i), sites rezoned to accommodate the lower-
                  income RHNA must permit owner occupied and rental multifamily residential

Once the City has amended the FDCSP or revised its zoning in some other way to
address the above requirements, the City should submit all pertinent documentation for
approval. HCD will review the documentation and issue correspondence identifying the
updated status of the City’s housing element compliance.

Please be aware, the California appellate decision in Martinez v. City of Clovis held that
when a local government employs overlay zoning to accommodate its lower-income
RHNA, both the base zone and the overlay zone must comply with the minimum density
requirements of Government Code section 65583.2, subdivision (h). Furthermore, New
Commune DTLA LLC v. City of Redondo Beach held that residential overlay zones
cannot satisfy mandatory minimum density and residential use requirements of
subdivision (h) when the underlying zoning allows for 100 percent nonresidential
development on sites rezoned to accommodate the shortfall of lower-income RHNA.
HCD reminds the City to continue compliance with these decisions and Government
Code section 65583.2, subdivision (h) as part of the City’s rezone strategy and
implementation. (See Martinez v. City of Clovis 90 Cal.App.5th 193, 307 Cal.Rptr.3d 64
(2023); New Commune DTLA LLC v. City of Redondo Beach 115 Cal.App.5th 111, 337
Cal. Rptr. 3d 782 (2025).)

Please be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD authority
to review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
inconsistent with an adopted housing element or Housing Element Law. HCD may
revoke housing element compliance if the local government’s actions or lack of actions
do not comply with state law.

Further, while the City has adopted zoning changes related to constraints on housing for
persons with disabilities, the City must continue to modify these zoning changes and
address identified constraints. Specifically, the element indicates Programs 2N
(Reasonable Accommodation), 2O (Definition of Single House Keeping Unit), and 2P
(Group Homes) have been completed, but upon review of the zoning amendments,
HCD finds the zoning amendments continue to contain several constraints on housing
for persons with disabilities. Please see HCD’s previous technical assistance for
additional information and consider the recent California Civil Rights Department (CRD)
settlement agreement. While HCD recognizes the City’s efforts to pursue rezones and
accommodate the RHNA, HCD also urges the City to expeditiously move forward and
revise zoning to meet the intent and objectives of Programs 2N, 2O, and 2P, including
engaging with commenters on this housing element review, stakeholders, HCD and
CRD. Failure to make expeditious progress will trigger a review of the housing element
that may result in revocation of substantial compliance

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City would meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.
