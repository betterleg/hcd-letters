         RE: Vista’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element
         Thank you for submitting the City of Vista’s (City) housing element adopted June 22, 2021
         and received for review on July 13, 2021. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585,
         subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing and Community Development
         (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.
         The adopted housing element addresses most statutory requirements described in
         HCD’s June 4, 2021 letter; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully comply
         with State Housing Element Law. The revisions needed are as follows:
         Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
         Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
         the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A).)
         Sites Inventory: The element provides general information regarding identified sites and
         affirmatively furthering fair housing (AFFH) but must provide more analysis to meet this
         requirement. For instance, while the element analyzes the sites inventory in terms of the
         number of sites, it must address all components of the assessment of fair housing (e.g.,
         segregation and integration, access to opportunity) by the number of units for all income
         groups, analyze the magnitude of impact on existing households by incomne and
         determine whether sites exacerbate or improve segregation and integration at a
         neighborhood.
         Goals and Actions: The element must craft programs responding to the identified
         contributing factors to fair housing issues within the jurisdiction. The element identifies
         large farmworker population and limiting land use policies as contributing factors to fair
         housing issues, but it must include specific and meaningful action responding to these
         contributing factors.
         Additionally, the following programs must be modified with specific commitment,
         milestones and metrics:

•   Program 3 (Residential Sites Inventory): The Program facilitates revitalization in the
    DVSP and Townsite Specific Plan but should specifically commit to how revitalization
    will occur, the timing of these commitments and metrics that target meaningful change
    in the planning period.
•   Program 4 (Residential Sites Monitoring): The Program monitors available residential
    sites, but it should also commit to how sites will be monitored to AFFH, including
    milestones and metrics as appropriate to target meaningful change in the planning
    period.
•   Program 5 (Affordable Housing Development): This Program must be modified with
    actions that demonstrate how the Program AFFH, including milestones and metrics to
    target meaningful change in the planning period. For example, the program could
    include actions to pursue affordable development in lower and higher resource areas
    along with milestones and metrics to target meaningful change in the planning period.
•   Program 10 (Safe and Healthy Housing Program): The Program should include specific
    commitment to AFFH and address contributing factors to fair housing issues, including
    milestones and metrics to target meaningful change in the planning period.
•   Program 21 (Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing): This program provides an overview
    of actions the City will take to AFFH. However, some actions must include milestones
    and metrics to target meaningful change in the planning period. For example, bullet
    three states the City will encourage mixed-income developments accessible to lower-
    income residents citywide but provides minimal detail on how the City plans to achieve
    this objective. Additional bullets to be revised include bullets 1, 2, 3 and 7.
•   Program 23 (Equitable Employment Program): The program should commit to specific
    commitment on how it will AFFH including milestones and metrics to target meaningful
    change in the planning period.
•   Program 24 (Community Placemaking Pilot Program): This Program states small-scale
    placemaking strategies but it should also commit to specific commitment on how it will
    AFFH including milestones and metrics to target meaningful change in the planning
    period.
•   Program 25 (Fair Housing Services): This program states that outreach efforts will be
    conducted but must be more specific as to where it will conduct outreach, the
    implementation timeline for outreach materials, frequency of meetings and provide
    relevant metrics to quantify the targeted impact of this program.
•   Program 26 (Segregation in Housing Implementation): In order to have meaningful
    impact during the planning period, this Program must include actions beyond
    monitoring. These actions must be described in-depth, have an implementation timeline,
    and must provide metrics for the anticipated impact of the program.
•   Program 27 (Fair Housing Program): The Program should generally be revised with
    metrics where appropriate. In addition, for housing mobility strategies, bullets 2 and 3
    should commit to when policies and programs will be revised. For place-based
    strategies to revitalize communities, bullets 1 and 3 should commit to when actions will
    be taken. For displacement risk, bullets 1 and 6 should commit to when actions will be
    taken.

The element will comply once the element has been revised and re-adopted to address
the above findings.
The City’s statutory deadline to adopt a housing element was April 15, 2021. For your
information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if a local
government fails to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of this statutory
deadline, then any rezoning to accommodate the regional housing needs allocation,
including for lower-income households, shall be completed no later than one year from
the statutory deadline. Otherwise, the local government’s housing element will no longer
comply with State Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial
compliance pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i).
For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
http://opr.ca.gov/docs/OPR_Appendix_C_final.pdf and
http://opr.ca.gov/docs/Final_6.26.15.pdf.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

HCD appreciates the hard work and dedication that the City’s housing element team

65400. If HCD can provide assistance in implementing the housing element, please
