         RE: City of Eureka’s 6th Cycle (2019-2027) Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Eureka’s draft Housing Element received for
         review on July 17, 2019, along with revisions received on August 28, 2019. Pursuant
         to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (b), the California Department of
         Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.
         The review was facilitated by a conversation with Brian Heaton, Senior Planner, on
         August 14, 2019. In addition, HCD considered comments from Legal Services of
         Northern California pursuant to Gov. Code Section 65585, subd. (c).

         HCD commends Eureka for creatively designing new approaches to meet the
         housing needs of its community. The element relies on new strategies and small,
         affordable-by-design units to accommodate its regional housing needs allocation
         (RHNA). However, the demand and production of these units is not established in
         Eureka. Further analysis is required to support the assumptions made in the Housing
         Element regarding levels of production and affordability for all five strategies. In
         addition, the city relies upon the production of small housing units to accommodate
         its RHNA. However, small units alone are not sufficient to accommodate the needs
         of families and large households.

         The draft Housing Element addresses many statutory requirements. However,
         revisions will be necessary to comply with state Housing Element law (Article 10.6 of
         the Government Code). The enclosed Appendix describes the revisions needed to
         comply with state Housing Element law.

         Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the Housing
         Element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the Housing Element
         process, the city should continue to engage the community, including organizations that
         represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
         available and considering and incorporating comments where appropriate.

To remain on an eight-year planning cycle, Eureka must adopt its Housing Element
within 120 calendar days from the statutory due date of August 31, 2019 for Humboldt
County Association of Governments’ localities. If adopted after this date, Gov. Code
section 65588, subd. (e)(4) requires the Housing Element be revised every four years
until adopting at least two consecutive revisions by the statutory deadline. For more
information on Housing Element adoption requirements, please visit HCD’s website at:
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-
memos/docs/sb375_final100413.pdf

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider Housing Element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities program; and the SB 2 Planning
grant as well as ongoing SB 2 funding consider Housing Element compliance and/or
annual reporting requirements pursuant to Gov. Code section 65400. With a compliant
Housing Element, Eureka meets Housing Element requirements for these funding
sources.

The following changes would bring Eureka’s Housing Element into compliance with Article 10.6
of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite the supporting
section of the Government Code.

Housing Element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-memos.shtml.
Among other resources, the Housing Element section contains HCD’s latest technical assistance
tool, Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks), available at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/index.shtml and includes the
Government Code addressing state Housing Element law and other resources.

A.    Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

      1. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including
         vacant sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for redevelopment
         during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for a designated
         income level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and public facilities and
         services to these sites. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

         The city has a regional housing need allocation (RHNA) of 952 housing units, of which
         378 are for lower-income households. To address this need, the element relies on
         vacant and underutilized sites, including mixed-use sites, accessory dwelling units
         (ADUs), internal conversions of existing structure, small-lot subdivisions and
         affordable-by-design incentives and local density bonuses. To demonstrate the
         adequacy of these sites and strategies to accommodate the city’s RHNA, the element
         must include complete analyses:

         Strategy 1: Maximize Development Potential of the Few Remaining Vacant and
         Underutilized Sites: The element includes Table 54 (Vacant Sites) and Table 55 (Non-
         Vacant Sites). Additional analysis and incentives are required in order to support the
         suitability of these sites.

         The city recently updated its General Plan and implemented zoning to allow increased
         densities. The element should provide analysis establishing the demand and
         feasibility of development at the increased densities to support the realistic capacity
         estimated the sites identified in Tables 54 and 55.

         Sites #1, #2, #3, and #4 on Table 54 appear to have potential to accommodate lower-
         income RHNA, however, have been identified to accommodate above moderate
         RHNA. Given the city’s reliance on new strategies, these sites could be incentivized to
         accommodate lower-income units.

         Site #5 on Table 54 appears to be an approved project. The element should describe
         if affordability is established for the project through deed restriction or anticipated
         rents.
      Due to the city’s limited reliance on sites to accommodate its lower-income RHNA,
      Site #1 on Table 55 should be incentivized through program actions.

      Sites #2, #3, #4, and #5 on Table 55 are all less than ½ acre in size. Sites smaller
      than an half acre in size are deemed inadequate to accommodate housing for
      lower-income housing unless it is demonstrated that sites of equivalent size were
      successfully developed during the prior planning period for an equivalent number of
      lower-income housing units as projected for the site or unless the Housing Element
      describes other evidence to HCD that the site is adequate to accommodate lower
      income housing (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd. (c)(2)(A).).

      While the sites are city-owned and the element includes program actions to offer them
      for sale/lease through the Request for Proposals (RFP) process to an affordable
      housing developer, these actions are insufficient to establish development will occur
      on these sites at the capacities and affordability proposed. Absent additional analysis,
      the element should remove the sites from the inventory, or consider them appropriate
      to accommodate only moderate or above-moderate RHNA.

      Small Lots: As the element relies on consolidated small sites to accommodate the
      RHNA, it should include a program(s) to facilitate lot consolidation and development
      of housing on small sites. For example, the program could commit to (1) granting
      density bonuses above state density bonus law (Gov. Code, § 65915.); (2) deferring
      fees specifically for consolidation; (3) expediting permit processing; (4) identifying and
      targeting specific financial resources; and, (5) modifying development standards. For
      additional information, see the Building Blocks’ at http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-
      development/building-blocks/program-requirements/address-remove-mitigate-
      constraints.shtml.

      Previously Identified Nonvacant and Vacant Sites: If nonvacant sites identified in a
      prior adopted Housing Element or vacant sites identified in two or more consecutive
      planning periods, the sites are inadequate to accommodate housing for lower-income
      households unless:

      •   The site’s current zoning is appropriate for the development of housing
          affordable to lower-income households by either including analysis or meeting
          the appropriate density. See Gov. Code section 65583.2, subdivision (c)(3), and

      •   The site is subject to a Housing Element program that requires rezoning within
          three years of the beginning of the planning period to allow residential use by right
          for housing developments in which at least 20 percent of the units are affordable
          to lower-income households (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd. (c).).

      Strategy 2: Accessory Dwelling Units: The element relies on sites for ADUs to
      accommodate a significant portion of adequate sites for lower-income households.
      Additional analysis and incentives are required in order to support the suitability of
      this strategy.

      The element must include additional analysis to support the projected production of
      ADUs in the planning period. Current analysis is based upon potential production
      based upon the number of electric meters on lots in the city. This analysis does not
      take into consideration the size and location of existing development on lots.

      In addition, affordability assumptions require additional analysis. While the analysis
      utilizes price per square foot, it should account for newer versus older rental stock.

      While the element notes ADU interest has strongly increased (from approximately 3
      units per year to 8 units per year), the element projects production at 36 ADU’s per
      year and does not appear to include sufficient strategies to achieve this aggressive
      assumption. As a result, a more conservative assumption (e.g. 16 ADUs per year)
      should be utilized. Also, additional incentives should be offered to accommodate the
      increased production volume estimated. For additional information, see the Building
      Blocks at http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/site-
      inventory-analysis/second-units.shtml.

      Strategy 3: Internal Conversions: The element relies on internal conversion/modification
      of the interiors of existing structures to create new residential structures. Additional
      analysis and incentives are required in order to support the suitability of this strategy.

      The element must include additional analysis to support the projected production of
      internal conversions in the planning period. Current analysis is based upon
      potential production based upon lot coverage and the number of electric meters on
      lots in the city. The analysis also relies on the “basic principle of real estate
      economics is well-understood by property owners” that converting existing space is
      less expensive than new construction. This analysis does not take into
      consideration the size and location of existing development on lots. To support this
      assumption, the element should include an inventory and analysis of specific sites
      with potential for internal conversion.

      In addition, affordability assumptions require additional analysis. The analysis could
      consider a community survey of existing new internal conversions for their rental
      rates and include other factors such as square footage, number of bedrooms,
      amenities, age of the new conversion, and general location.

      While the element notes ADU interest has strongly increased (from approximately
      3 units per year to 8 units per year), the element projects production of internal
      conversions as 40 units per year and does not appear to include sufficient strategies
      to achieve this aggressive assumption. As a result, a more conservative assumption
      should be utilized. Also, additional incentives should be offered to accommodate the
      volume of production estimated. For additional information, see the Building Blocks
      at http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/site-inventory-
      analysis/second-units.shtml.

      Strategy 4: Small Lot Subdivisions and Conservation Subdivisions: The element
      utilizes potential capacity for small lot subdivisions toward a portion of the city’s
      RHNA. Additional analysis and incentives are required in order to support the
      suitability of this strategy.

      The element must include additional analysis to support the projected production of
      small lot and conservation subdivisions in the planning period. Current analysis is
      based upon potential production due the number and size of lots in the city. The
      analysis assumes the configuration of improvements and the topography of each lot
      allows for subdivision. The element should include an inventory and analysis of
      specific sites with potential for subdivision. Realistic capacity assumptions should be
      based on density and potential.

      Strategy 5: Geographically-Dispersed Affordable Housing through Affordable-by-
      Design Incentives and Local Density Bonuses: Additional analysis and incentives
      are required in order to support the suitability of this strategy.

      The element includes some description and programs to encourage and facilitate
      the success of Strategies #1 thru #4. The effectiveness of the incentives and
      strategies will be monitored through Program IMP H-35: Implementation of Program
      Monitoring. Action three commits to conducting a mid-point evaluation of the
      effectiveness of the programs and amendments are to be made based upon the
      evaluation of the results. Action three should be revised to provide specific actions
      and timelines, including the rezoning of sites pursuant to Gov. Code section
      65583.2, subd. (h) and (i) to accommodate all remaining RHNA for lower-income
      households within five years of the beginning of the planning period.

      The element should include analysis establishing sites, strategies, and incentives
      will accommodate housing for all segments of the community, including families and
      large families. Small units alone are not sufficient to accommodate the needs of all
      households in Eureka. Families and large families require larger units to mitigate
      overcrowding. The element should describe how larger units will be accommodated
      for all income levels, including very-low, low-, and moderate-income households.

      Two percent of the city lies within the Coastal Zone. The element includes program
      actions to update the City’s Local Coastal Plan and estimates completion in 2022.
      However, the city’s new strategies, including increased densities, are not available
      within the Coastal Zone until a Local Coastal Plan Amendment is processed. The
      element should analyze the amount of reliance it has on areas lying within the
      coastal zone. The analysis could include a description of uses within the area and
      quantify the results of each strategy anticipated in the coastal zone.

B.    Housing Programs

      1.   Include a program which sets forth a schedule of actions during the planning period,
           each with a timeline for implementation, which may recognize that certain programs
           are ongoing, such that there will be beneficial impacts of the programs within the
           planning period, that the local government is undertaking or intends to undertake to
           implement the policies and achieve the goals and objectives of the Housing Element
           through the administration of land-use and development controls, the provision of
           regulatory concessions and incentives, and the utilization of appropriate federal and
           state financing and subsidy programs when available. The program shall include an
           identification of the agencies and officials responsible for the implementation of the
           various actions. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c).)

           and to facilitate implementation, programs should include: (1) a description of the
           city’s specific role in implementation; (2) definitive implementation timelines; (3)
           objectives, quantified where appropriate; and (4) identification of responsible
           agencies and officials. Programs to be revised include the following:

           completed.

           Program H-5: Similar to Program H-2, Program H-5 should include a provision to

E.    Public Participation

      Local governments shall make a diligent effort to achieve public participation of all economic
      segments of the community in the development of the Housing Element, and the element
      shall describe this effort. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.(c)(8).)

      While the element includes a general summary of the public participation process (pages
      5 and 6), including conducting a workshop on April 10, 2019, it must also demonstrate
      diligent efforts were made to involve all economic segments of the community in the
      development of the Housing Element. The element could describe the efforts to circulate
      the Housing Element among low- and moderate-income households and organizations
      that represent them and to involve such groups and persons in the element throughout
      the process. In addition, the element could also summarize the public comment and
      describe how comments were considered and incorporated into the element. For
      additional information, see the Building Blocks at http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-
      development/building-blocks/getting-started/public-participation.shtml.
