         RE: Town of Atherton’s 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the Town of Atherton’s (Town) housing element that was
         adopted October 16, 2024 and received for review on November 4, 2024. Pursuant to
         Government Code section 65585, the California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. In addition, HCD considered
         comments from Vickie Peng, Amir Motamedi, Jeff Bui, Zhanglin, Liang Li, Housing
         Leadership Council of San Mateo County, John E. Riddle, Jane Garratt and
         Carolyn Ordonez pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (c).

         The adopted housing element addresses most statutory requirements described in
         HCD’s April 4, 2023 review; however, additional revisions are necessary to substantially
         comply with State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq), as follows:

              An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including
              vacant sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for redevelopment
              during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for a designated
              income level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and public facilities and
              services to these sites. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

              Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period
              with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities
              to accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing
              need for each income level that could not be accommodated on sites identified in the
              inventory completed pursuant to paragraph (3) of subdivision (a) without
              rezoning…(Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

              Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: While the element now discusses some recent
              development trends, it must still demonstrate the redevelopment potential of
              nonvacant sites, as follows:

       •   Menlo and Sacred Heart Schools: The element now discusses recent
           development trends on school sites in the region. However, the element
           should also discuss the circumstances and site conditions on the Menlo and
           Sacred heart School sites and how those circumstances and site conditions
           demonstrate the potential for redevelopment. For example, similar to
           development trends, the element should explain the available areas of the
           school sites that could be developed, potential site planning and any existing
           conditions that preclude additional development.

       •   Development Trends and Redevelopment Indicators: The element utilizes
           improvement to land value ratio, age of structure and existing floor area ratio
           (FAR) as redevelopment indicators but should also support the validity of
           these indicators utilizing development trends. For example, the element could
           list developments in Table HE-17 by these redevelopment indicators.

   Electronic Sites Inventory: Pursuant to Government Code section 65583.3,
   subdivision (b), the Town must utilize standards, forms, and definitions adopted by
   HCD when preparing the sites inventory and submit an electronic version of the sites
   inventory. While the Town has submitted an electronic version of the sites inventory,
   if changes occur, any future re-adopted versions of the element must also submit the
   electronic version of the sites inventory.

Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete site analysis.
Depending upon the results of that analysis, the Town may need to add or revise
programs. In addition, the element and recent rezoning should be revised, as follows:

   •   Programs 3.811 (New Multifamily Development) and 3.813 (CEQA on Multifamily
       Development): While the Town has recent adopted land use and zoning changes
       (Zoning Changes) to facilitate multifamily development and accommodate the
       regional housing need allocation (RHNA), these Zoning Changes do not appear
       to meet all by right requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65583.2,
       subdivisions (h) and (i). For example, the Zoning Changes do not appear to
       permit housing developments with 20 percent affordability to lower-income
       households without discretionary action.

       In addition, please be aware, the recent California appellate decision in Martinez
       v. City of Clovis found that while overlays can be used in a rezone, when the
       base zone allows residential development, both the base zone and the overlay
       zone must comply with the minimum density requirements of Government Code
       section 65583.2, subdivision (h). The Town should analyze how this decision
       may impact its current rezone strategy using overlays and may need to adjust
       accordingly. Martinez v. City of Clovis (2023) 90 Cal.App.5th 193, 307
       Cal.Rptr.3d 64.

       The Town should either provide documentation to demonstrate compliance with
       all the by right requirements, as noted above, or modify the Zoning Changes and
       submit the appropriate ordinances, resolutions and pertinent documents to HCD
       for review.

       For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of
       2021), as the Town did not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of
       the statutory deadline (January 31, 2023), programs to address a shortfall of
       capacity to accommodate the regional housing needs allocation (RHNA) must be
       completed no later than one year from the statutory deadline, pursuant to
       Government Code sections 65583, subdivision (c)(1)(A). As this year has passed
       and all necessary rezonings have not been completed, the housing element is
       out of compliance and will remain out of compliance until the rezoning has been
       completed to meet all requirements.

   •   Program 3.811(B) (New Single Family Housing Development from SB9
       Implementation): While the Program commits to evaluate constraints on SB 9 lot
       splits, it should also commit to evaluate development trends and consistency with
       assumptions in the sites inventory (48 units) and, if necessary, identify alternative
       sites or strategies by a specified date.

The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once
rezoning has been completed to meet all requirements and the element has been
revised, re-adopted (if needed), submitted to, and reviewed by HCD to comply with the
above requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

As a reminder, the Town’s 6th cycle housing element was due January 31, 2023. As of
today, the Town has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
Town’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the Town to revise the element as described above, adopt if needed, and
submit to HCD to regain housing element compliance.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Affordable Housing and Sustainable
Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing Allocation consider
housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing element, the Town will
meet housing element requirements for these and other funding sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the Town to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.
