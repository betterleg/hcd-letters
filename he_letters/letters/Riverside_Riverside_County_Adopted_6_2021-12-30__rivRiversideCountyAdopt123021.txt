          Thank you for submitting the County of Riverside’s (County) housing element adopted
          September 28, 2021 and received for review on October 1, 2021. Pursuant to
          Government Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of
          Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. In
          addition, HCD considered comments from the Leadership Council of Justice and
          Accountability, San Gorgonio Chapter of the Sierra Club, CA YIMBY Law and
          Residents of Eastern Coachella Valley communities Government Code section 65585,
          subdivision (c). In addition, HCD continued to consider comments on the prior draft
          since many comments do not appear to be addressed or incorporated. Those
          commenters include Public Interest Law Project, California Rural Legal Assistance,
          California Institute for Rural Studies, Consejo de Federaciones Mexicanas, and Pueble
          Unido Community Development Corporation.

          The adopted housing element addresses most statutory requirements described in
          HCD’s September 1, 2021 letter; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully

          Appendix describes the revisions needed to comply with State Housing Element Law.

          submit to HCD to regain housing element compliance.

                                       APPENDIX
                                   COUNTY OF RIVERSIDE

The following changes are necessary to bring the County’s housing element into compliance with
Article 10.6 of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite the
supporting section of the Government Code.

Housing element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-memos.shtml.
Among other resources, the housing element section contains HCD’s latest technical assistance
tool, Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks), available at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/index.shtml and includes the
Government Code addressing State Housing Element Law and other resources.


A. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

   1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
      Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
      the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A).)

      Racially/Ethnically Concentrated Areas of Poverty (R/ECAP): The element now includes
      some information on trends and housing types related to R/ECAPs in the County. The
      element further concludes to address discrepancies in housing types and conditions and
      access to resources, the County has added a program to target investment in
      rehabilitation and various community infrastructure to combat segregation and poverty.
      However, that program (H-14) appears significantly limited when considering the needs
      discussed in the element. Further, the element still does not complete an analysis of
      R/ECAPs which is particularly important given disparities in access to opportunity and
      equitable quality of life. The element must include analysis beyond reporting poverty
      trends and specifically examine the needs of the communities. The analysis should
      address the characteristics of the communities, comparisons to other neighborhoods in
      terms of quality of life and consider other relevant factors and local data and knowledge
      (see discussion below). Programs must be added or modified in stride with the magnitude
      of the needs identified in the analysis to encourage and facilitate an equitable quality of
      life.

      Disparities in Access to Opportunity: The element now lists some policies from other
      elements of the general plan, but it must still analyze disparities in access to opportunity
      and analyze the effectiveness of policies and other practices and investments related to
      environmentally neighborhoods. For example, the analysis should address whether these
      policies and other efforts have been successful and what actions may be needed to
      promote an equitable quality of life. Please see HCD’s prior review and consider the many
      meaningful policy suggestions in current and prior public comments.

      Disproportionate Housing Needs, including Displacement: The element includes some
      information on overcrowding, homelessness and displacement risk. However, it must still

      analyze protected characteristics of persons experiencing homelessness and patterns
      within eastern and western Riverside County and address potential displacement patterns
      and types of displacement. In addition, the element did not address findings related to
      poor housing conditions and farmworkers. Please see HCD’s prior review.

      Local Data and Knowledge and Other Relevant Factors: The element now includes a
      history of development patterns and mentions comments from stakeholder regarding fair

      remarkable information such as community input, findings, information on other relevant
      planning documents and policy suggestions related to housing. Also, HCD received public
      comments with many thoughtful policy suggestions. The element should incorporate this
      other relevant information and add or modify programs with metrics and milestones.

      Contributing Factors to Fair Housing Issues: While the element was revised to prioritize
      contributing factors, these factors and priorities should be re-assessed based on a
      complete assessment of fair housing.

      Goals and Actions, including Metrics and Milestones: As noted in the prior review, the
      element must add or modify goals and actions based on the outcomes of a complete
      analysis. These actions must be significant, meaningful and include metrics and
      milestones that are responsive to a compete analysis. Please see HCD’s prior review for
      additional information.

   2. Include an analysis and documentation of household characteristics, including level of
      payment compared to ability to pay, housing characteristics, including overcrowding, and
      housing stock condition. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(2).)

       Housing Conditions: While the element includes a statement about senior housing
       being replaced, as noted in the prior review, it must analyze the condition of the
       existing housing stock and estimate the total number of units in need of rehabilitation
       and replacement. This analysis should consider differences between eastern and
       western County.

       Manufactured Housing: The element was not revised to address this requirement.
       Please see HCD’s prior review.

       Vacancy Rate: While the element now further reports vacancy rates between easter
       and western County, as stated in the previous letter, it should analyze this atypical
       vacancy rate and add or modify policies and programs as appropriate.

   3. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including vacant
      sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for redevelopment during the
      planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for a designated income level, and an
      analysis of the relationship of zoning and public facilities and services to these sites. (Gov.
      Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

      Specific Plans and Progress toward the Regional Housing Need Allocation (RHNA): As
      noted in the prior review, the element must demonstrate the availability and affordability of
      housing potential in specific plans. The element must still be revised (please see HCD’s
      prior review for additional information), as follows:

          •   Availability of Specific Plans: The element now adjusts specific plans by 70
              percent to account for planning horizons extending beyond the 8-year planning
              period of the housing element. However, the element should include discussion
              to support this assumption. For example, the element could discuss the planning
              horizons and anticipated build outs of larger specific plans (e.g., Travertine
              Specific Plan) to support this assumption.
          •   Specific Plans and Affordability: The element should clearly state whether
              specific plans are being utilized as pending projects or identified sites and make
              appropriate revisions as described in the prior review. For example, if utilizing the
              Valente Specific Plan as an identified site, the element should list the site(s) by
              various statutory factors and include acreage information on sub-areas by
              intensity such as the six anticipated Very High-Density Residential Areas.
          •   Valente and Travertine Specific Plans: The element now rescales pending
              affordability assumptions between lower and moderate-income households. For
              your information, these assumptions must be utilized for the purposes of
              complying with No Net Loss Law pursuant to Government Code section 65863.

      Manufactured Homes: While the element now rescales the anticipated number of
      manufactured homes based on additional records, regarding affordability, it should also
      incorporate land, transportation and financing costs and rescale affordability assumptions
      as appropriate.


B. Housing Programs

   1. Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period with
      appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities to
      accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing need for
      each income level that could not be accommodated on sites identified in the inventory
      completed pursuant to paragraph (3) of subdivision (a) without rezoning, and to comply
      with the requirements of Government Code section 65584.09. Sites shall be identified as
      needed to facilitate and encourage the development of a variety of types of housing for all

      income levels, including multifamily rental housing, factory-built housing, mobilehomes,
      housing for agricultural employees, supportive housing, single-room occupancy units,
      emergency shelters, and transitional housing. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

      As noted in Finding A2, the element does not include a complete site analysis, therefore,

   2. The Housing Element shall contain programs which assist in the development of adequate
      housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and moderate-income
      households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

      While the Program H-18 (Farmworker Assistance) now commits to complete the survey
      before the end of the planning period, it must still be revised to incorporate lessons
      learned from the prior planning period, timing of steps upon completion of the survey and
      should consider discrete timing earlier in the planning period (e.g., within 2 years) and
      consider public comments as appropriate.

   3. Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
      nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and development of
      housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for persons with disabilities.
      The program shall remove constraints to, and provide reasonable accommodations for
      housing designed for, intended for occupancy by, or with supportive services for, persons
      with disabilities. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

      While Program H-7 (Land Use Amendments) now includes clear commitment to revise the
      reasonable accommodation ordinance, it must still commit to revise zoning as appropriate
      to promote certainty and objective procedures regardless of state licensing requirements
      and should not be limited to the specified health and safety codes provisions for group
      homes for seven or more persons.

   4. Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
      throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race, religion, sex,

      characteristics protected by the California Fair Employment and Housing Act (Part 2.8
      (commencing with Section 12900) of Division 3 of Title 2), Section 65008, and any other
      state and federal fair housing and planning law. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

      As noted in Finding A1, the element must include a complete assessment of fair housing.
      Based on the outcomes of that analysis, the element must add or modify programs. For
      additional guidance on program requirements to affirmatively furthering fair housing
      (AFFH), please see HCD’s guidance at https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-

   5. The housing program shall preserve for low-income household the assisted housing
      developments identified pursuant to paragraph (9) of subdivision (a). The program for
      preservation of the assisted housing developments shall utilize, to the extent necessary,
      all available federal, state, and local financing and subsidy programs identified in
      paragraph (9) of subdivision (a), except where a community has other urgent needs for
      which alternative funding sources are not available. The program may include strategies
      that involve local regulation and technical assistance. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(6).)

      HCD’s prior review noted Program H-23 (Monitor At-risk) should be strengthened by
      committing to add funding assistance or assist with funding applications. In response,
      the Program now commits to assist with completing application; however, to have a
      beneficial outcome in the planning period, the Program should clearly commit to support
      applications as part of scoring criteria and consider funding if available.

   6. Develop a plan that incentivizes and promotes the creation of accessory dwelling units that
      can be offered at affordable rent, as defined in Section 50053 of the Health and Safety
      Code, for very low, low-, or moderate-income households. For purposes of this paragraph,
      “accessory dwelling units” has the same meaning as “accessory dwelling unit” as defined
      in paragraph (4) of subdivision (i) of Section 65852.2. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(7).)

      HCD’s prior review noted Program H-9 should go beyond “exploring” options for
      establishing a loan program and commit to its implementation. In response, the Program
      was revised to “assess the feasibility” of establishing a loan program, essentially the same
      lack of commitment to a tangible outcome in the planning period. The Program should be
      revised beyond “exploring” or “assess the feasibility” and include a clear commitment to
      establish a loan program or clear commitment to establish other incentives to promote the
      affordability of accessory dwelling units (ADU). In addition, the Program commits to
      actions if ADU production and affordability assumptions are not met, but it should also
      commit to when those actions will be complete.


C. Public Participation

   Local governments shall make a diligent effort to achieve public participation of all economic
   segments of the community in the development of the Housing Element, and the element
   shall describe this effort. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(8).)

   As noted in the prior review, providing the opportunity for public review is essential to
   complying with statutory requirements to make a diligent effort to encourage the public
   participation in the development of the element. HCD received several comments that the
   public was not provided sufficient time or outreach wasn’t sufficient to gather comments. HCD


Riverside County’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element                  Page 5
December 30, 2021
   also observes that past comments included meaningful policy and program suggestions that
   could be incorporated into the housing and safety elements. Yet, the adopted element does
   not reflect this input. Moving forward, the County should consider additional methods to
   gather input from all segments of the community and must proactively make future revisions
   available to the public, including any commenters, with sufficient time to comment and prior to
   submitting any revisions to HCD and diligently consider and address comments, including
   revising the document where appropriate. The County’s consideration of public comments
   must not be limited by HCD’s findings in this review letter.
