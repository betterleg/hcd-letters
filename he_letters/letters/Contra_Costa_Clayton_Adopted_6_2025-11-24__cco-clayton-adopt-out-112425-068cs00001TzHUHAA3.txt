         RE: Clayton’s 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Clayton’s (City) housing element that was adopted
         August 19, 2025 and received for review on October 2, 2025. Pursuant to Government
         Code section 65585, the California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

         HCD is pleased to find the adopted housing element meets the statutory requirements
         of State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq). The adopted element
         meets the statutory requirements described in HCD’s May 14, 2024 review. However,
         the housing element cannot be found in substantial compliance until the City has
         completed necessary rezones to address the shortfall of sites to accommodate the
         RHNA (See Below). Once the City completes the rezone, a copy of the resolution,
         ordinance or other relevant zoning documentation should be transmitted to HCD. HCD
         will review the documentation and issue correspondence identifying the updated
         compliance status of the City’s housing element.

         Pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), a jurisdiction that did
         not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days from the statutory deadline
         cannot be found in compliance until rezones to make prior identified sites or
         accommodate a shortfall of sites are completed pursuant to Government Code sections
         65583, subdivision (c)(1)(A) and 65583.2, subdivisions (c), (h) and (i). As this year has
         passed and the necessary rezones are not complete (Programs D1 (General Plan
         Amendments) and D2 (Zoning Code Amendments)), the housing element is out of
         compliance and will remain out of compliance until the rezoning have been completed.
         HCD encourages the City to expeditiously complete the rezoning to be found in
         substantial compliance and welcomes the opportunity to provide technical assistance
         through the rezoning process, including review of draft rezoning documents.

For your information, since the element relies on nonvacant sites to accommodate 50
percent or more of the lower-income regional housing needs allocation (RHNA),
requirements are triggered to make findings based on substantial evidence (as part of
an adoption resolution) that the existing use is not an impediment and will likely
discontinue in the planning period pursuant to Government Code section 65583.2,
subdivision (g)(2). For additional information, please see HCD’s Sites Inventory
Guidebook (p. 26) at https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-
element/housing-element-memos/docs/sites_inventory_memo_final06102020.pdf.

Additionally, the City must continue timely and effective implementation of all programs
including but not limited to the following:

   •   Program B1: Accessory Dwelling Units
   •   Program B2: Town Center Mixed Use
   •   Program B3: Affordable Housing Development
   •   Program B4: Housing on Institutional Lands
   •   Program C: Adequate Sites
   •   Program D1: General Plan Amendments
   •   Program D2: Zoning Code Amendments
   •   Program F1: Town Center Specific Plan Amendments
   •   Program H1: Funding Assistance
   •   Program H5: Shared Housing
   •   Program I1: At-risk Preservation
   •   Program J5: Housing Mobility

The City must monitor and report on the results of these and other programs through
the annual progress report, required pursuant to Government Code section 65400.
Please be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD authority
to review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
inconsistent with an adopted housing element or housing element law. This includes
failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke
housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state
law.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Affordable Housing and Sustainable
Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing Allocation consider
housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing element, the City will meet
housing element requirements for these and other funding sources.
