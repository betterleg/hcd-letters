          RE: City of Tustin 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

          Thank you for submitting the Tustin Housing Element housing element adopted
          October 4, 2022 and received for review on October 5, 2022. Pursuant to Government
          Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing and
          Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

          HCD is pleased to find the adopted housing element in full compliance with State Housing
          Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code). The adopted element was found to be
          substantially the same as the revised draft element that HCD’s September 12, 2022, review
          determined met statutory requirements.

          Additionally, the City must continue timely and effective implementation of all programs
          including but not limited to the following:

             •    Program 1.1 (Residential Development – Available Sites) – This program commits to
                  process required zoning amendments to the Tustin Legacy Specific Plan (TLSP),
                  Enderle Center, and Tustin Market Place and update its zoning code to allow by-
                  right residential development pursuant to government code, 65583.2 provisions by
                  October 2024.
             •    Program 1.2 (Objective Design Standards) – The program commits to remove
                  subjective design guidance in TCC 9272 (Design Review) and adopt objective design
                  standards by December 2024.
             •    Program 1.4 (Voluntary Workforce Housing & Specific Plan Reassessments) – This
                  program commits to identify development constraints and adjustments necessary to
                  incentivize housing development and subsequently amend the TCC to remove
                  development as it relates to the City’s current Voluntary Workforce Housing Ordinance
                  and Residential Allocation Reservation (RAR) process by 2025.
Matthew S. West, City Manager

  •   Program 1.5 (Zoning Code Streamlining Program) – This program commits to remove
      the Conditional Use Permit requirement for triplexes in the R2 and R3 zones and for
      residential uses Citywide in all zones by 2023.
  •   Program 1.10 (Development and Permit Streamlining) – This program will track the
      processing time frames for processing of reviews and permits for low- and moderate-
      income housing and adjust internal procedures to shorten review timeframes by June
      2023.
  •   Program 1.19 (Lot Consolidation Overlay Program) – This program commits to adopting
      code amendment to provide lot consolidation development incentives by October 2024.
  •   Program 2.1 (Emergency, Transitional, and Supportive Housing) - The Municipal Code
      will be amended to include definitions of transitional and supportive housing, and
      explicitly permit transitional and supportive housing subject only to those zoning
      regulations that apply to other residential dwellings of the same type in the same zone
      by October 2023.
  •   Program 2.4 (Housing for Persons with Disabilities) – This program will revise its
      reasonable accommodation procedures to, among other things, remove the application
      fee, revise so that the reasonable accommodation can proceed without the need for any
      additional corrections of nonconformities to property, state that a reasonable
      accommodation may be acted upon independently of any other applications, remove
      the annual inspection requirement, and remove the deed restriction recordation by
      December 2023.

The City must monitor and report on the results of this and other programs through the
annual progress report, required pursuant to Government Code section 65400. Please be
aware, Government Code section 65585(i) grants HCD authority to review any action or
failure to act by a local government that it determines is inconsistent with an adopted
housing element or housing element law. This includes failure to implement program
actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke housing element compliance if
the local government’s actions do not comply with state law.

In addition, the element includes Program 1.1 (Residential Development – Available Sites)
which commits to rezone at least 135 acres by October 2024 permitting multifamily uses
without discretionary action, requiring a minimum density of 20 units per acre to address
the identified shortfall of 3,790 units.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City meets housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.
Matthew S. West, City Manager
