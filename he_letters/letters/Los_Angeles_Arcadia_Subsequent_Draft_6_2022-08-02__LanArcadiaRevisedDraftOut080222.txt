         RE: Arcadia’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Arcadia’s (City) revised draft housing element received
         for review on June 3, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (b), the
         California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the
         results of its review. Our review was facilitated by a telephone conversation on July 28, 2022
         with you; Lisa Flores of your staff; and Stephanie Ellsworth and Dave Barquist, your
         consultants.

         The draft element addresses many statutory requirements of the February 25, 2022 review;
         however, revisions will be necessary to comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6
         of the Gov. Code). The enclosed Appendix describes the revisions needed to comply with
         State Housing Element Law.

         For your information, pursuant to Senate Bill 197 (Chapter 70, Statutes of 2022), as the City
         did not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the statutory deadline
         (October 15, 2021), as of this writing, any rezoning must be completed within one year of
         the statutory deadline (October 15, 2022). However, if the City’s adopted element is found in
         compliance by October 15, 2022, the City may maintain its scheduled rezone deadline of
         October 2024 (Programs 5-7, 5-8, 5-9, 5-10, 5-11, 5-12, 5-13, and 5-14). HCD encourages
         the City to adopt and submit its housing element by August 15, 2022 to ensure completion
         of the review of the housing element by the October 15, 2022. If the element is not found in
         compliance by October 15, 2022, HCD cannot find the element in compliance until the
         rezoning is complete

         Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing element
         is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element process, the City
         should continue to engage the community, including organizations that represent lower-

                                          APPENDIX
                                       CITY OF ARCADIA

The following changes are necessary to bring the City’s housing element into compliance with
Article 10.6 of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite the
supporting section of the Government Code.

Housing element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-memos.shtml.
Among other resources, the housing element section contains HCD’s latest technical assistance
tool, Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks), available at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/index.shtml and includes the
Government Code addressing State Housing Element Law and other resources.


A.   Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

     1.   Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
          Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
          the jurisdiction (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A))

          Fair Housing Enforcement and Outreach: As stated in the previous letter, the element
          must describe the degree of outreach to all sectors of the community as well as
          describe any findings, lawsuits, or enforcement actions taken related to fair housing
          within the City. While element was revised to describe outreach, it must describe any
          findings, lawsuits, or enforcement actions taken related to fair housing.

          Racial/Ethnic Concentrated Areas of Affluence (RCAA): As stated in the previous letter,
          the element must analyze RCAAs. While the element was revised to include a local
          analysis of RCAAs, it must also include a regional analysis. The analysis should
          describe the regional perspective, evaluating the patterns and changes over time and
          considering other relevant factors, such as public participation, past policies, practices,
          and investments and demographic trends.

          Goals, Priorities, Metrics, and Milestones: Goals and actions must significantly seek to
          overcome contributing factors to fair housing issues. The element did not address this
          requirement. While the element lists various programs that fall under each category,
          metrics and milestones, as well as geographic targeting, were not added to the
          programs. Given that most of the City is considered a high resource community, the
          element could focus on metrics and milestones that enhance housing mobility and
          encourage development of more housing choices and affordable housing.

     2.   An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including
          vacant sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for redevelopment
          during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for a designated income
          level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and public facilities and services to
          these sites. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

         The City has a regional housing need allocation (RHNA) of 3,214 housing units, of
         which 1,672 are for lower-income households. To address this need, the element relies
         on vacant and nonvacant sites, including sites in Specific Plan Areas and within the
         Mixed-Use Overlay areas. To demonstrate the adequacy of these sites and strategies
         to accommodate the City’s RHNA, the element must include complete analyses:

         Arcadia 3 Golf Course: As stated in the previous letter, the element must address
         current and potential general plan designations, allowable densities, support for
         residential capacity assumptions, existing uses and any known conditions that preclude
         development in the planning period and the potential schedule for development for the
         Arcadia 3 Golf Course. The element should also describe the timing of the sale and
         indicate if the sale will occur within 36 months of housing element adoption or with the
         creation of the specific plan. Although Program 5-14 was revised to commit to drafting
         a Specific Plan that will include the above-named standards, it must be revised to
         include the rezone requirements pursuant to Government Code 65583.2(h) and (i). In
         addition, the timeline should be revised for completion within one year of adoption.
         Lastly, the number of affordable units listed in the program do not match the number
         estimated in the element and should be revised for consistency.

         HCD understands that the City received ten letters of interest for affordable housing on
         this site after releasing a notice of availability pursuant to the State Surplus Land Act.
         However, none of the responding housing entities could meet the City’s price and
         terms. A part of program 5.14, the City must commit to facilitating all necessary steps
         to ensure that affordable housing can and will be built on this site pursuant to the
         capacity of the sites inventory. The element should also include a monitoring program.
         Should the City not achieve what was identified in the inventory additional sites must
         be identified to meet RHNA.

         Realistic Capacity: While the element references a feasibility percentage identified by
         the City through an analysis of development trends in the City and historical
         development used to calculate realistic capacity, it did not fully address the
         requirements necessary to substantiate the 80 percent buildout capacity and support
         the analysis with affordability levels. Please refer to HCD’s previous findings letter for
         additional information.

         As stated in the previous letter, the element must also consider the calculation for sites
         that allow nonresidential uses (e.g., mixed-use) based on the likelihood of
         nonresidential development, performance standards, development trends supporting
         residential development, and any existing or planned policies, programs, or local
         guidance or efforts promoting residential development in nonresidential zones. Please
         refer to HCD’s previous findings letter for additional information.

         Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: As stated in the previous letter, the element must
         provide sufficient evidence to describe the suitability of nonvacant sites for residential
         development. The element describes development trends on nonvacant sites and
         includes feasibility assumptions but did not address the extent existing uses on
         nonvacant sites would impede residential development on those sites. The element
         must clarify how sites in the R-3 were identified and selected. In the inventory, the

         Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU): As stated in the previous letter, the element must
         adjust its projected ADU estimates for planning period. While the element adjusted the
         assumption to 34 ADUs per year, it still does not support this projection given the

         constitute adequate support for increases in ADU projections. The element should be
         updated to either provide further support for the projected number of ADUs and include
         a program committed to monitoring production and affordability every two years,
         increasing incentives and identifying additional sites within six months if the City is not
         meeting its initial assumptions. While Program 17 includes a monitoring component, it
         must commit to monitoring production and affordability every two years, not just the
         first two years of the planning period.

          Local Processing and Permit Procedures: As stated in the previous letter, the element
          must describe its processing and permitting procedures. While the element lists the
          processing time for each permit type, it does not describe the typical processing time

B.   Housing Programs

     1.   Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period
          with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities to
          accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing need
          for each income level that could not be accommodated on sites identified in the
          inventory completed pursuant to paragraph (3) of subdivision (a) without rezoning, and
          to comply with the requirements of Government Code section 65584.09. Sites shall be
          identified as needed to facilitate and encourage the development of a variety of types
          of housing for all income levels, including multifamily rental housing, factory-built
          housing, mobilehomes, housing for agricultural employees, supportive housing, single-
          room occupancy units, emergency shelters, and transitional housing.
          (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

         As noted in Finding A2, the element does not include a complete site analysis,
         therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the results
         of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City may need to add or revise
         programs to address a shortfall of sites or zoning available to encourage a variety of
         housing types. In addition, the element should be revised as follows:

         Rezone Program for Adequate Sites: While the minimum density for each program was
         clarified, programs to rezone (Programs 5-7, 5-8, 5-9, 5-10, 5-11, 5-12 and 5-13) do not
         ensure that requirements set forth in Government Code section 65583.2 (h) will be met
         as follows:
             • Sites identified must demonstrate that they will allow at least 16 units.
             • As more that 50 percent of the shortfall for lower-income is on sites zoned for
                 mixed use, the program must commit to allowing 100 percent residential
                 projects and require residential use occupy 50 percent of the total floor area of a
                 mixed-use project.
             • The programs must also clarify whether all of the zones have a minimum density
                 of 20 units per acre. The element states “assumed to develop affordably at a
                 minimum density of…” but this does not commit to requiring a minimum density.

         Program 5-28 (Affordable Housing for Families and Persons with Special Needs):
         While the program was revised to reference implementing an inclusionary housing
         policy, the element must include specific commitments towards actions to assist
         extremely low-income households.

    2.   Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
         nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and development of
         housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for persons with
         disabilities. The program shall remove constraints to, and provide reasonable
         accommodations for housing designed for, intended for occupancy by, or with
         supportive services for, persons with disabilities. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

         Constraints on Housing for Persons with Disabilities: While Program 3-34 (Mitigating
         Constraints to Housing Choice for Persons with Disabilities) commits to revising the
         definition of family, it must specifically state how it will be revised. In addition, the
         program must clarify the actions regarding residential care facilities for seven or more
         residents. The program must clarify where the City will allow residential care facilities

C.   Public Participation

     Local governments shall make a diligent effort to achieve public participation of all economic
     segments of the community in the development of the Housing Element, and the element
     shall describe this effort. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.(c)(9).)



     The previous letter required that the element be revised to demonstrate diligent efforts to
     involve all economic segments of the community in the development of the housing
     element. While the element was revised to state that outreach was included for persons
     experiencing homelessness, the City must demonstrate diligent efforts to involve all
     economic segments of the community. The City could create a program to ensure that
     they provide outreach to all economic segments of the community throughout the
     planning period.
