         RE: City of Anaheim’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Anaheim’s (City) Ordinance Number 6615 and
         Resolution Number 2025-84 (Zoning Documents). The Zoning Documents were
         adopted on September 9, 2025 and received for review on September 25, 2025.
         Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, the California Department of Housing
         and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

         On July 10, 2025, HCD found the adopted housing element and rezones in substantial
         compliance with State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq). That finding
         was based, among other things, implementation of Programs 1H, 1J, 1K and 1R. While
         the City has completed rezoning related to Programs 1H, 1J, 1K and 1R (Programs),
         HCD instructed the City to complete near-term implementation (within 60 days) actions
         to clarify technical provisions related to (1) non-discretionary review for housing
         developments with 20 percent affordability; (2) requiring minimum densities; (3)
         residential performance standards in non-residential zones; and (4) cross-reference to
         sites used in prior planning period. Upon review of the Zoning Documents, HCD is
         pleased to find the City has completed actions to address the requirements that were
         described in HCD’s July 10, 2025 review. As a result, the adopted housing element
         remains in substantial compliance with State Housing Element Law as of the date of this
         letter.

         Additionally, the City must continue timely and effective implementation of all programs

The City must monitor and report on the results of these and other programs through
the annual progress report, required pursuant to Government Code section 65400.
Please be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD authority
to review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
inconsistent with an adopted housing element or housing element law. This includes
failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke
housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state
law.

Finally, per HCD’s prior letter, the City should closely monitor development standards
such as lot coverage and parking requirements and make adjustments as appropriate to
facilitate achieving maximum densities and address constraints to development.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City continues to meet housing element requirements for these and other
funding sources.
