     RE: Review of San Diego County’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

     Thank you for submitting the County of San Diego’s (County) housing element adopted
     July 14, 2021 and received for review on August 8, 2021. Pursuant to Government Code
     section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing and Community
     Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

     HCD finds the adopted housing element in substantial compliance with State Housing
     Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code). The adopted element addresses the
     statutory requirements described in HCD’s May 11, 2021 review. HCD’s finding was based
     on, among other reasons, several programs and actions that encourage development on
     nonvacant sites, processes that promote clarity and streamline developments, and
     strategies that affirmatively further fair housing (AFFH). HCD specifically notes the
     following:

 •   Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing – New Housing Choices in Areas of High
     Opportunity: The element identified several programs that will encourage and promote
     affordable housing choices in areas of high opportunity including Program 3.6.5.A –
     Removal of Constraints to Multi-Family Housing in Areas of Opportunity, Program 3.6.5.B
     – Enhanced Housing Choices and Affordability in Areas of Opportunity, including, Rancho
     Santa Fe, Program 3.1.1.D – Low to Moderate Inclusionary Ordinance, and Program
     3.1.4.D – Develop an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) Subsidy Program. As the County
     stated on Page 6-40, the County will focus program outreach, development, and
     implementation in areas of high opportunity as identified in the Assessment of Fair
     Housing on Page 6-I-32. The County must implement these programs in a timely manner
     with a focus on high opportunity areas to meet the program requirements for AFFH and
     address disparities in access to areas of high opportunity.

•   Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing – Place-Based Strategies: The element identified a
    series of programs as well as county-wide actions through its Environmental Justice
    Element to implement place-based strategies that conserve and revitalize specific
    communities. These programs include, but are not limited to, Program 3.6.6.B –
    Conserved and Improved Assets in Areas of Lower Opportunity and Concentrated
    Poverty, Program 3.6.6.C – Negative Environmental, Neighborhood, Housing, Health
    Impacts, Strategy, Program 3.6.6.E – Fallbrook Subarea Plan, Program 3.6.6.F – Twin
    Oaks Community Plan Update, Program 3.6.6.G – Placed-Based Strategies in Low-
    Resource Communities and Environmental Justice Communities, As the County has
    stated on Page 6-43, the County must prioritize implementation of these programs in
    communities disproportionally impacted by fair housing issues that have been identified on
    Pages 6-I-38 and 6-I-59.

•   Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA) – Nonvacant Sites: The County is
    accommodating 49 percent of its lower-income RHNA on nonvacant sites. Through
    analysis, the County demonstrated that the existing uses will not impede development.
    However, HCD finds that because the County lacks experience in redeveloping nonvacant
    sites for affordable housing, the inclusion of programs that incentivize and encourage
    redevelopment were essential to receiving compliance. As such, the County must
    implement Program 3.1.1.L – Coordination and Outreach with Developers, Builders, and
    Owners, Program 3.1.3.A – Zoning Ordinance Cleanups, Program 3.1.1.H – Housing
    Yields in Mixed-Use Zones, and Program 3.1.1.B – By-Right Approval for Projects with 20
    Percent Affordable by the timelines indicated in the element.

•   Permit Processes: HCD received several comments throughout the housing element
    development and review process on the County’s permit processes, timeframes, and
    constraints from organizations that develop housing in the unincorporated areas of the
    County. To address constraints to permit processes, approve development applications for
    affordable housing in a timely manner, and provide certainty to the development process,
    the County must implement actions and initiatives starting on page 6-B-34, Program
    3.1.1.K – Expand Eligibility of Checklist Exemptions and Program 3.2.2.A – Affordable
    Housing Projects.

    The County must continue timely and effective implementation of all programs including
    the ones noted above. The County must monitor and report on the results of this and other
    programs through the annual progress report, required pursuant to Government. Code
    section 65400. Please be aware, Government Code section 65585(i) grants HCD authority
    to review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
    inconsistent with an adopted housing element or housing element law. This includes
    failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke
    housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state
    law.

    For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
    element adoption. HCD reminds the County to consider timing provisions and welcomes

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element compliance
as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill (SB) 1 Sustainable
Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s Affordable Housing and
Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing Allocation
consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing element, the County meets
housing element requirements for these and other funding sources.
