          Thank you for submitting the City of Orange’s (City) housing element adopted
          February 8, 2022 and received for review on February 12, 2022. Pursuant to
          Government Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of
          Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. In
          addition, HCD considered comments from Elder Law & Disability Rights Center, the
          Kennedy Commission, and City residents pursuant to Government Code section
          65585, subdivision (c).

          The adopted housing element addresses most statutory requirements described in
          HCD’s September 13, 2021, letter; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully
          comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code), see enclosed
          Appendix.

          For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021),
          as the City failed to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the statutory
          deadline (October 15, 2021), any program requiring rezoning to accommodate the
          regional housing needs allocation (RHNA) must be completed no later than one year
          from the statutory deadline. Otherwise, the local government’s housing element will no
          longer comply with State Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of
          substantial compliance pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i).

          Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
          element is essential to effective housing planning. During the housing element revision
          process, the City must continue to engage the community, including organizations that
          represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information
          regularly available while considering and incorporating comments where appropriate.
          Please be aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local

The following changes are necessary to bring the City’s housing element into compliance with Article
10.6 of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite the supporting
section of the Government Code.

Housing element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-memos.shtml.
Among other resources, the housing element section contains HCD’s latest technical assistance tool,
Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks), available at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/index.shtml and includes the
Government Code addressing State Housing Element Law and other resources.


   A. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

      1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
         Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
         the jurisdiction (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A))

          Enforcement and Outreach: While the element includes key themes from outreach
          efforts, it must still provide data related to the number of lawsuits, enforcement actions,
          settlements, or judgements related to fair housing or civil rights. This data should be
          analyzed for any patterns or trends by community area or census tract and include
          additional local knowledge, relevant factors, and a conclusion of summary of issues. In
          addition, the element states that the City works with the Fair Housing Foundation to
          provide fair housing services. However, the analysis must also describe compliance
          with existing fair housing laws and regulations and include information on fair housing
          outreach capacity.

          Integration and Segregation: While the element discusses its dissimilarity index and
          includes some information on familiar status and persons with disabilities and does not
          analyze levels of segregation and integration for race and ethnicity, income, familial
          status, persons with disabilities for patterns and trends over time. For example, in order
          to the groups that experience the highest levels of segregation, the element could
          provide a more detailed categorization of potential concentrations at the block group,
          census track, or neighborhood level. Additionally, the element must provide regional
          comparison and analyze for patterns and trends over time.

          Racially Concentrated Areas of Affluence (RCAAs): While the element outlines six
          potential RCAAs (block groups) in the City, it must provide a regional comparison and a
          concluding discussion of the data to inform policies, goals, actions, and programs.

          Access to Opportunity: While the element indicates that the city is a mix of low and
          moderate resource to high resource, the analysis and discussion must include overlaps

         in areas that have more amenities such as transportation, schools, parks, and healthy
         food options.

         Disproportionate Housing Need: The discussion on homelessness must be expanded to

         assess changes over time. The analysis does not currently consider the increase in the
         homeless population in the city compared to the region at large. While the element
         mentions two regional shelters the city helps fund through the North SPA, (Buena Park
         Center and Placentia Center), there is no discussion on how many beds are available to
         the City of Orange. The element lists its collaboration with the police department to
         assist the homeless population but does not quantify or describe how this program that
         has been operating since 2013 has helped persons experiencing homelessness obtain
         services or long-term care towards housing.

         section as well that impact a meaningful analysis of sites. For example, the element

         The City has a regional housing need allocation (RHNA) of 3,936 housing units, of
         which 1,671 are for lower-income households. To address this need, the element relies
         on sites that are residentially zoned or in a mixed-use zone that permits residential
         uses. To demonstrate the adequacy of these sites and strategies to accommodate the
         City’s RHNA, the element must include complete analyses:

         Realistic Capacity: While the element was revised to include language from the City’s
         municipal code which states that residential development is allowed in the Urban Mix
         Use (UMU) zone, either as part of a mixed-use project or as a freestanding use, the
         capacity methodology must still account for the likelihood of residential development on
         sites proposed in the UMU zone. For example, the analysis could evaluate the number
         of 100 percent commercial projects proposed in the last planning period in the UMU
         zone and compare to the number of projects with residential.

         Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: Additionally, while the element was revised to detail the
         existing uses on the site, the analysis must still demonstrate the site potential for
         redevelopment. While the element indicates key indicators for consideration of
         appropriateness of sites, it does not support reasons why these indicators where
         selected and how they demonstrate the potential for new residential development on the
         selected sites. In addition, these factors all related to potential market factors but do not
         relate to whether the existing uses on selected sites are likely to impede new residential
         development. For example, the element could analyze and discuss why the charter
         school is likely to redevelop into residential or expand data and discussion on market
         trends regarding the discontinuation of existing uses for big box retail stores like Best
         Buy. The element could also relate the characteristics of the projects listed on Table B-2
         to the characteristics of sites within the inventory. As stated in the previous review, the
         element could also consider indicators such as age and condition of the existing
         structure expressed developer interest, low improvement to land value ratio, and other
         factors. For existing malls, while the element lists regional malls within the County that
         have recently completed planning and entitlement efforts to permit residential uses, the
         element must still clarify what similar characteristics these malls have with the Outlets at
         Orange that make such a comparison meaningful. Lastly, while the City stated it
         removed all UC Regent’s owned cites, the UCI MDA ALS Clinic still remains in the sites
         inventory. If the site remains, the element must indicate whether this site is expected to
         be put on a surplus list or if conversations with the Regents has occurred related to
         potential residential development.

         The housing element relies upon nonvacant sites to accommodate more than 50
         percent of the RHNA for lower-income households, therefore the element must
         demonstrate that the existing uses are not an impediment to additional residential
         development in the planning period. (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd. (g)(2).) Absent
         findings (e.g., adoption resolution) based on substantial evidence, the existing uses will
         be presumed to impede additional residential development and will not be utilized
         toward demonstrating adequate sites to accommodate the regional housing need
         allocation. HCD is not aware that the City has made the appropriate finding. Any future

         Sites with Zoning for a Variety of Housing Types:

         Emergency Shelters: While the element was revised to provide information on
         Section 17.13.040 of the City’s municipal code as it relates to emergency shelters,
         these standards must be thoroughly analyzed for constraints and an assessment
         should be made whether these hinder the development of emergency shelters in the
         M-1 and M-2 zones and for consistency with Government Code section 65583,
         subdivision (a)(4)(A). For example, the requirement that emergency shelters be at
         least 500 feet from any public institutions, not near parks, and must be located within
         half mile of a transit stop are outside the scope of allowable standards relating to
         siting of emergency shelters. The element must include a program to amend the
         zoning ordinance to ensure consistency with state law.

         Accessory Dwelling Units: The element did not address this finding. The element shows
         that accessory dwelling units (ADUs) are conditionally permitted in R-1 through R-5
         zones, but ADUs should be permitted in all zones that allow residential.

      1. Include a program which sets forth a schedule of actions during the planning period,
         each with a timeline for implementation, which may recognize that certain programs are
         ongoing, such that there will be beneficial impacts of the programs within the planning
         period, that the local government is undertaking or intends to undertake to implement
         the policies and achieve the goals and objectives of the Housing Element through the
         administration of land use and development controls, the provision of regulatory
         concessions and incentives, and the utilization of appropriate federal and state financing
         and subsidy programs when available. The program shall include an identification of the

         agencies and officials responsible for the implementation of the various actions. (Gov.
         Code, § 65583, subd. (c).)

         Programs must still be revised to demonstrate that they will have a beneficial impact
         within the planning period. Beneficial impact means specific commitment to
         deliverables, measurable metrics or objectives, definitive deadlines, dates, or
         benchmarks for implementation. All programs must be evaluated to ensure specific
         action steps to implement policies and attain the set objectives and metrics. Programs
         containing unclear language (e.g., “Evaluate”; “Consider”; “Encourage”; etc.) should be
         amended to include more specific and measurable actions and deliverables should
         occur early in the planning period to ensure actual housing outcomes. Please see
         HCD’s prior review dated September 13, 2021 for more information.

      2. Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period with
         appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities to
         accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing need
         for each income level that could not be accommodated on sites identified in the
         inventory completed pursuant to paragraph (3) of subdivision (a) without rezoning, and
         to comply with the requirements of Government Code section 65584.09. Sites shall be
         identified as needed to facilitate and encourage the development of a variety of types of
         housing for all income levels, including multifamily rental housing, factory-built housing,
         mobilehomes, housing for agricultural employees, supportive housing, single-room
         occupancy units, emergency shelters, and transitional housing. (Gov. Code, § 65583,
         subd. (c)(1).)

         As noted in Finding A3, the element does not include a complete site analysis,
         therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the results
         of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City must revise programs to address
         zoning available to encourage a variety of housing types.

         Housing Policy Action 2J and 2K: Development of ADUs: The element is assuming
         production of 40 ADUs per year for a total of 320 units within the planning period. This
         projection is more that the average production for the previous four years of 29 per year.
         Housing Policy Action 2K monitors the production of ADUs throughout the planning
         period, in order to support the increase in production levels, Housing Policy Action 2J
         should include additional resources and incentives to facilitated ADU production. As
         stated in the previous review this can take the form of flexible zoning requirements,
         development standards, or processing and fee incentives that facilitate the creation of
         ADUs, such as reduced parking requirements, fee waivers and more. Other strategies
         could include developing information packets to market ADU construction, targeted
         advertising of ADU development opportunities or establishing an ADU specialist within
         the planning department.
