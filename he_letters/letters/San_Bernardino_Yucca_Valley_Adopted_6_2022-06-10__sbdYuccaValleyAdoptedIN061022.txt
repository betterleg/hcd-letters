         RE: Town of Yucca Valley 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the Town of Yucca Valley (Town) housing element adopted
         April 5, 2022 and received for review on April 12, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code
         section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. Our review was facilitated by a
         conversation on May 24, 2022 with you, the Town’s consultant Amy Sinsheimer,
         Lindsey Klein, and Jennifer Gastelum from PlaceWorks.

         HCD is pleased to find the adopted housing element in full compliance with State
         Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code). The adopted element addresses
         the statutory requirements described in HCD’s December 28, 2021 review. HCD’s
         finding was based on, among other reasons, programs that affirmatively further fair
         housing (AFFH), demonstrate adequate sites to accommodate the Town’s lower-income
         regional housing need allocation (RHNA), and establish appropriate zoning for
         emergency shelters.

         Additionally, the Town must continue timely and effective implementation of all
         programs including but not limited to the following:

             •    Program H1-4: Emergency Shelters Zoning and Development Standards – The
                  Town has recently completed zoning amendments to allow emergency shelters
                  without discretion in the industrial zone pursuant to Government Code Section
                  65583. Upon review of the emergency shelter zoning resolution and indicated in
                  the element, the Town’s parking standards do not comply with AB 139 (Statutes,
                  2019). Specifically, parking standards should be limited only to employee parking
                  and standards should not be more than other residential or commercial uses in
                  that zone. Program H1-4 commits to amending the operational standards for
                  emergency shelters for consistency with state law. For your information,
                  implementation of this Program must also include revising parking requirements.

   •   Program H2-6: Specific Plan Requirements – The element has accommodated a
       portion of its lower-income RHNA in the C-MU Zone. Additionally, the element
       stated that the Town only allows for residential development of 40 units per acre
       when proposed with a mixed-use development and a specific plan in the C-MU
       zone. The Town has clarified that the specific plan was not required to establish
       zoning or development standards and that zoning was in place prior to the
       planning period. The Town has also recently updated its permitting requirements
       to remove the specific plan and commercial requirements for multifamily projects.
       Program H2-6 commits to monitoring project applications and removing any
       potential constraints, if they arise, throughout the planning period.

   •   Program H5-3: Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing – This Program includes
       various actions and references to other programs to implement place-based
       strategies for community preservation and revitalization, promote new housing
       choices and affordability in higher opportunity areas, and enhance housing
       mobility.

   •   Program H5-2: Reasonable Accommodation – The Town requires a finding to be
       made regarding surrounding uses when approving reasonable accommodation
       requests. This is considered a constraint for persons with disabilities. Program
       H5-2 commits to removing this constraint by 2022.

The Town must monitor and report on the results of this and other programs through the
annual progress report, required pursuant to Government Code section 65400. Please
be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD authority to
review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
inconsistent with an adopted housing element or housing element law. This includes
failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke
housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state
law.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the Town meets housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the Town to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.
