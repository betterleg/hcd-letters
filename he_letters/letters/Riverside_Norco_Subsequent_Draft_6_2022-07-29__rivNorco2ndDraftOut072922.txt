         RE: City of Norco’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Norco’s (City) revised draft housing element
         received for review on June 1, 2022, along with revisions received on July 20, 2022.
         Pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (b), the California
         Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of
         its review. Our review was facilitated by a conversation on July 14, 2022 with Joshua
         Altopp and Alma Robles of your staff and Danielle Thayer, your consultant.

         The revised draft element, incorporating the revisions submitted on July 20, 2022
         addresses many statutory requirements; however, revisions will be necessary to comply
         with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code), as follows:

             1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15
                (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
                assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
                (c)(10)(A).)

                  Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH) - Goals, Priorities, Metrics, and
                  Milestones: While (p. 59) includes a description of objectives and metrics for
                  Program 5.1 to address AFFH requirements, as stated in the previous letter,
                  goals and actions must go beyond status quo actions and significantly seek to
                  overcome contributing factors to fair housing issues and include related metrics
                  and milestones. Specifically, programs should include specific commitment,
                  timelines, geographic targeting and metrics or numerical targets to meaningfully
                  address housing mobility, place-based strategies, and displacement. Finally,
                  some examples of programs that require outcomes and metrics include, but are
                  not limited to, Action 3.4 (Riverside County Partnership Program), Action 5.2

   2. An analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints upon the

       Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
       nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and
       development of housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for
       persons with disabilities… (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

       Development Standards: As stated in the previous letter, the element must
       describe and analyze requirements related to parking. While the element
       provides a parking analysis, the element concludes parking standards might be a
       constraint but does not provide support for the conclusions. In addition, Action
       3.5 (Parking Requirements) only commits to consider a reduction in parking
       standards to one space per bedroom or studio if the developer can demonstrate
       it is a financial or physical constraint. This places the responsibility on the
       developer and could impact the applicability of other laws such as the Housing
       Accountability Act. This program must be revised to include specific
       commitments from the City and should include a monitoring component to
       sufficiently address parking constraints.

The element will meet statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it has
been revised and adopted to comply with the above requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65585.

HCD understands the City has not made draft revisions available to the public prior to
submittal to HCD. Please note that new public participation requirements pursuant to
AB 215 (Chapter 342, Statutes of 2021) require the City to post draft revisions on its
internet website and email a link to the draft revision to all individuals and organizations
that have previously requested notices relating to the local government’s housing
element at least seven days before submitting the draft revision to HCD. By not
providing an opportunity for the public to review and comment on a revised draft of the
element in advance of submission, the City has not yet complied with statutory
mandates. The availability of the document, including revisions received July 20, 2022,
to the public and the opportunity for public comment prior to submittal to HCD is
essential to the public process and HCD’s review. The City must proactively make the
revised draft, including July 20, 2022 revisions, available to the public pursuant to
