         RE: County of Merced’s 6th Cycle (2024-2032) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the County of Merced’s (County) revised draft housing
         element received for review on August 26, 2025. Pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is
         reporting the results of its review. In addition, HCD considered comments from
         Karen Martin from Pacific Planning Group on behalf of Stonefield Home and the
         Leadership Counsel for Justice and Accountability pursuant to Government Code
         section 65585, subdivision (c).

         The revised draft element addresses many statutory requirements described in HCD’s
         January 9, 2025 review; however, revisions will be necessary to comply with State
         Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq), as follows:

              1. Review the previous element to evaluate the appropriateness, effectiveness, and
                 progress in implementation, and reflect the results of this review in the revised
                 element. (Gov. Code, § 65588 (a) and (b).)

                  Special Housing Needs: The element now includes some explanation of
                  outcomes related to the housing needs of special needs populations (e.g.,
                  elderly, persons with disabilities, large households, female-headed households,
                  farmworkers, and persons experiencing homelessness). However, the element
                  still must evaluate the effectiveness of these strategies in meeting the housing
                  needs of all special-needs populations. For example, the element mentions that
                  zoning was completed but should also evaluate whether zoning was effective in
                  facilitating appropriate housing to meet the needs of special needs populations.
                  Based on the outcomes of a complete analysis, programs should be revised as
                  appropriate.

      Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
      throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,
      religion, sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or
      disability, and other characteristics... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

      Enforcement and Outreach: While the element now includes a discussion of
      findings, lawsuits, consent decrees, judgments, and any other legal matters
      regarding fair housing, it must still analyze how the County complies with federal,
      state and local fair housing laws and regulations. Please see HCD’s
      January 9, 2025 review for additional information.

      Identified Sites and Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH): This analysis
      discusses identified sites by income group within CDPs or geographic sub-areas
      and identifies that sites are isolated by income group by a geography smaller
      than census tract. However, the element must still discuss whether the
      distribution of sites improves or exacerbates conditions. If sites exacerbate
      conditions, the element should identify further program actions that will be taken
      to promote equitable quality of life throughout the community (e.g., anti-
      displacement and place-based community revitalization strategies).

      Local Data and Knowledge and Other Relevant Factors: While the element
      briefly utilizes some local data and knowledge and other relevant factors, it
      should expand use of this information to better understand the patterns and
      trends of socioeconomic concentrations and disparities in access to
      opportunities, particularly within community plan areas. For example, the element
      now generally discusses community plan areas and zoning but should discuss
      other factors such as newer areas versus older areas, access to parks or other
      community development issues to better understand disparities in access to
      opportunity and formulate appropriate policies and programs.

      Contributing Factors: Based on the outcomes of a complete analysis, the
      element should re-assess and prioritize contributing factors to fair housing issues
      and add or modify programs as appropriate.

      Programs: Based on the outcomes of a complete analysis, the County must add
      or modify programs. Program must have specific commitment, geographic
      targeting, metrics and milestones and must address, as appropriate, housing
      mobility enhancement, new housing choices and affordability in relatively higher
      income and opportunity areas, place-based strategies for community
      revitalization and displacement protection.

      including… …land use controls... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(5).)

      Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and

      Land Use Controls: The element briefly mentions one community plan area and
      minimum lot sizes but should still list and analyze all zoning and development
      standards for each of the community plan areas that are unique from County-
      wide zoning. The analysis should address impacts on housing supply, cost and
      the ability to achieve maximum allowable densities and based on the outcomes
      of a complete analysis, programs should be added or modified to address
      identified constraints. In addition, the element now mentions that minimum lot
      sizes in one community plan area are a constraint on higher density residential
      development but should also add or modify programs to address the identified
      constraint.

      On/Off-Site Improvements: The element now explains that on and off-site

   5. The Housing Element shall contain programs which assist in the development of
      adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and
      moderate-income households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

      Program 10 (Housing Preservation and Rehabilitation): The Program now
      commits assess remaining damage in Planada but should also commit to
      subsequent action with discrete timelines.

      Program 11 (Lower Income and Special Needs Housing): The Program should
      be revised with discrete timelines (e.g., at least annually) for all relevant actions.

      Program 12 (Farmworker Housing): The Program should commit to how often the
      County will work with regional and other organizations to address the living
      conditions of farmworkers.

      Program 14 (Community and Economic Development): This Program should be
      revised with discrete timing for all actions or describe how often actions will be
      implemented with metrics for specific commitments.

The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once
rezones have been completed (see below) and the element has been revised,
submitted to, and reviewed by HCD to comply with the above requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65585.

As a reminder, the County’s 6th cycle housing element was due January 31, 2024. As
of today, the County has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle.
The County’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements.
HCD encourages the County to revise the element as described above, adopt, and
submit to HCD to regain housing element compliance.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
a local government does not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
statutory deadline (January 31, 2024), then any rezoning to make prior identified sites
available or accommodate the RHNA pursuant to Government Code sections 65583,
subdivision (c) and 65583.2, subdivision (c), shall be completed no later than one year
from the statutory deadline. As this year has passed and Programs 2 (Adequate Sites)
and 3 (By Right Approval) have not been completed, the housing element will remain
out of compliance until all necessary rezoning has been completed.

process, the County must continue to engage the community, including organizations

The County should ensure that information is shared consistently through a variety of
outreach methods and that public input is meaningfully considered and incorporated

Justice and Accountability, provided meaningful suggestions. HCD encourages the
County to continue working with the public, including commenters on this housing
element review, and adjust policies and programs, as appropriate. Please be aware,
any revisions to the element must be posted on the local government’s website and to
email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested notices
relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
submitting to HCD.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Affordable Housing and Sustainable
Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing Allocation consider
housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing element, the County will
meet housing element requirements for these and other funding sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the County to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Land Use and Climate Innovation at:
https://www.lci.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.

HCD appreciates the coordination and time of the housing element update team to

We are committed to assisting the County in addressing all statutory requirements of
