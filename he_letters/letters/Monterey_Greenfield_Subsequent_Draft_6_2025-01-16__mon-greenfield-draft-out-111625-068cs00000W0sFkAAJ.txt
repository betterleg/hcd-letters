         RE: City of Greenfield’s 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Greenfield’s (City) revised draft housing element
         received for review on November 21, 2024. Pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is
         reporting the results of its review.

         The revised draft element addresses many statutory requirements described in HCD’s
         June 17, 2024 review; however, revisions will be necessary to comply with State
         Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq), as follows:

              1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15
                 (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
                 assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
                 (c)(10)(A.))

                  Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
                  throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,
                  religion, sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or
                  disability, and other characteristics. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

                  element was revised to address HCD's prior findings, it should be updated
                  accordingly based on revisions to the anticipated income levels of residential
                  development on sites in the inventory and add or modify programs, as
                  appropriate.

                  Programs: Given that most of the City is considered a lower-resource community,
                  the element could focus on place-based strategies toward community

      Design Review: In response to HCD’s prior review, the element identifies various
      approval findings but should evaluate these approval findings for impacts on
      approval certainty, timing and housing costs and supply. For example, approval
      findings such as “… enhance the character…”, “…quality of design…” and
      “…compatible with the character…” lack clarity and impact approval certainty.
      The element should specifically analyze approval findings and add or modify
      programs to address identified constraints.

      Constraints on Housing for Persons with Disabilities: In response to HCD’s prior
      review, the element was revised to state that the purpose of the conditional use
      permit (CUP) is to ensure compatibility of residential care facilities with
      surrounding areas and uses and additionally identifies required findings related to
      the peace, morals, comfort, and general welfare of the public. The element must
      analyze these findings for impact on timing, cost, supply, and approval certainty
      for housing for persons with disabilities and include programs as appropriate.

   3. Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning
      period with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and
      facilities... Sites shall be identified as needed to facilitate and encourage the
      development of a variety of types of housing for all income levels... (Gov. Code, §
      65583, subd. (c)(1).)

      Program 6.4.F (Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) Incentives): The Program was
      revised to include frequent monitoring of permitted ADUs and affordability but
      should benchmark alternative actions to the monitoring instead of adoption of the
      housing element. For example, the Program could include language such as
      “…take appropriate action such as adjusting assumptions or rezoning within one
      year of an evaluation if ADU assumptions are not met”.

      Program 6.4.L (Funding for Farmworker Housing): The Program was revised to
      clarify that the City will explore regulatory incentives and funding opportunities for
      farmworker housing by the end of 2025 but should include benchmarks for
      implementation throughout the planning period, including specific commitments
      to adopt identified incentives by a specified date. In addition, the program should
      include measurable metrics or numeric objectives.

      Program 6.6.C (Utility Infrastructure Improvements): The element was not
      revised to address this requirement. Please see HCD's June 17, 2024 review for
      additional information.

      By-Right Permanent Supportive Housing: The element describes that permanent
      supportive housing for six or fewer zones is permitted without discretionary
      action. However, the element does not demonstrate compliance with
      Government Code section 65651 which requires multifamily to be permitted
      without discretionary in zones allowing multifamily, including non-residential
      zones in addition to various other provisions such as parking and process timing.
      The element should include a program to amend zoning to comply with
      Government Code section 65651.

   4. The Housing Element shall contain programs which assist in the development of
      adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and
      moderate-income households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

      Program 6.2.F (ELI Households): While the Program was revised to commit the
      City to assisting, supporting, and pursuing funding applications and conducting
      outreach and coordinating with affordable housing developers, the Program
      should also include discrete timing for implementation throughout the planning
      period. For example, the Program could commit to pursue funding and outreach
      to developers annually. In addition, the program should include measurable
      metrics or numeric objectives.

      Program 6.4.B (Housing for Farmworkers): The Program was revised to commit
      to proactive outreach and encourage agricultural family housing projects.
      However, the Program should still include specific and measurable actions to
      identify specific development opportunities, for example, by maintaining an
      inventory of sites appropriate for farmworker housing and updating the list on an
      annual basis.

   5. Establish the number of housing units, by income level, that can be constructed,
      rehabilitated, and conserved over a five-year time frame. (Gov. Code, § 65583,
      subd. (b)(1 & 2).)

      The element was not revised to address this requirement. Please see HCD's
      January 18, 2024, and June 17, 2024 reviews for additional information.


The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once
rezoning has been completed (see below) and the element has been revised, adopted,
submitted to, and reviewed by HCD to comply with the above requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65585.

As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due December 15, 2023. As of
today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the City to revise the element as described above, adopt if needed, and
submit to HCD to regain housing element compliance.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
a local government does not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
statutory deadline (December 15, 2023), then any rezoning to make prior identified sites
available or accommodate the Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA) pursuant to
Government Code sections 65583, subdivision (c) and 65583.2, subdivision (c), shall be
completed no later than one year from the statutory deadline. As this year has passed
and Programs 6.2.J (Prior Identified Sites) has not been completed, the housing
element is out of compliance and will remain out of compliance until the rezoning have
been completed.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the City should continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
available and considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be
aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local government’s website
and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested
notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
submitting to HCD.

Please note, pursuant to Government Code section 65583.3, subdivision (b), the City
must utilize standards, forms, and definitions adopted by HCD when preparing the sites
inventory. While the City has submitted an electronic version of the sites inventory, if
changes occur, any future re-adopted version of the element must also submit the
electronic version of the sites inventory. The City can reach out to HCD at
sitesinventory@hcd.ca.gov for technical assistance.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Affordable Housing and Sustainable
Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent Local Housing Allocation consider
housing element compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to
Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing element, the City will meet
housing element requirements for these and other funding sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.

HCD is committed to assisting the City in addressing all statutory requirements of State
Housing Element Law. If you have any questions or need additional technical
assistance, please contact Clare Blackwell, of our staff, at Clare.Blackwell@hcd.ca.gov.
