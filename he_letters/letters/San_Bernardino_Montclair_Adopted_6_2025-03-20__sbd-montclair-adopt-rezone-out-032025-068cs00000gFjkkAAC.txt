         RE: City of Montclair’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Montclair’s (City) housing element, which was
         adopted December 2, 2024, and received for review on January 22, 2025. The
         California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) also received
         Ordinances 24-1009 and 24-1010 and associated planning documents (Ordinances and
         Planning Documents) related to rezoning. Pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, HCD is reporting the results of its review.

         On July 27, 2022, HCD found the revised draft housing element met statutory
         requirements of State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et seq). The adopted
         element was found to be substantially the same as the revised draft element that HCD’s
         July 27, 2022 review determined met statutory requirements. However, the housing
         element could not be found in substantial compliance until the City had completed
         necessary rezones (Policy Action 3.7 – Adequate Sites) to accommodate the regional
         housing needs allocation (RHNA). While the Ordinances and Planning Documents are
         intended to meet these rezoning requirements and do meet some requirements such as
         allowing 100 percent residential uses, the Ordinances and Planning Documents do not
         meet all statutory requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65583.2,
         subdivisions (h) and (i). Specifically, each of the Planning Documents (Montclair
         Corridors Code and Arrowhead Highway Mixed-Use Development Specific Plan) note
         all projects shall be consistent with the analysis in the housing element. However, the
         Planning Documents, for the lower-income RHNA shortfall, should explicitly:

              •   Permit multifamily residential use by-right for developments in which at least 20
                  percent of the units are affordable to lower-income households. By-right
                  generally means non-discretionary review.

              •   Require at least a minimum density of 20 units per acre. Please note, this
                  requirement is met in the Commercial – Mixed-Use and Corridor Zones but is not

Further, allowable heights in the Commercial – Mixed-Use, Neighborhood Edge and
Corridor and Neighborhood Zones appear to be constraints on development. The City
should evaluate the impacts of these heights on the ability to facilitate maximum
densities and make adjustments as appropriate as part amendments to the associated
planning documents. For example, increasing heights by two stories in each of the
zones would facilitate achieving maximum allowable densities.

Finally, the City submitted an electronic sites inventory to HCD pursuant to Government
Code section 65583.3, subdivision (b). Many of these sites identified toward the RHNA
do not appear to be listed in Exhibit B of the Ordinance and as a result, adequate
capacity does not appear to be identified to accommodate the RHNA, particularly for
lower-income households. The City should reconcile this information to ensure
adequate sites are available to accommodate the RHNA.

Once the City has amended the Planning Documents to address the above
requirements, the City should submit all pertinent approval documentation. HCD will
review the documentation and issue correspondence identifying the updated status of
the City’s housing element compliance. HCD welcomes the opportunity to review draft
documents to assist in meeting these requirements.

The City must monitor and report on the results of these and other programs through
the annual progress report, required pursuant to Government Code section 65400.
Please be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD authority
to review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
inconsistent with an adopted housing element or Housing Element Law. This includes
failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke
housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state
law.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant, the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities program, and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

HCD appreciates the hard work and commitment of the City in taking significant steps
toward a compliant housing element and is committed to assisting the City in
addressing all statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law. If you have any
questions or need additional technical assistance, please contact Alex Goelzer, of our
staff, at Alex.Goelzer@hcd.ca.gov.
