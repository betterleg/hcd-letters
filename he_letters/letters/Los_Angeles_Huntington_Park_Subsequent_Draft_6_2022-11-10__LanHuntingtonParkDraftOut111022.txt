         RE: City of Huntington Park’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Huntington Park’s (City) revised draft housing
         element update received for review on September 27, 2022. Pursuant to Government
         Code section 65585, subdivision (b), the California Department of Housing and
         Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. Our review was
         facilitated by a telephone conversation on November 1, 2022 with you, City Attorney
         Araceli Almazan, and your housing element consultants. In addition, HCD considered
         comments from Communities for a Better Environment (CBE), Disability Rights
         California, and the Public Interest Law Project (PILP) pursuant to Government Code
         section 65585, subdivision (c). The review was expedited in accordance with
         Government Code section 65754, subdivision (a).

         The draft housing element addresses most statutory requirements described in HCD’s
         January 14, 2022 letter; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully comply with
         State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code), see enclosed Appendix.

         Pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), a jurisdiction that failed
         to adopt a compliant housing element within one year from the statutory deadline
         cannot be found in compliance until rezones to accommodate a shortfall of sites
         pursuant to Government Code section 65583, subdivision (c),paragraph (1),
         subparagraph (A) and Government Code section 65583.2, subdivision (c) are
         completed. As this year has passed and Action10-5 (Transit-Oriented Development
         Overlay District) has not been completed, the housing element is out of compliance and
         will remain out of compliance until the rezoning have been completed.

Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting

element, the City meets housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

                                        APPENDIX A
                                 CITY OF HUNTINGTON PARK

The following changes are necessary to bring the City’s housing element into compliance with
Article 10.6 of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite the
supporting section of the Government Code.

Housing element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-memos.shtml.
Among other resources, the housing element section contains HCD’s latest technical assistance
tool, Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks), available at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/index.shtml and includes the
Government Code addressing State Housing Element Law and other resources.


   A. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

      1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
         Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
         the jurisdiction (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A))

          Disproportionate Housing Needs: As stated in the previous review the element, a complete
          analysis of substandard housing should identify local patterns and trends and could
          address, for example, the actual number and percentage of households with one or more of
          the four housing problems, comparing findings throughout the jurisdiction. This is critical as
          the element states that according to the 2020-2025 Five-Year Consolidated Plan,
          approximately 12,395 units citywide are estimated to need some level of rehabilitation
          and/or may require replacement (page II-13). Given the increase in homelessness within
          the City, the element should include a complete analysis of characteristics of persons
          experiencing homelessness including by protected characteristics such as race and
          ethnicity, familial status, and persons with disabilities.

          Site Inventory: The sites inventory now includes sites within the proposed. Transit-
          Oriented Development (TOD) overlay. The element should evaluate the displacement
          pressures on existing residents in these areas and could also evaluate displacement
          pressures of the sites inventory on small business owners in the Downtown Specific
          Plan area and TOD overlay. The element should also further evaluate siting housing in
          areas with high pollution exposure and include programs with specific actions, metrics,
          and milestones to address both displacement risk and environmental risk.

          Goals, Priorities, Metrics and Milestones: While the element now identifies programs to
          address contributing factors, many programs must still be revised to include
          quantifiable objectives and concrete actions. Programs must include metrics and
          milestones for evaluating progress on programs, actions, and fair housing results.
          Given that most of the City is considered low or moderate resource programs could
          concentrate on increasing housing choice, protection from displacement, and place-
          based strategies for economic and community revitalization. In addition, the element
          should revise programs as follows:

          assist in remediation, the element must identify the sites that are likely to require

          preclude residential development within the planning period.

          to access to infrastructure were included, it is still unclear whether sufficient existing or
          planned water, sewer, and other dry utilities supply capacity to accommodate the City’s
          regional housing need for the planning period.

          Zoning for a Variety of Housing Types:

          Emergency Shelters: The element now states that “Article 20 contains a number of
          provisions that exceed standards allowed by state law, and several standards that require
          discretion to apply” (page IV-15). While the element includes Action 7-1 (Zoning Changes

          identify which standards will be modified or removed to comply with state law. For example,
          it is HCD’s understanding that some requirements include requiring provider of emergency

          are not required for other uses in same zone; and a broad requirement to include “other
          amenities” as decided by the Director of Community Development. In addition, as noted in
          the prior review, the element must analyze the impact of the 30-bed limit to ensure that the
          standard does not unduly constrain the development of emergency shelters.

      3. An analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints upon the maintenance,
         improvement, or development of housing for all income levels, including the types of
         housing identified in paragraph (1) of subdivision (c), and for persons with disabilities
         as identified in the analysis pursuant to paragraph (7), including land use controls,
         building codes and their enforcement, site improvements, fees and other exactions
         required of developers, and local processing and permit procedures. The analysis shall
         also demonstrate local efforts to remove governmental constraints that hinder the
         locality from meeting its share of the regional housing need in accordance with
         Government Code section 65584 and from meeting the need for housing for persons
         with disabilities, supportive housing, transitional housing, and emergency shelters
         identified pursuant to paragraph (7).

          Fees and Exactions: Action 8-1 (Evaluation Fees) to review planning and development fees
          to ensure fees are not excessive and are appropriate to cover the cost of services provided
          does not substitute the required analysis as part of housing element. The element must still
          include an analysis of the fees, as stated in the previous review, including an estimate of
          total fees or their proportion of total development costs for single-family or multi-family
          housing.

          Processing and Permit Procedures: The element include Action 7-2 (Address
          Constraints) to modify permit requirements for multi-family residential projects from a
          conditional use permit (CUP) to a development permit. However, while the element
          describes the development permit process it does not analyze the process as a
          potential constraint. For example, the revised element states that one of the findings is
          “the proposed development would be harmonious and compatible with existing and
          planned future developments within the zoning district and general area, as well as
          with the land uses presently on the subject property” (page IV-21). The element must
          evaluate the subject nature of such findings and describe and evaluate the approval
          process and time frames, for impact on approval certainty, supply, affordability, timing,
          and other relevant factors. The element should add or modify programs to mitigate or
          remove identified constraints.

          Housing for Persons with Disabilities: The element continues to not include an
          evaluation of the CUP process for group homes of 7 or more as a constraint on
          housing persons with disabilities and generally consults the CUP does not pose a
          constraint. Zoning code regulations that isolate and regulate various types of housing
          for persons with disabilities based on the number of people and other factors may pose
          a constraint on housing choice for persons with disabilities. Zoning and standards
          should simply implement a barrier-free definition of family instead of requiring the
          development to meet special regulations, such a conditional use permit, potentially
          subjecting housing for persons with disabilities to higher discretionary standards where
          an applicant must demonstrate compatibility with the neighborhood, unlike other
          residential uses. The element should analyze the process as a potential constraint on
          housing for persons with disabilities and add or modify programs as appropriate to
          ensure zoning permits group homes objectively with approval certainty.

   B. Housing Programs

      1. Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period
         with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities to
         accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing need
         for each income level that could not be accommodated on sites identified in the
         inventory completed pursuant to paragraph (3) of subdivision (a) without rezoning, and
         to comply with the requirements of Government Code section 65584.09. Sites shall be
         identified as needed to facilitate and encourage the development of a variety of types
         of housing for all income levels, including multifamily rental housing, factory-built
         housing, mobilehomes, housing for agricultural employees, supportive housing, single-
         room occupancy units, emergency shelters, and transitional housing.
         (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

          As noted in Finding A2, the element does not include a complete site analysis;
          therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning was not established. Based on the results
          of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City may need to add or revise
          programs to address a shortfall of sites or zoning available to encourage a variety of
          housing types. In addition, the element should be revised as follows:

          Action 10-5: To accommodate the City’s remaining RHNA, the element is adopting a
          TOD overlay. While HCD recognizes that zoning has not yet been created to
          implement the new overlay, the element should generally describe and commit to
          expectations for development standards in these zones. For example, the element
          should describe expected height limits, parking, and other standards that will facilitate
          development at the densities proposed. The program should also indicate how
          multifamily developments will be permitted and process for approval of applications.

          In addition, for sites to be rezoned to accommodate the lower-income RHNA the
          element must ensure that Action 10-5 complies with the requirements of Government
          Code section 65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i). The program must commit to:

      2. The Housing Element shall contain programs which assist in the development of
         adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and moderate-

          The element does not address this finding. Please refer to HCD’s prior review to meet

      3. Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
         nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and development of
         housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for persons with
         disabilities. The program shall remove constraints to, and provide reasonable
         accommodations for housing designed for, intended for occupancy by, or with
         supportive services for, persons with disabilities. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

          As noted in Findings A3, the element requires a complete analysis of potential
          governmental and nongovernmental constraints. Depending upon the results of that
          analysis, the City may need to revise or add programs and address and remove or
          mitigate any identified constraints.

          Action 7-4 (Zoning Changes to Address Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH)
          Issues): While the program now commits to remove the requirement for a Reasonable
          Accommodation application fee, it should also commit to revising the ordinance to be
          consistent with state and federal reasonable accommodation guidance. For example,
          HCD understands that the City considers a reasonable accommodation request as a

          conditional use permit and states that it cannot waive any other entitlements and
          mandates that a reasonable accommodation requires planning commission approval in
