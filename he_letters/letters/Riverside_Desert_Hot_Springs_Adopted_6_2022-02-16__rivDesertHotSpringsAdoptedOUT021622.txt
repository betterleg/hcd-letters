         RE: Desert Hot Springs 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Desert Hot Springs (City) housing element adopted
         November 2, 2021 and received for review on November 18, 2021. Pursuant to
         Government Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing
         and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. In addition,
         HCD considered comments from Inland Counties Legal Services, Inc (ICLS). pursuant
         to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (c).

         The adopted housing element addresses most statutory requirements described in
         HCD’s August 12, 2021 review; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully
         comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code), as follows:

             1. Review the previous element to evaluate the appropriateness, effectiveness, and
                progress in implementation, and reflect the results of this review in the revised
                element. (Gov. Code, § 65588 (a) and (b).)

                  The element did not address this requirement. As part of the evaluation of
                  programs in the past cycle, the element must provide an analysis of the
                  effectiveness of goals, policies, and related actions in meeting the housing needs
                  of special needs populations (e.g., elderly, persons with disabilities, large
                  households, female headed households, farmworkers and persons experiencing
                  homelessness).

      Integration and Segregation: The element should be revised and updated to
      include complete local and regional data on integration and segregation. While
      the element includes local and regional data on income levels and states that the
      Hispanic population is "moderately segregated" with an isolation index of 56 (p.
      B-27), it does not analyze the data for patterns and trends over time. In addition,
      while the element provides an explanation of the Divergence Index, it does not
      provide actual data for the City.

      Racially/Ethnically Concentrated Areas of Poverty (R/ECAP): While the element
      concludes that it does not have racially and ethnically concentrated areas of
      poverty, Census tracts 044507 and 044509 have been designated by HUD as
      R/ECAPs, and additional tracts within the City have been designated by
      TCAC/HCD Opportunity maps as areas of high segregation and poverty. The
      element should be revised to include updated local and regional data and
      analyze this data for trends and patterns. In addition, the element should
      consider input from commenters (ICLS) on this review as part of this analysis.

      Site Inventory: The City should re-evaluate the sites inventory to adequately
      reflect location of R/ECAPs (census tracts 044507 and 044509) and must further
      analyze to ensure sites will. Based on the findings, the site inventory analysis
      should analysis if sites identified improve conditions (or if sites exacerbate
      conditions, how a program can mitigate the impact), whether the sites are
      isolated by income group and should be supported by local data and knowledge.

      Goals, Actions, Metrics and Milestones: As noted above, the element must
      include a complete analysis of AFFH. Based on the outcomes of that analysis,
      the element must add or modify programs. In addition, actions must have specific
      commitment, metrics, and milestones and be significant and meaningful enough
      to overcome identified patterns and trends.

      Permit Processing Procedures: The element was revised to include some
      additional information relating the development timeframes for single family
      subdivisions and describes the development permit. However, it does not
      describe the typical processing timeframes for multifamily housing and does not
      analyze the subjective approval findings for development permit for potential
      constraints to the development of housing. For example, the City should analyze
      findings such as compatibility requirements with existing future developments,
      and that the project is not detrimental to the public interest, health, safety,
      convenience, or welfare of the City lack are subjective and lack certainty and
      transparency in the development review process. In addition, the revised element
      now references the need for architectural review but does not describe or
      analyze the requirements or process for architectural review.

      Housing For Persons with Disabilities: While the element now describes findings
      for granting reasonable accommodations and includes a program to develop a
      brochure, the element does not describe the actual process for applying for
      reasonable accommodation, including who reviews and approves requests.

      Programs: As noted above, the element requires a complete analysis of potential
      governmental constraints. Depending upon the results of a complete analysis,
      the City may need to revise or add programs and address and remove or
      mitigate any identified constraints.

   5. The housing element shall contain programs which assist in the development of
      adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and
      moderate-income households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

      While the element includes Program 2.6 (Extremely Low Income [ELI]) supports
      ELI populations through Programs 2.1 (Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher
      Program), 2.4 (Regional Plan for Development of 2,000 Affordable Housing
      Units), and 4.2 (Homeowner Housing Rehabilitation Program), each program
      must clearly identify and state that it is assisting in development of housing to
      affordable to ELI households. In addition, the City should consider public
      comments from ICLS when updating programs actions to meet this requirement.


The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it
has been revised and re-adopted to comply with the above requirements.

As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due October 15, 2021. As of
today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the City to revise the element as described in this letter, re-adopt, and
submit to HCD to regain housing element compliance. For more information on
housing element adoption requirements, please visit HCD’s website at:
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-
memos/docs/sb375_final100413.pdf.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
a local government fails to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
statutory deadline (October 15, 2021), then any rezoning to accommodate the
Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA), including for lower-income households,
shall be completed no later than one year from the statutory deadline. Otherwise, the
local government’s housing element will no longer comply with State Housing Element
Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial compliance pursuant to
Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i).

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.
