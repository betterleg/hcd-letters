         RE: City of Pico Rivera’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Pico Rivera’s (City) revised draft housing element
         received for review on March 24, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585,
         subdivision (b), the California Department of Housing and Community Development
         (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

         The revised draft housing element addresses many statutory requirements described in
         HCD’s October 18, 2021 review; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully
         comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code). The enclosed
         Appendix describes the revisions needed to comply with State Housing Element Law.

         Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
         element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
         process, the City must continue to engage the community, including organizations that
         represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
         available while considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be
         aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local government’s website
         and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested
         notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
         submitting to HCD.

         As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due October 15, 2021. As of
         today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
         City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
         encourages the City to revise the element as described above, adopt, and submit to
         HCD to regain housing element compliance.

         For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
         a local government fails to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of this

The following changes are necessary to bring the City’s housing element into compliance with
Article 10.6 of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite the
supporting section of the Government Code.

Housing element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-
memos.shtml. Among other resources, the housing element section contains HCD’s latest
technical assistance tool, Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks),
available at http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/index.shtml and
includes the Government Code addressing State Housing Element Law and other resources.


A. Review and Revision

   Review the previous element to evaluate the appropriateness, effectiveness, and progress
   in implementation, and reflect the results of this review in the revised element. (Gov. Code,
   § 65588 (a) and (b).)

   General: In response to HCD’s prior review, the element now includes some minimal
   revisions to Appendix A, but additional information is required to address HCD’s findings.
   Specifically, the element was not revised to address prior findings related to Programs 5, 6,
   9, 12, 16, and 18. For example, Programs 5 (Developer Consultation) and 12
   (Development of Housing for Large Households) specify that the City held pre-application
   meetings with developers but should include information on quantified metrics and
   milestones and whether those efforts were effective in addressing program goals. As
   another example, Program 16 (Lot Consolidation) states that no requests were received for
   lot consolidation in the prior planning period, but should also describe lessons learned,
   challenges to implementation, and evaluate the effectiveness of past efforts. Based on the
   outcomes of a complete review, the element should add or modify programs.

   Housing for Special Needs: In response to HCD’s prior review, the element now includes
   some additional information in Appendix A on various efforts toward addressing the
   housing of special needs populations including seniors and persons with disabilities.
   However, additional information on past efforts of goals, policies, and programs should
   include quantified metrics and milestones for each of these groups. As an example, the
   element describes pre-application consultation meetings the City held with developers, but
   does not provide information on timelines, number of meetings held, and the results and
   effectiveness of such efforts.

   Adequate Sites Rezone: In response to HCD’s prior review, the element includes revisions
   to Appendix A for Program 15 (Provision of Adequate Sites for Housing Development) that
   indicated that rezoning in the prior planning period occurred to address a shortfall of sites

B. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

   1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing (AFFH) in accordance with Chapter 15
      (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
      assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A).)

      Enforcement and Outreach: In response to HCD’s prior review, the element now
      includes additional information that quantifies the number of housing discrimination
      inquiries and provides some analysis of the data. However, the element must still
      address HCD’s findings, specifically on compliance with fair housing laws and
      supplemental information on fair housing aside from the Analysis of Impediments (AI).
      Please see HCD’s prior review for additional information.

      Integration and Segregation: In response to HCD’s prior review, the element now
      includes some additional information on racially concentrated areas of affluence
      (RCAAs), disability status, and familial status (page 3-53), but still does not include
      trend and pattern analysis by income, particularly at the local level. In addition, the
      element was not revised to consider spatial analysis of racial and income dissimilarities.
      Please see HCD’s prior review for additional information.

      Disparities in Access to Opportunity: In response to HCD’s prior review, the element
      now includes some additional analysis and information for each of the indicators
      (education, economic, transportation, and environmental) at a regional level on pages 3-
      56 to 3-66. However, additional information is required. More specifically, the element
      must include information on local disparities in each of the four areas at a neighborhood
      or tract level. The analysis should also include local and regional knowledge, evaluate
      patterns and changes over time, and consider other relevant factors, such as public
      participation, past policies, practices, and investments and demographic trends.

      Disproportionate Housing Needs, Including Displacement: In response to HCD’s prior
      review, the element now includes some additional analysis on pages 3-66 to 3-82
      regarding cost burden, overcrowding, and displacement. However, the element must
      still address homelessness and housing conditions and include data and mapping
      analysis, evaluate spatial patterns and trends, and conclude with a summary of fair
      housing issues based on these observations.

      Local Data and Knowledge, and Other Relevant Factors: The element was not revised
      to address this finding. Please see HCD’s prior review for additional information.

      Contributing Factors to Fair Housing Issues: Based on the outcomes of a complete

      AFFH and Identified Sites: In response to HCD’s prior review, the element now includes

      overcome contributing factors to fair housing issues. For programs to be meaningful,
      actions must have metrics and milestones that overcome identified patterns and trends,
      address systemic inequities in fair housing, and increase opportunities in the areas of
      housing mobility, community preservation, revitalization, among others. Please see
      HCD’s prior review for additional information.

D. Public Participation

   Local governments shall make a diligent effort to achieve public participation of all
   economic segments of the community in the development of the housing element, and the
   element shall describe this effort. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(9).)

   As noted in the prior review, the City should employ additional methods for public outreach
   efforts, particularly including lower-income and special needs households and
   neighborhoods with higher concentrations of lower-income and special needs households.
   In response, the element now includes Program 6D (Community Outreach) to conduct
   community outreach throughout the City with a focus in under-represented communities.
   First, Program 6D should commit to also employ a variety of outreach methods, beyond
   workshops and public meetings. Second, Program 6D should commit to action based on
   the outreach within a specified time in a manner that has beneficial housing and community
   development outcomes. Lastly, the prior finding was intended to inform this housing
   element update, not just implementation. As part of this update, including through adoption,
   the City should employ additional methods for public outreach. Please see HCD’s prior
   review for additional information.
