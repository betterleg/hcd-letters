         RE: City of Oceanside’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Oceanside’s revised draft housing element received
         for review on December 6, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585,
         subdivision (b), the California Department of Housing and Community Development
         (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. In addition, HCD considered comments from
         the Building Industry Association (BIA), Diane Nygaard and David Kellogg, pursuant to
         Government Code section 65585, subdivision (c).

         The revised draft element addresses many statutory requirements from HCD’s April 22, 2022
         review; however, the following revisions will be necessary to comply with State Housing
         Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code).

             1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15
                (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
                assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
                (c)(10)(A)).

                  throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,

         •   Housing Mobility – HCD’s prior review indicated that programs must
             address enhancing housing mobility. For reference, housing mobility is
             strategically removing barriers and enhancing housing choices and
             affordability throughout the community, including areas of higher
             opportunity or relatively higher income areas. In response, the revised
             element identified Program 5 (Accessory Dwelling Units) and Program 12
             (Housing Choice Vouchers). These programs must be revised with
             additional actions, significant metrics and more proactive outreach and
             implementation efforts.

         •   Increasing Housing Choices and Affordability in Higher Resources/Income
             Areas –Programs 3 (Inclusionary) and 7 (Adequate Sites) should be
             revised with accurate geographic targeting. For example, it is unclear
             whether the City’s rezone efforts, regional housing need allocation
             (RHNA) sites, or Smart Sustainable Corridors Plan encompass higher
             resourced/higher income areas. In addition, these programs should
             include implementation steps, geographic targeting, and clarification on
             how these efforts relate to providing housing choices and affordability in
             higher opportunity or relatively higher income areas.

         •   Place-Based Strategies toward Community Revitalization – As noted in
             HCD’s prior review and supported by the City’s analysis of affirmatively
             furthering fair housing (AFFH), many of the central census tracts from east
             to west include households that are disproportionately lower incomes;
             majority-minority communities; experiencing higher rates of overcrowding;
             and low access to resources and opportunities including environmental
             impacts. Additionally, as noted in the revised analysis and prior reviews,
             the City has a census tract designated as higher poverty and segregation

             notably Eastside and Crown Heights neighborhoods. The analysis also
             indicated that community feedback demonstrated that lack of investment

      Progress in Meeting RHNA: HCD’s prior review noted that the element had
      included an additional 1,928 units as credit towards meet the City’s RHNA. As a
      result, HCD found that the element needs demonstrate the availability of these
      units during the planning period. The element was revised to reduce the number
      of units to 1,900 and included updated info about the status of a project (e.g.,
      whether it’s been entitled or approved). This does not address HCD’s finding.
      The element could discuss how the status of the project demonstrates that these
      units will be built and available during the planning period. In addition, the
      analysis can consider other factors to demonstrate the availability of units within
      the planning period such as infrastructure schedules, City’s past completion rates
      on pipeline projects, outreach with project developers, and should describe any
      expiration dates on entitlements, anticipated timelines for final approvals, and
      any remaining steps for projects to receive final entitlements.

      Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: For your information, the element now relies on
      nonvacant sites to accommodate more than 50 percent of the RHNA for lower-
      income households. The element must demonstrate existing uses are not an
      impediment to additional residential development and will likely discontinue in the
      planning period. (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd. (g)(2).) Absent findings (e.g.,
      adoption resolution) based on substantial evidence, the existing uses will be
      presumed to impede additional residential development and will not be utilized
      toward demonstrating adequate sites to accommodate the (RHNA).

      Small Sites: As stated in HCD’s prior review, sites smaller than a half-acre in size
      are deemed inadequate to accommodate housing for lower-income housing
      unless it is demonstrated that sites of equivalent size were successfully
      developed during the prior planning period for an equivalent number of lower-
      income housing units as projected for the site or unless the housing element
      describes other evidence the site is adequate to accommodate lower income
      housing.

      While the element was revised to include three project examples for above
      moderate development on vacant smaller sites that utilized lot consolidation, this
      generally does not address the finding. Specifically, the element should include
      additional examples that reflect the sites in the inventory and the methodology
      discussed. For example, the element stated that smaller sites and lot
      consolidation is likely due to contiguous parcels with similar redevelopment
      potential, and commercial blocks with marginal uses. To better support the
      methodology, the element should include additional examples with similar site
      characteristics. Lastly, the City should consider adding or modifying programs to
      include incentives for lot consolidation.

      Electronic Sites Inventory: For your information, pursuant to Government Code
      section 65583.3, the City must submit an electronic sites inventory with its
      adopted housing element. The City must utilize standards, forms, and definitions
      adopted by HCD. Please see HCD’s housing element webpage at
      https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-
      element/index.shtml#element for a copy of the form and instructions. The City
      can reach out to HCD at sitesinventory@hcd.ca.gov for technical assistance.

      Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete analysis;
      therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the
      results of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City may need to add or
      revise programs.


The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it
has been revised and, if necessary, re-adopted to comply with the above requirements
pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

Chapter 654, Statutes of 2022 (AB 2339), adds additional specificity on how cities and
counties plan for emergency shelters and ensure sufficient and suitable capacity. If the
draft is not found in compliance by April 1, 2023, then future submittals of the housing
element may need to address these statutory requirements. For additional information
and timing requirements, please see HCD’s memo at
https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/planning-and-community/ab2339-
notice.pdf.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021),
as the City failed to adopt a housing element that was found in compliance within 120
days of the statutory deadline (April 15, 2021), Program 7 (Adequate Residential Sites
to Accommodate the RHNA and No Net Loss) to rezone 1,086 units to accommodate
the RHNA must be completed no later than one year from the statutory deadline. As
that timeframe has passed and in accordance with Government Code Section 65588,
subsection (e)(4)(C)(iii), HCD cannot find the housing element in substantial compliance
with housing element law until the rezones are completed.
Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. During the housing element revision
process, the City must continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
available while considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be
aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local government’s website
and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested
notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
submitting to HCD.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.

We are committed to assisting the City in addressing all statutory requirements of State
Housing Element Law. If you have any questions or need technical assistance, please
contact Sohab Mehmood, of our staff, at sohab.mehmood@hcd.ca.gov.
