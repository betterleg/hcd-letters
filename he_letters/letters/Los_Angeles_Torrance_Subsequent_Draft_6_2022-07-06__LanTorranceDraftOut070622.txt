         RE: City of Torrance’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Torrance’s (City) revised draft housing element
         which was received for review on May 10, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, subdivision (b), the California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. Our review was facilitated by a
         conversation on June 23, 2022 with you, Gregg Lodan, Planning Manager;
         Carolyn Chun, Senior Planning Associate; Kevin Joe, Planning Associate and your
         Consultants Patricia Bluman, Veronica Tam, and Claudia Tedford. In addition, HCD
         considered comments from California for Homeownership pursuant to Government
         Code section 65585, subdivision (c).

         The revised draft element addresses many statutory requirements; however, revisions
         will be necessary to comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov.
         Code). The enclosed Appendix describes revisions needed to comply with State
         Housing Element Law.

         As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due October 15, 2021. As of
         today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
         City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
         encourages the City to revise the element as described above, adopt, and submit to
         HCD to regain housing element compliance.

         For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
         a local government fails to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
         statutory deadline (October 15, 2021), then any rezoning to accommodate the regional
         housing needs allocation (RHNA), including for lower-income households, shall be
         completed no later than one year from the statutory deadline. Otherwise, the local
         government’s housing element will no longer comply with State Housing Element Law,

The following changes are necessary to bring the City’s housing element into compliance with
Article 10.6 of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite the

Housing element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-memos.shtml.
Among other resources, the housing element section contains HCD’s latest technical assistance
tool, Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks), available at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/index.shtml and includes the
Government Code addressing State Housing Element Law and other resources.


A. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

   1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
      Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
      the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A).)

      Sites Inventory: The element was revised to include an analysis addressing the income
      categories of identified sites for all components of the assessment of fair housing (e.g.,
      segregation and integration, access to opportunity). However, the analysis should address
      the sites by income group and location (e.g., census tract, neighborhood) and then
      evaluate the impact of identified sites on existing patterns of socio-economic
      concentrations, including any isolation of the identified sites by income group.

      Local Data and Knowledge: The element generally was not revised to address this
      requirement and must still complement federal, state, and regional data with local data and
      knowledge where appropriate to capture emerging trends and issues, including utilizing
      knowledge from local and regional advocates, public comments, and service providers.
      Other examples of local data and knowledge include information and outreach from local
      officials (e.g., planning, engineering and codes departments), a local or regional analysis
      of fair housing impediments and, other planning documents such as an environmental
      justice element of a general plan, past redevelopment plans and capital improvement
      plans.

      Goals, Priorities, Metrics, and Milestones: As noted in the prior review, based on a
      complete assessment of fair housing, the element may need to add or modify programs.
      While the element added analysis and includes many meaningful actions with milestones
      under Program 19 (Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing), individual actions should have
      metrics to target meaningful outcomes in the planning period. In addition, the element
      should include actions with specific commitment to explicitly affirmatively furthering fair
      housing (AFFH) by, for example, targeting geographic areas throughout the community.
      Lastly, the element should include programs to enhance housing mobility strategies and

   2. Include an analysis of population and employment trends and documentation of
      projections and a quantification of the locality's existing and projected needs for all income

   4. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including vacant
      sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for redevelopment during the
      planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for a designated income level, and an

      Parcel Listing: The parcel listing was revised to include the following columns: reason for
      change (deletion, revisions, etc.), existing use, age a factor, LV>IV, and other. Footnotes
      are also included with exceptions/clarification for some sites. The element also clarified the
      mixed-income sites assume that 80 percent of the units will be available to above-
      moderate households with the remaining 20 percent available to lower-income households
      (p. 123). However, as noted in the prior review, the parcel listing must specify the number
      of units being credited toward lower, moderate, and above moderate. Therefore, the parcel
      listing should be revised for sites identifying the affordability level of the units as “mixed”
      and reflect the anticipated number of units by affordability level.

      Progress in Meeting the Regional Housing Need Allocation (RHNA): The element was
      revised to indicate that 22501 Hawthorne Boulevard (APN 7368-001-015) and
      4111 Pacific Coast Highway (APN 7529-019-003) include 17 and 11 very low-income units
      pending approval, respectively. However, the element must demonstrate how affordability
      of the units was determined based on actual sales price or rent levels or other
      mechanisms ensuring affordability (e.g., deed restrictions).

      Realistic Capacity: The element assumes the Commercial General (C-GEN), Commercial
      City Center (C-CTR), and Residential Office (R-OF) will be developed with 100 percent
      residential uses (Table H-53). However, as noted in the prior review, the element must
      include an analysis to support the assumption and adjust capacity calculations if
      necessary. While the element lists several recent projects and built densities, it still must
      account for the likelihood of 100 the likelihood of 100 nonresidential occurring. For
      example, the element notes recent mixed-use development but provides no information on
      100 percent nonresidential developments. To address this requirement, the element could
      analyze all development activity in these nonresidential zones, how often residential
      development occurs and adjust residential capacity calculations, policies, and programs
      accordingly.

      Small Sites: The element now mentions the sites inventory assumes some smaller sites
      will consolidate to accommodate housing for lower income households and also lists
      recent developments that included consolidation. However, the element should list recent
      projects with consolidation by affordability and other characteristics to relate the recent
      trend to what is being assumed in the inventory. Alternatively, almost all smaller sites (less
      than half an acre) are proposed with the religious institutional overlay and the analysis
      could focus on these sites and their feasibility to accommodate housing for lower-income
      households.

      Large Sites: Appendix C identifies APN 7366-019-133 (21.1 acres) and APN 4067-013-
      001 (13.64 acres) toward mixed (includes 80 percent above moderate and 20 percent
      lower) and lower affordability levels, respectively. As noted in the prior review, sites larger
      than ten acres in size are deemed inadequate to accommodate housing for lower-income
      housing unless it is demonstrated that sites of equivalent size and affordability were
      successfully developed during the prior planning period or other evidence that
      demonstrates the adequacy of these sites to accommodate housing for lower-income
      households.

      Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: The element was revised to include the criteria used to
      identity sites with potential for redevelopment (pp. 119 to 120). However, as noted in the
      prior review, the element must support the factors with past redevelopment trends and
      relate the trends to the sites identified in the sites inventory for both residential and non-
      residential. For example, the element could relate the characteristics of recently entitled
      and constructed projects (Appendix E) to the criteria and characteristics of identified sites.
      In addition, the element lists several criteria used to identify nonvacant sites and then
      indicates sites do not meet all criteria – leaving no explanation of how the criteria were
      used (p. 119). Further, the inventory only lists two criteria, and some criteria have multiple
      values such as developer interest, similar to recent development or similar to sites in the
      corridor. In sum, the element does not have a clear indication of what criteria are met and
      whether the criteria are valid markers of redevelopment potential. To address this
      requirement, the element should clearly explain how the criteria were applied to sites (e.g.,
      meeting all criteria or not), support the validity of those criteria using recent experience in
      redevelopment and list the values of all those criteria in the inventory.

      In addition, the element lists several sites proposed with the religious institutional overlay
      zone but must also provide explanation of the extent existing uses impeded additional
      development. Currently, the inventory simply states there is opportunity for additional
      multifamily development and should add how there is opportunity for additional
      development such as expressed interest from the congregation or nonprofit developer,
      surplus land or parking space and potential for demolition and replacement of existing
      structure.

      For your information, as noted in the prior review, the housing element must demonstrate
      existing uses are not an impediment to additional residential development and will likely
      discontinue in the planning period. (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd. (g)(2).) Absent findings
      (e.g., adoption resolution) based on substantial evidence, the existing uses will be
      presumed to impede additional residential development and will not be utilized toward
      demonstrating adequate sites to accommodate the RHNA.

      City-owned Sites: The element was revised to indicate that there are several City-owned
      parcels in the Study Area, including parking lots in Downtown Torrance (which are
      included in the Sites Inventory). Additionally, the element indicates that there is a
      recommended action in the Downtown Plan stating, “under a request for bids, the City can
      prioritize affordable housing be part of a scoring system, with more points going to bids
      that included a minimum number of affordable units” (p. 136). However, as noted in the
      prior review, the element must include an analysis to demonstrate their suitability and
      availability in the planning period. Specifically, the analysis should address general plan
      designations, zoning, allowable densities, support for residential capacity assumptions,
      existing uses and any known conditions that preclude development in the planning period
      and the potential schedule for development. If zoning does not currently allow residential
      uses at appropriate densities, then the element must include programs to rezone sites
      pursuant to Government Code section 65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i).

      Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU): The element was revised to project 480 ADUs to be
      constructed over the planning period, averaging 68.7 units per year (p. 104). This
      projection was based on 33, 77, and 96 ADUs permitted for 2018, 2019, 2020,
      respectively (p. 104). However, HCD records indicate 15 ADUs for 2018, 37 ADUs for
      2019, 39 ADUs for 2020, and 87 ADUs for 2021. As noted in the prior review, the element
      should reconcile these figures.

      Environmental Constraints: The element now explains that an environmental review was
      prepared for the housing element that concludes the element will not have a significant
      adverse impact. However, the element should evaluate whether there are any known
      environmental or other constraints (e.g., title conditions, shape, contamination) that
      preclude or negatively development on identified sites in the planning period.

      Electronic Sites Inventory: For your information, as noted in the prior review, pursuant to
      Government Code section 65583.3, the City must submit an electronic sites inventory with
      its adopted housing element. The City must utilize standards, forms, and definitions
      adopted by HCD. Please see HCD’s housing element webpage at
      https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/index.shtml#element for
      a copy of the form and instructions.

   5. An analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints upon the maintenance,
      improvement, or development of housing for all income levels, including the types of
      housing identified in paragraph (1) of subdivision (c), and for persons with disabilities as
      identified in the analysis pursuant to paragraph (7), including land use controls... (Gov.

      Land Use Controls: The element was revised to indicate that a stakeholder meeting with
      developers, and architects revealed that existing development standards in the H-NT, H-
      PR, and H-MP Sub-Districts of the HBCSP were constraints to mixed-use development,
      including a 100-foot setback from Hawthorne Boulevard for residential portions of mixed-
      use projects, and a requirement for at least 30 percent of the floor area of the development
      be commercial (p.74). As a result, the element added Action 6 to Program 3 (Removal of
      Governmental Constraints). Action 6 indicates it will consider reducing setbacks, minimum
      percent of commercial use, maximum floor area ratio (FAR), add objective standards, and
      consider other standards identified as a constraint during the review process (p. 157).
      However, as noted in the prior review, the analysis should specifically address
      requirements related to heights, unit sizes and garage requirements for parking.

   6. An analysis of potential and actual nongovernmental constraints upon the maintenance,
      improvement, or development of housing for all income levels, including… …requests to

      subd. (a)(6).)

      Requests for Lower Density: As noted in the prior review, the element must analyze
      requests to develop housing at densities below those identified in the inventory.

   7. Analyze existing assisted housing developments that are eligible to change to non-low-

      As noted in the prior review, the element identified two developments that are at-risk of
      converting to market rate uses and as a result, must include a complete analysis. In
      response, the element generally states the owners have no intention of converting to
      market rate uses. However, regardless of intentions, the developments are still at-risk of
      conversion and given the crucial importance of preserving affordable housing stock, the
      element must still include an analysis, as described in the prior review, and add or modify
      programs as appropriate. Please see HCD’s prior review for additional information.

B. Housing Programs

   1. Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period with
      appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities to
      accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing need for
      each income level that could not be accommodated on sites identified in the inventory
      completed pursuant to paragraph (3) of subdivision (a) without rezoning, and to comply
      with the requirements of Government Code section 65584.09. Sites shall be identified as
      needed to facilitate and encourage the development of a variety of types of housing for all
      income levels, including multifamily rental housing, factory-built housing, mobilehomes,
      housing for agricultural employees, supportive housing, single-room occupancy units,
      emergency shelters, and transitional housing. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

      As noted in Finding A4, the element does not include a complete site analysis, therefore,
      the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the results of a complete
      sites inventory and analysis, the City may need to add or revise programs to address a
      shortfall of sites or zoning available to encourage a variety of housing types. In addition,
      the element should be revised as follows:

      Adequate Sites The element generally was not revised to address this requirement, as
      noted in the prior review for any program intended to address a shortfall of sites at
      appropriate densities to accommodate the RHNA for lower-income households, the
      program must meet all requirements pursuant to Government Code sections 65583.2,
      subdivisions (h) and (i). In addition, these programs should identify the shortfall, minimum
      acreage to be rezoned and anticipated allowable densities. Programs that may require
      revising include Programs 1 (Adequate Sites to Accommodate the RHNA, 7 (Religious
      institution Housing Overlay Zone) and 8 (Residential and Mixed-Use Corridors).

      Lot Consolidation: Program 3 (Removal of Governmental Constraints) includes Action 10
      Lot Consolidation. Action 10 was revised to include zoning code incentives (p. 158).
      Specifically, the program indicates it will consider adding incentives in the Zoning Code for
      development projects that result in lot consolidation for sites included in the Sites Inventory
      and/or for projects that include lower- and/or moderate-income units. However, as noted in
      the prior review, actions should be modified to be proactive and include discrete timing.
      The program should be revised as follows:

         •   Technical Assistance (Action 10B) should include commitment to proactive
             technical assistance (i.e., meetings with developers and property owners) with
             discrete timing (e.g., at least annually).
         •   Surveys (Action 10D) should revise the “conduct periodic, informal surveys” to
             include more discrete timing (e.g., every other year).

   2. The Housing Element shall contain programs which assist in the development of adequate
      housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and moderate-income
      households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

   5. Develop a plan that incentivizes and promotes the creation of accessory dwelling units that

      Program 6 ADUs: The program was revised to indicate the City will develop a program to
      promote ADU development within one year of the statutory deadline of the Housing
      Element. However, as noted in the prior review, the element should consider actions

      program should commit to more frequent monitoring of ADU production and affordability
      and timing (e.g., within 6 months) for when policies and efforts will be revised if ADUs are
      not permitted according to assumptions in the element.


C. Public Participation

   Local governments shall make a diligent effort to achieve public participation of all economic
   segments of the community in the development of the Housing Element, and the element
   shall describe this effort. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.(c)(9).)

   As noted in the prior review, the City must continue to proactively make future revisions
   available to the public, including any commenters, prior to submitting any revisions to HCD
   and diligently consider and address comments, including making revisions to the document
