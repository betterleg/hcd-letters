         RE: City of Rialto’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Rialto’s (City) housing element adopted
         March 8, 2022 and received for review on August 11th ,2022. Pursuant to Government
         Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing and
         Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. In addition, HCD
         considered comments from Inland Counties Legal services pursuant to Government
         Code section 65585, subdivision (c).

         The adopted housing element addresses many statutory requirements described in
         HCD’s June 3, 2022, review; however, additional revisions will be necessary to comply
         with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code), as follows:

             1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15
                (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
                assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
                (c)(10)(A).)

                  Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote
                  housing throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of
                  race, religion, sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status,
                  or disability, and other characteristics... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

                  Disproportionate Housing Needs including Displacement: The element was not
                  revised to address this finding. Please see HCDs prior review.

                  Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH) and Identified Sites: The element
                  was not revised to address this finding. Please see HCDs prior review.

      Local Data and Knowledge and Other Relevant Factors: While the element
      Notes feedback from stakeholders related to the need for community
      revitalization and affordable housing, it should utilize local data and knowledge
      and other relevant factors to complement the evaluation of patterns and trends
      related to fair housing issues. For example, the evaluation could discuss past
      zoning and land use practices (e.g., role of land use in patterns north of the 210
      and south of Foothill Boulevard), redlining, past investment and assessments
      such as capital improvement programs, the role of freeways or other major
      roadways, past redevelopment areas, information from code enforcement
      officers related to housing conditions and demographic trends (e.g., tenure,
      housing types).

      Goals, Actions, Metrics, and Milestones: As noted above, the element does not
      include a complete analysis of AFFH and based on the outcomes of a complete
      analysis, the element must add or modify programs as appropriate. In addition,
      programs generally must be revised with specific commitment, discrete
      timelines, geographic targeting and metrics or numeric targets related to AFFH
      outcomes. Finally, the element should consider additional actions related to
      placed based strategies for community revitalization, particularly in areas of
      high segregation and poverty and new housing choices and affordability in
      areas of relatively higher opportunity or income.

      Realistic Capacity: The element explains that the calculation of residential
      capacity is based on various factors such as net versus gross areas, land use
      controls and conservatively assumes 80 percent of maximum allowable density.
      However, as generally stated in prior reviews, the analysis lacks coherence.
      First, the net listed area is the same as the gross area. The element now states
      that those areas are the same but the calculation still only assumes 80 percent
      of the maximum allowable densities. However, the inventory appears to use
      maximum allowable densities. Second, the element lists three recent projects
      from Rialto and several others, mostly from nearby Fontana, despite the City
      apparently building, permitting or approving many other projects and then
      provides no information on zones or maximum allowable densities of these

      listed projects as requested in HCD’s prior review. Finally, the element does not
      contain any revisions to address the likelihood of 100 percent nonresidential
      development. To address this requirement, the element should (1) list all recent
      projects by zone and allowable densities and either support or rescale the 80

      Nonvacant Sites: HCD’s prior review found the element should relate recent
      experience to identified sites and demonstrate existing uses do not impede
      additional development such as improvement to land value ratios, existing
      versus allowable densities, age of structure, absences of investment, for sale
      signs, space vacancies and frequent property turnover. In response, the
      element basically only adds improvement to land value ratio and describes
      recent turnover on a few properties; however, the element must include
      analysis as found in HCD’s prior review. Please see HCD’s prior review.

      Specific Plans: HCD’s prior review found the element should sites in specific
      plans by specific plan districts, zoning districts or sub-districts utilized to
      implement the specific plan. The purpose is to better understand the allowable
      uses and densities. While the element now appears to list some sites by
      implementing zoning, it still should list sites for the Lytle Creek Ranch Specific
      Plan and Renaissance Specific Plan as described in HCD’s prior review.

      Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete site
      analysis, therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. The
      element may need to add or modify programs based on a complete analysis to
      demonstrate adequate sites to accommodate the regional housing need
      allocation (RHNA). In addition, the element should be revised as follows:

         •   Housing Program 2B (Rezone to Accommodate RHNA): The element
             was not revised to address this finding. Please See HCDs prior review.
         •   Program 2S (Surplus Land Act): HCD’s prior review found the City must
             go beyond actions to “consider” and must include a schedule of actions
             to facilitate development. In response, the Program now states the City
             may take some actions depending on circumstances. This does not
             constitute a schedule of actions toward development. The Program
             should include specific commitments and steps with discrete timelines
             toward development and should eliminate language that potentially leads
             to no action in the planning period (e.g., “if”, “anticipates”, “plans” and
             “may”.

      regional housing need in accordance with Government Code section 65584...
      (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(6).)

      development of housing... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

      Land Use Controls: The element generally was not revised to address HCD’s
      prior finding regarding minimum lot size and lot coverage for the R-3 zone.

      In addition, Program 4b (Remove Development Constraints) should go beyond
      reviewing parking standards and make specific commitment to reduce or revise
      parking requirements and ensure parking requirements do not constrain
      development, particularly for studio and one-bedroom units.

      Approval and Building Permit Timing: The element still must discuss the time
      between approval and building permit application to identify any hinderance on
      the development of housing and include programs as appropriate.

      Programs: As noted above, the element requires a complete analysis of
      potential governmental constraints. Depending upon the results of that analysis,
      the City may need to revise or add programs and address and remove or
      mitigate any identified constraints. In addition, the element should be revised as
      follows:

          •   Program 4B (Remove Development Constraints): The element was not
              revised to address this requirement. Please see HCD’s prior review for
              additional information.
          •   Program 5F (Housing for Persons with Developmental Disabilities): The
              City should remove “should constraints be found” language from the
              program as constraints have been identified for housing for persons with
              disabilities.

The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once
it has been revised and re-adopted to comply with the above requirements.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the City must continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information
regularly available while considering and incorporating comments where appropriate.
Please be aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local
government’s website and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have
previously requested notices relating to the local government’s housing element at
least seven days before submitting to HCD.

As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due October 15, 2021. As of
today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the City to revise the element as described above, adopt, and submit to
HCD to regain housing element compliance.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021),
as the City failed to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
statutory deadline (October 15, 2021), Programs 2B (Adequate Sites) and 2F (AB
1397 By Right Requirements) must be completed no later than one year from the
statutory deadline unless the City is found to comply with State Housing Element Law
prior to October 15, 2022. Otherwise, the local government’s housing element will no
longer comply with State Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of
substantial compliance pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i).

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual
reporting requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant
housing element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other
funding sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.
