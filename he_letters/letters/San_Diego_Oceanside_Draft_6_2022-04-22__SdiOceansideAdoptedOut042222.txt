         RE: City of Oceanside’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Oceanside’s adopted housing element, adopted on
         June 16, 2021 (City) and received for review on February 22, 2022. Pursuant to
         Government Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing
         and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

         The draft element addresses many statutory requirements from HCD’s June 2, 2021
         review; however, the following revisions will be necessary to comply with State Housing
         Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code). The element will meet the statutory
         requirements of State Housing Element Law once it has been revised and re-adopted to
         comply with the above requirements.

      Local Data and Knowledge and Other Relevant Factors: HCD’s prior review stated
      that the City has several census tracts considered as low resource. As such, the
      element must analyze this data for local trends, patterns, and local knowledge and
      conclude with a summary of issues. The element was revised to include several
      different data points regarding demographics. Additionally, the analysis illustrated
      that the City’s lower resourced census tracts also correspond with other fair housing
      issues including concentrations of minority and lower-income households, and
      households vulnerable to displacement. As stated in HCD’s prior review, the element
      must incorporate local knowledge and other relevant factors related to this analysis.
      For example, local data and knowledge is information obtained through community
      participation, consultation with stakeholders, and peoples lived experiences in your
      City and captures unique aspects about your community that is not usually reflective
      in state or federal data. Lastly, other relevant factors could include past changes in
      zoning and land use rules that have impacted segregation patterns; known past
      redlining activities; restrictive covenants; or any other discriminatory practices such
      as community opposition; lack of investment in certain communities; historical
      context and relevant demographics.

      Goals, Actions, Metrics, and Milestone: While the element included some revisions,
      it generally did not address HCD’s prior review. Specifically, as stated in HCD’s
      review, several programs did not include 1) metrics to measure program
      effectiveness 2) timelines for implementation 3) firm commitments and 4)
      demonstrate that actions directly respond to the City’s identified fair housing issues
      and meaningfully overcome contributing factors. Lastly, as stated in HCD’s prior
      review, programs must generally address enhancing housing mobility strategies,
      encourage development of new affordable housing in higher resourced areas,
      implement place-based strategies focused on specific census tracts to encourage
      community conservation and revitalization and address displacement risk. Most
      notably, the City has identified most of its lower-income Regional Housing Needs
      Allocation (RHNA) in census tracts experiencing several fair housing issues. The
      element must include strong place-based strategies that focus on revitalizing these
      communities. For example, the element referenced several local plans that could be
      incorporated to address place-based strategies including the Smart and Sustainable
      Corridors Specific Plan (SSCSP), Climate Action Plan, and Pedestrian and Bicycle
      Plan.

      For more information, please see HCD’s guidance at
      https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/affh/index.shtml.

      Progress in Meeting RHNA: The revised element now includes an additional
      category of projects that will be counted as credit towards RHNA. Specifically, the
      element is counting an additional 1,928 units for “projects under review.” With this
      revision, the element must demonstrate the availability of these units during the
      planning period.

      Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: The element was revised to include a list of projects
      and their existing uses, affordability, and size. However, this generally does not
      address HCD’s prior review. As stated in HCD’s prior review, while the element
      identified the existing uses for most of the sites in the inventory, the element must
      include additional analysis to demonstrate development potential during the planning
      period. For example, the element listed various factors that were considered when
      identifying sites likely to redevelop including degree of underutilization, improvement
      to land value (ILV), building age, underperforming uses, etc., First, the element
      should clearly detail the methodology utilized in identifying redevelopment potential.
      For example, the element should clarify which factors were used to identify sites and
      whether those factors were applied to all or only some sites. Second, the element
      could list the thresholds for each factor, where applicable such as for building age,
      ILV, and FAR. Third, the element must support the methodology with development
      trends or other relevant information. To better relate example projects to the
      methodology, the element could describe the various site characteristics of these
      and other projects such as ILV, building age, FAR, and other relevant
      characteristics.

      For your information, while the housing element does not currently rely on nonvacant
      sites to accommodate more than 50 percent of the RHNA for lower-income
      households, if the housing element is revised to increase their reliance on nonvacant
      sites, the element must demonstrate existing uses are not an impediment to
      additional residential development and will likely discontinue in the planning period
      (Gov. Code, § 65583.2, subd. (g)(2).). Absent findings (e.g., adoption resolution)
      based on substantial evidence, the existing uses will be presumed to impede
      additional residential development and will not be utilized toward demonstrating
      adequate sites to accommodate the (RHNA).

      While the element was revised to include three project examples for above moderate
      development on vacant smaller sites that utilized lot consolidation, this generally
      does not address the finding. Specifically, the element should include additional
      examples that reflect the sites in the inventory and the methodology discussed. For
      example, the element stated that smaller sites and lot consolidation is likely due to
      contiguous parcels with similar redevelopment potential, and commercial blocks with
      marginal uses. To better support the methodology, the element should include
      additional examples with similar site characteristics. Lastly, the City should consider
      adding or modifying programs to include incentives for lot consolidation.

      Electronic Sites Inventory: For your information, pursuant to Government Code
      section 65583.3, the City must submit an electronic sites inventory with its adopted
      housing element. The City must utilize standards, forms, and definitions adopted by
      HCD. Please see HCD’s housing element webpage at
      https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-
      element/index.shtml#element for a copy of the form and instructions. The City can
      reach out to HCD at sitesinventory@hcd.ca.gov for technical assistance.

      Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete analysis;
      therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the
      results of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City may need to add or
      revise programs.

      Program 5 – Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs): The element was revised to reduce
      the number of projected ADUs to accommodate the City’s RHNA. However, as
      stated in HCD’s review, the element must commit to more frequent monitoring. This
      program should be revised to monitor ADU trends biennial and include a
      commitment to adopt alternative measures such as rezoning or amending the
      element within a specific time (e.g., six months) if ADU assumptions for the number
      of units and affordability are not met. Additionally, while the element included a
      commitment to promote ADUs through outreach and brochures, the City could
      consider additional actions to further incentivize and promote ADUs. Examples
      include exploring and pursuing funding, modifying development standards and
      reducing fees beyond state law, stronger community awareness programs, pre-
      approved plans and homeowner/applicant assistance tools.

      Programs should be added or modified as appropriate to address this constraint.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021),
as the City failed to adopt a housing element that was found in compliance within 120
days of the statutory deadline (April 15, 2021), Program 7 (Adequate Residential Sites
to Accommodate the RHNA and No Net Loss) to rezone 1,015 units to accommodate
the regional housing needs allocation must be completed no later than one year from
the statutory deadline. As that timeframe has passed, and in accordance with
Government Code Section 65588, subsection (e)(4)(C)(iii) HCD cannot find the housing
element in substantial compliance with housing element law until the rezones are
completed.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. During the housing element revision
process, the City must continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
available while considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be
aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local government’s website
and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested
notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
submitting to HCD.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing element
adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes the
opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical Advisories
issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html
