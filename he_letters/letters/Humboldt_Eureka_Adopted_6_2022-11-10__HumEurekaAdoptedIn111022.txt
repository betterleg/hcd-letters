         RE: City of Eureka’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Eureka’s (City) housing element that was adopted
         October 18, 2022 and received for review on November 3, 2022. Pursuant to
         Government Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of Housing
         and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

         HCD is pleased to find the adopted housing element in full compliance with State
         Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code). The adopted element was found
         to be substantially the same as the revised draft element that HCD’s August 10, 2022
         review determined met statutory requirements.

         As noted in HCD’s August 10, 2022 findings letter, while the element analyzes the City’s
         history of developing residential uses on small sites, the analysis does not fully
         demonstrate the suitability of small sites for the development of housing affordable to
         lower-income households. Therefore, HCD does not consider Sites 22, 23, 24, 25, 26,
         29, 30, 34, and 56 (Table 63, Vacant Sites) as adequate sites to accommodate any of
         the City’s RHNA for lower-income households and these sites should not be utilized for
         the purposes of No Net Loss Law. (Gov. Code, § 65863.)

         The City must continue timely and effective implementation of all programs including but
         not limited to the following:

             •    IMP H-3 (Revision of Specific Development Regulations): This program commits
                  to obtain Coastal Commission Certification of the City’s updated Local Coastal
                  Program in 2023, and comprehensively update the City’s subdivision and historic
                  preservation regulations in 2024.
             •    IMP H-5 (Process Improvement Action Plan): This program commits to conduct a
                  multi-department customer service survey, propose modifications and workflow
                  process improvements to City Council, and revise processes that restrain or
                  impede housing development in 2022.

   •   IMP H-34 (Affordable Housing on City-owned Properties): This program commits
       to issue Requests for Proposal for the development of affordable housing on all
       City-owned sites in the site inventory within 6 months of HCD’s certification of the
       housing element.

The City must monitor and report on the results of this and other programs through the
annual progress report, required pursuant to Government Code section 65400. Please
be aware, Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i) grants HCD authority to
review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines is
inconsistent with an adopted housing element or housing element law. This includes
failure to implement program actions included in the housing element. HCD may revoke
housing element compliance if the local government’s actions do not comply with state
law.

As a reminder, all City-owned sites are subject to the Surplus Lands Act (SLA). (Gov.
Code § 54221.) On February 24, 2022, HCD issued a letter to the City regarding the
SLA’s implications on a proposed land swap. Among other things, the letter identified
actions required to comply with the SLA. The sites proposed for acquisition are now
identified in the housing element as City Sites 6-9 (APNs 008-052-024, -025, -026, and -
027). The City must use these sites for the development of very low and low-income
housing. If the City elects to dispose of City-owned sites identified in the housing
element for the development of affordable housing as opposed to developing the
affordable housing itself, the City must follow the standard SLA process or seek an
affordable housing exemption under Government Code section 54221, subdivision
(f)(1). Questions on SLA requirements should be directed to PublicLands@hcd.ca.gov.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City now meets housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to assist. For information, please see the Technical Advisories issued by
the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
https://www.opr.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.
