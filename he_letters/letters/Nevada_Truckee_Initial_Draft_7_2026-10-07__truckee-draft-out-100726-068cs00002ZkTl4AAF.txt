         however, revisions will be necessary to comply with State Housing Element Law (Gov.

         government’s website and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have
         previously requested notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least
         seven days before submitting to HCD.

                                        APPENDIX
                                     TOWN OF TRUCKEE

The following changes are necessary to bring the Town’s housing element into compliance
with Article 10.6 of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite
the supporting section of the Government Code.

Housing element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
https://www.hcd.ca.gov/planning-and-community-development/hcd-memos. Among other
resources, the housing element section contains HCD’s latest technical assistance tool,
Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks), available at
https://www.hcd.ca.gov/planning-and-community-development/housing-elements/building-
blocks and includes the Government Code addressing State Housing Element Law and other
resources.


A. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

    1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
       Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
       the jurisdiction (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A))

       Identified Sites and Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH): The element
       discusses the percent of sites identified in low, moderate, and higher resource tracts
       including an evaluation of identified sites by income group (e.g., lower, moderate and
       above-moderate income) and geography relative to fair housing conditions (e.g., race,
       disability, income, poverty, overpayment). While this analysis addresses statutory
       requirements, it must be updated based on the latest sites inventory.

       Local Data and Knowledge: The element includes various information such as historical
       settlement and land use patterns, short term rental and planned investments in
       infrastructure. However, the element should supplement this information with additional
       local data and knowledge and other relevant factors to better describe disparities in
       access to opportunities between neighborhoods. For example, the element should
       describe any differences or lack of differences in terms of access, condition and quality
       between neighborhoods related to school performance, accessibility improvements,
       sidewalks, streetscapes, safe routes to school, active transportation, parks, healthy
       foods, community amenities and recreational programs.

       Contributing Factors to Fair Housing Issues: Based on the outcome of a complete
       analysis, the element should reassess and prioritize contributing factors to fair housing
       issues and then formulate appropriate policies and programs.

    2. Include…. (A) analysis of any special housing needs, such as those of… (B) the
       housing needs of acutely and extremely low income households. (Gov. Code, § 65583,
       subd. (a)(7).)

       Acutely Low-Income (ALI) and Extremely Low-Income (ELI) Households: The element
       collectively addresses the housing needs of ALI and ELI households. While these
       income groups may have similarities, the element should distinguish the housing needs
       of ALI households to better formulate appropriate policies and programs. For example,
       the element could quantify the number of households by tenure and income range
       utilizing Table HA-11, differences in affordability gaps utilizing Table HA-22, differences
       in housing situations based on local knowledge and service providers and unique
       housing strategies. Based on the outcomes of a complete analysis, the element should
       formulate appropriate policies and programs.

    3. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including vacant
       sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for redevelopment during the
       planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for a designated income level, and
       an analysis of the relationship of zoning and public facilities and services to these sites.
       (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

       Progress in toward the Regional Housing Need Allocation (RHNA): As you know, the
       Town’s RHNA may be reduced by the number of new units pending, approved,
       permitted or built (pipeline projects) after June 30, 2027, by demonstrating affordability
       and availability in the planning period. In several cases; the element describes that
       pipeline projects are requesting building permits or anticipated to start construction in
       2026. In these cases where construction appears to be nearing completion before June
       30, 2027, the element should either remove the units from being counted toward the 7th
       cycle RHNA or describe how the units will be reported as completed after June 30,
       2027.

       Suitability of Nonvacant Sites: The element must demonstrate the potential for
       redevelopment on nonvacant sites by addressing various factors such as past
       experiences converting existing uses to residential development, current market
       demand for the existing use, whether any leases or conditions would preclude additional
       residential development. While in several cases the element includes adequate
       analysis, it should be revised, as follows:

           •   Site 4 (Tahoe Forest Hospital Gateway): The element must address the need for
               replacement parking, whether replacement parking precludes additional
               development and the likelihood of residential development given the site allows
               100 percent non-residential uses.
           •   Site 5 (Hospital Site): The element must address the need for replacement
               parking and whether replacement parking precludes additional development.
           •   Sites 7 (Arnold DRH) and 8 (Arnold DMU): The element should discuss whether
               remediation is likely and any impacts on development within the planning period.
           •   Site 10 (Hilltop – JAR Hilltop North): The element must address the need for
               replacement parking and whether replacement parking precludes additional
               development.
           •   Site 17 (Sutton Site): The element must demonstrate how the use will likely
               discontinue or the site will otherwise be redeveloped in the planning period such

       Electronic Sites Inventory: Pursuant to Government Code section 65583.3, subdivision
       (b), the Town must utilize standards, forms, and definitions adopted by HCD when
       preparing the sites inventory and submit an electronic version of the sites inventory.

    4. An analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints upon the maintenance,
       improvement, or development of housing for all income levels, including… land use
       controls… …site improvements... historic preservation practices and policies and an
       assessment of how existing and proposed historic designations affect the locality’s
       ability to meet its share of the housing need…(Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(5).)

       Land Use Controls (Parking): The element discusses parking requirements for studios
       including requiring garages for every unit and 1.85 parking spaces per efficiency unit
       (p. HB-22). These requirements are generally considered a constraint on development.
       The element discusses implementation of AB 2097 (2022) related to minimum parking
       requirements. However, these provisions are limited to specific areas and properties
       and does not encompass all the Town. The element should add or modify programs to
       address parking requirements for studios (garage and number of spaces) as a
       constraint. For example, a program could commit to remove the garage requirement
       and reduce the number of spaces to one space per studio.

       On/Off-Site Improvements: The element identifies typical off-site improvements;
       however, it must also analyze their impact on total development costs.

       Historical Preservation: The element must describe historical preservation practices and
       policies and evaluate how existing and proposed historic designations may influence the
       locality’s ability to meet the RHNA. For example, the analysis could identify how
       preservation-related procedures, active uses, and design review requirements can
       constrain redevelopment potential on identified sites or impact the cost, supply and
       timing of housing.

       Disclosure Statement: The element must include a potential and actual governmental
       constraints disclosure statement containing:

          •   An identification of each new or amended potential or actual governmental
              constraint, or revision increasing the stringency of a governmental constraint,
              adopted after the due date of the previous housing element and before
              submitting the current draft housing element to the department; and
          •   An identification of any new or amended potential or actual governmental
              constraint, or revision increasing the stringency of a governmental constraint, that
              the Town anticipates adopting during the first three years of the planning period
              commencing on the date the housing element is considered to be in substantial
              compliance.

B. Housing Programs

    1. Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period with
       appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities to
       accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing need
       for each income level… … and encourage the development of a variety of types of
       housing for all income levels... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

       As noted in Finding A3, the element does not include a complete site analysis;
       therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the results
       of a complete sites inventory and analysis; the Town may need to add or revise
       programs to address a shortfall of sites or zoning available to encourage a variety of
       housing types.

       In addition, Program H-2.G (Publicly Owned Land) identifies land owned by Tahoe-
       Truckee Unified School District (TTUSD) to accommodate a portion of the RHNA. The
       Program commits to support project approval and construction through 2037. However,
       the Program should commit to support construction during the planning period,
       completing construction by at least 2035.

    2. Assist in the development of adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low, very
       low, low-, and moderate-income households... …(B)… …the program shall also assist
       in the development of adequate housing to meet the needs of acutely low income
       households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

       Program H-3.D (Shelter and Housing Solutions for Persons Experiencing Homelessness):
       This Program should be revised with discrete timing on when or how often actions will be
       implemented (e.g., at least annually).

       Acutely Low-Income Households: As noted in Finding A2, the element must include
       programs, as appropriate, based on a complete analysis of ALI households.

       Employee Housing: As noted throughout the element, the Town has a distinct need for
       employee and workforce housing. While the element includes Program H-2.H (Deed-
       Restricted Housing for Truckee’s Workforce), it should consider additional actions such
       as regional collaboration, coordination with major employers and industries and
       pursuing actions to assist in the development and conservation of housing.

    3. Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
       nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and development of
       housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for persons with
       disabilities. The program shall remove constraints to, and provide reasonable
       accommodations for housing designed for, intended for occupancy by, or with
       supportive services for, persons with disabilities. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(3).)

       As noted in Finding A4, the element requires a complete analysis of potential
       governmental constraints. Depending upon the results of that analysis, the Town may
       need to revise or add programs and address and remove or mitigate any identified
       constraints. In addition, the element should be revised, as follows:

           •   Parking: As noted in Finding A4, the element should add or modify programs to
               address parking requirements for studios (garage and number of spaces) as a
               constraint.
           •   Program H-2.C (Inclusionary Housing Ordinance): In addition to targeted
               outreach to lower-income households and stakeholders, Program H-2.C should
               explicitly seek input from development community as part of outreach efforts.

    4. Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
       throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race, religion,
       sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or disability, and other
       characteristics... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

       The element must be revised to add or modify goals and actions based on the
       outcomes of a complete analysis as noted in Finding A1. Goals and actions must
       specifically respond to the analysis and to the identified and prioritized contributing
       factors to fair housing issues and must be significant and meaningful enough to
       overcome identified patterns and trends. Actions must have specific commitment,
       geographic targeting, metrics and milestones and, as appropriate, must address
       housing mobility enhancement, new housing choices and affordability in relatively
       higher opportunity and income areas, place-based strategies toward community
       revitalization and displacement protection.

       In addition to adding or modifying programs based on a complete analysis, the element
       should expand housing mobility actions and place-based strategies toward community
       revitalization. Examples of housing mobility actions include targeting zoning and
       incentives to promote housing choices and affordability in relatively higher income and
       low-density areas. Examples of place-based strategies toward community revitalization
       include improving neighborhood and community conditions such as community amenities
       and programs, parks, housing conditions, active transportation, infrastructure and safe
       routes to school. For more information, please see HCD’s guidance (starting on p. 72) at:
       https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/affh/docs/AFFH_Document_Final_4-27-
       2021.pdf.

       Finally, programs should be revised, as follows:

               commit to geographic targeting such as relatively higher income areas.
           •   Program H-2.E (Local Funding Sources): The Program should commit to
               geographic targeting such as relatively higher income areas.

C. Quantified Objectives

    A statement of the community’s goals, quantified objectives, and policies relative to
    affirmatively furthering fair housing and to the maintenance, preservation, improvement,
    and development of housing… … The quantified objectives shall establish the maximum
    number of housing units by income category that can be constructed, rehabilitated, and
    conserved over a five-year time period. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (b))

    While the element includes quantified objectives for most income categories, it should also
    include rehabilitation and conservation objectives for acutely low, extremely-low and very low-
    income households.
