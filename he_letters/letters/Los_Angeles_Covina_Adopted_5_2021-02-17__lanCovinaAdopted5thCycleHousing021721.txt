         RE: Review of Covina’s 5th Cycle (2013-2021) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Covina’s (City) housing element adopted on
         November 17, 2020 and received for review on November 23, 2020. Pursuant to
         Government Code section 65585, subdivision (h), the California Department of
         Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

         The adopted element meets the requirements described in HCD’s July 30, 2020
         review. For example, the City has now adopted zoning to permit emergency
         shelters without discretionary action in addition to several other zoning
         amendments to facilitate a variety of housing types. However, the City did not adopt
         its housing element within 120 days of the statutory due date (October 15, 2013)
         and missed the due date for the four-year update (October 15, 2017). Therefore,
         pursuant to Government Code section 65588, subdivision (e), prior to obtaining
         housing element compliance, Covina must adopt a compliant four-year update.

         Specifically, Government Code section 65588, subdivision (e)(4), requires a
         jurisdiction that failed to adopt its housing element within 120 calendar days from
         the statutory due date to revise its element every four years until adopting at least
         two consecutive revisions by the applicable due dates. The City did not meet this
         requirement; therefore, it is subject to the four-year revision requirement.

         The four-year update requires, among other things, standalone public outreach and
         participation, separate from the public outreach conducted for the initial 5th cycle
         housing element. The element must also demonstrate meaningful progress in
         implementation since adoption of the initial 5th cycle housing element.

         In addition, the element contains Program 4.6 that, among other items, commits to
         review the Town Center Specific Plan’s (TCSP) development standards and site
         plan review procedures. Program 4.6 commits to amend the standards and

procedure “if they are found to unreasonably… limit the ability to achieve maximum
densities or impact the cost and supply of housing”. As noted in HCD’s prior review,
these standards and procedure are constraints on housing. As a result, Program
4.6 must be implemented immediately to address the constraints regardless of
future evaluation in 2021. The City should report on the progress in implementation
of Program 4.6, as described above, pursuant to Gov. Code section 65400.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the City must engage the community, including individuals and
organizations that represent lower-income and special needs households, by
making information regularly available and considering and incorporating comments
where appropriate.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate
Bill (SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities program and ongoing SB 2
funding (Permanent Local Housing Allocation) consider housing element
compliance and/or annual reporting requirements pursuant to Government Code
section 65400. Without a compliant housing element, the City does not meet
housing element requirements for these and other funding sources.

HCD is committed to assist the City in addressing all statutory requirements of
housing element law. If you have any questions or need additional technical
assistance, please contact DC Navarrette, of our staff, at
david.navarrette@hcd.ca.gov.
