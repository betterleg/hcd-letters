         RE: City of Livingston’s 6th Cycle (2024-2032) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Livingston’s (City) revised draft housing element
         received for review on March 14, 2025. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585,
         the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) is reporting
         the results of its review.

         The revised draft element addresses many statutory requirements; however, revisions
         will be necessary to comply with State Housing Element Law (Gov. Code, § 65580 et
         seq), as follows:

              1. Review the previous element to evaluate the appropriateness, effectiveness, and
                 progress in implementation, and reflect the results of this review in the revised
                 element. (Gov. Code, § 65588 (a) and (b).)

                  General: While the element includes some information on the progress in
                  implementation from the previous element (Table E1-2), it must still evaluate the
                  effectiveness of programs and reflect the results in the current element. For
                  example, the element should evaluate the effectiveness and progress for
                  Programs 9 (Extremely Low-Income (ELI) Housing Development) and 10
                  (Farmworker Housing).

                  Special Housing Needs: As part of the evaluation of programs in the past cycle,
                  the element must still provide an explanation of the cumulative effectiveness of
                  goals, policies, and related actions in meeting the housing needs of special
                  needs populations (e.g., elderly, persons with disabilities, large households,
                  female-headed households, farmworkers, and persons experiencing
                  homelessness). Programs should be revised as appropriate to reflect the results
                  of this evaluation.

   2. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15
      (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
      assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
      (c)(10)(A).)

      Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
      throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,
      religion, sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or
      disability, and other characteristics... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

      Local Data and Knowledge and Other Relevant Factors: While the element
      includes some brief information on residential and childcare facilities and
      historical land use in the City, it must still incorporate local data and knowledge
      and other relevant factors to better understand disparities in access to
      opportunities. For example, the element should discuss the industrial uses to the
      north relative to residential uses or mention the influence of infrastructure such
      as highways and irrigation canals on land use patterns. Please see HCD’s prior
      review for additional information. HCD will also send its guide to local data and
      knowledge to assist with this analysis.

      Contributing Factors to Fair Housing Issues: The element should re-assess and
      prioritize contributing factors to fair housing issues upon completion of analysis
      and make revisions as appropriate.

      Programs: The element must be revised to add or modify goals and actions
      based on the outcomes of a complete affirmatively further fair housing (AFFH)
      analysis. Please see HCD’s prior review for additional information regarding
      program requirements.

      In addition, while the element includes many meaningful actions to AFFH, it
      should expand and improve housing mobility actions and place-based strategies
      toward community revitalization. For example, the element should commit to
      applying for funding (beyond prioritizing the capital improvement program) at
      least every other year toward broader community development issues such as
      neighborhood revitalization, infrastructure, streetscapes, safe routes to school,
      active transportation, public space, community facilities and pollution burden.
      Examples of housing mobility actions include promoting more housing choices
      and affordability in lower-density areas, religious institutional sites, city-owned
      sites, prioritizing funding, facilitating affordability and enhancing accessory
      dwelling units (ADU), junior accessory dwelling units (JADU) or additional
      conversion of existing single-family space, and home sharing strategies.

      redevelopment during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for
      a designated income level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and

      Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning

      Progress in Meeting the RHNA: To demonstrate the availability of pending
      projects in the planning period, the element must still address the affordability
      and availability of these units in the planning period. Specifically, the element
      must demonstrate affordability to moderate-income households based on actual
      or anticipated rents or sales prices. For availability, the element must discuss
      status, remaining steps to completion and any known barriers to development in
      the planning period.

      Electronic Site Inventory: Pursuant to Government Code section 65583.3,
      subdivision (b), the City must utilize standards, forms, and definitions adopted by
      HCD when preparing the sites inventory and submit an electronic version of the
      sites inventory. While the City has submitted an electronic version of the sites
      inventory, if changes occur, any future adopted versions of the element must also
      submit the electronic version of the sites inventory.

      Programs: As noted above, the element does not include a complete site
      analysis, therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established.
      Depending on the results of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City
      may need to add or revise programs to address a shortfall of sites or zoning
      available to encourage a variety of housing types.

      In addition, as noted on page E6-3, the element should modify Program 2
      (Adequate Sites for RHNA) to monitor pending projects and, if necessary, identify
      alternative sites by a specified date.

      Address and, where appropriate and legally possible, remove governmental and
      nongovernmental constraints to the maintenance, improvement, and
      development of housing, including housing for all income levels and housing for
      persons with disabilities. The program shall remove constraints to, and provide
      reasonable accommodations for housing designed for, intended for occupancy
      by, or with supportive services for, persons with disabilities. (Gov. Code, §
      65583, subd. (c)(3).)

      Constraints on Housing for Persons with Disabilities: While the element lists
      decision-making criteria for considering a reasonable accommodation request, it
      should also analyze decision-making criteria, such as “Potential impact on
      surrounding uses”. The analysis should address timing, cost, approval certainty
      and other impacts on housing for persons with disabilities and programs should
      be added or modified to address identified constraints.

      Program 6 (Zoning Ordinance Amendments): The Program commits to allow
      multifamily by-right in the R-2 and Downtown Commercial (DTC) zones to
      “minimize” the constraint of a conditional use permit (CUP) for multifamily
      development larger than 25 units. But, this commitment does not remove the
      CUP requirement. Further, this commitment does not address the constraint in
      the R-3 zone. Instead, the Program should commit to remove or replace the CUP
      requirement for multifamily development in the R-2, DTC and R-3 zones by a
      specified date.

   5. The Housing Element shall describe the means by which consistency will be
      achieved with other general plan elements and community goals. (Gov. Code, §
      65583, subd. (c)(7).)

      The element was not revised to address this finding. Please see HCD’s
      September 30, 2024 review for additional information.


The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once
rezones have been completed to meet all requirements (see below) and the element
has been revised, adopted, submitted to, and reviewed by HCD to comply with the
above requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65585.

As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due January 31, 2024. As of
today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the City to revise the element as described above, adopt, and submit to
HCD to regain housing element compliance.

For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if
a local government does not adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the
statutory deadline (January 31, 2024), then any rezoning to make prior identified sites
available or accommodate the RHNA pursuant to Government Code sections 65583,
subdivision (c) and 65583.2, subdivision (c), shall be completed no later than one year
from the statutory deadline. As this year has passed and Programs 2 (Adequate Sites
for RHNA) and 3 (By-Right Approval) have not been completed, the housing element
will remain out of compliance until all necessary rezoning has been completed.

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the City should continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower-income and special needs households, by making information regularly
available and considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Please be
aware, any revisions to the element must be posted on the local government’s website
and to email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested
notices relating to the local government’s housing element at least seven days before
submitting to HCD.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Land Use and Climate Innovation at:
https://www.lci.ca.gov/planning/general-plan/guidelines.html.

HCD appreciates the coordination and hard work of the housing element update team to
prepare the housing element as part of the multi-jurisdictional housing element process.
We are committed to assisting the City in addressing all statutory requirements of State
Housing Element Law. If you have any questions or need technical assistance, please
contact Andrea Grant, of our staff, at andrea.grant@hcd.ca.gov.
