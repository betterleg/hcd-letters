         Dear Kenneth Phung:

         RE: City of Perris 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Perris’s adopted housing element, adopted
         January 25, 2022 and received for review January 28, 2022. The California Department
         of Housing and Community Development (HCD) received the adopted housing element
         while the City’s revised draft (received January 20, 2022) was under review. As a result,
         HCD will discontinue its 60-day review of the draft housing element and reports the
         results of the review of the adopted element, pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, subdivision (h). Our review was facilitated by a telephone conversation on
         March 21, 2022 with you, Nathan Perez, and Michele Ogawa and the City’s consultants
         Sarah Walker and Cynthia Mejia from National Core.

         The adopted housing element addresses most statutory requirements described in
         HCD’s December 3, 2021 letter; however, additional revisions are necessary to fully
         comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code), see enclosed
         Appendix. The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element
         Law once it has been revised and re-adopted to comply with the above requirements.

         For your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021),
         as the City failed to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of the statutory
         deadline (October 15, 2021), Program 2.4 (Ensure Adequate Sites to Accommodate the
         regional housing needs allocation (RHNA)) to rezone 228 units to accommodate the
         RHNA must be completed no later than one year from the statutory deadline.
         Otherwise, the local government’s housing element will no longer comply with State
         Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial compliance
         pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i).

Public participation in the development, adoption and implementation of the housing
element is essential to effective housing planning. Throughout the housing element
process, the City should continue to engage the community, including organizations that
represent lower- income and special needs households, by making information regularly
available and considering and incorporating comments where appropriate. Additionally,
pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (b) (AB 215, Statutes of
2021), any subsequent draft revision, the local government must post the draft revisions
on its website and email a link to all individuals and organizations that have previous
requested notices related to local governments housing element at least seven days
before resubmitting to HCD.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. HCD reminds the City to consider timing provisions and welcomes
the opportunity to provide assistance. For information, please see the Technical
Advisories issued by the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
http://opr.ca.gov/docs/OPR_Appendix_C_final.pdf and
http://opr.ca.gov/docs/Final_6.26.15.pdf.

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City meets housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

We are committed to assisting the City in addressing all statutory requirements of State
Housing Element Law. If you have any questions or need additional technical
assistance, please contact Tristan Lanza, of our staff, at Tristan.lanza@hcd.ca.gov.

                                          APPENDIX
                                       CITY OF PERRIS

The following changes are necessary to bring the City’s housing element into compliance with
Article 10.6 of the Government Code. Accompanying each recommended change, we cite the
supporting section of the Government Code.

Housing element technical assistance information is available on HCD’s website at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/housing-element-memos.shtml.
Among other resources, the housing element section contains HCD’s latest technical assistance
tool, Building Blocks for Effective Housing Elements (Building Blocks), available at
http://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/building-blocks/index.shtml and includes the
Government Code addressing State Housing Element Law and other resources.


   A. Housing Needs, Resources, and Constraints

      1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15 (commencing with
         Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an assessment of fair housing in
         the jurisdiction (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(10)(A))

         Fair Housing Issues and Contributing Factors: HCD’s prior review stated that the
         element included a table of fair housing issues (Table 5-22); however the table listed
         programmatic requirements, not fair housing issues. The element was not revised to
         address this finding. The element must clearly identify and list fair housing issues.
         Additionally, the element was revised to include a discussion of three contributing
         factors; however Table 5-22 listed contributing factors that were not discussed and is
         inconsistent with the element. For example, the element indicated that that an
         abundance of aging single-family housing is a contributing factor to a non-specified fair
         housing issue. However, Table 5-22 which lists all contributing factors does not include
         aging single-family homes. The element must clearly list the fair housing issue, the
         factor that contributed to the issue, and programs and actions to address the issue.

         Metrics and Milestones: HCD’s prior review stated that all programs addressing fair
         housing issues must include metrics to measure program effectiveness and milestones
         to target meaningful outcomes in the planning period. While some programs were
         modified, the revised element did not meet this finding. Several programs identified as
         addressing fair housing issues (Table 5-22) did not include metrics including Action
         2.10, 3.3, 3.5, 3.3, 3.5, 3.7, 4.2, 4.4, 4.7, 4.8 4.10, and 4.11. The element must be
         revised to include metrics that measure the effectiveness of actions listed for programs
         addressing fair housing issues.

         Strategies and Actions: HCD’s prior review stated that the element must 1) include
         actions and strategies that seek to overcome contributing factors to fair housing issues
         and 2) programs address housing mobility, displacement risks, promoting new housing
         opportunities in areas of high opportunity and implementing place-based strategies to
         encourage community preservation and revitalization. While the element includes

      2. An inventory of land suitable and available for residential development, including
         vacant sites and sites having realistic and demonstrated potential for redevelopment
         during the planning period to meet the locality’s housing need for a designated income
         level, and an analysis of the relationship of zoning and public facilities and services to
         these sites. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (a)(3).)

         The City has a regional housing needs allocation (RHNA) of 7,805 housing units, of
         which 3,157 are for lower-income households. To address this need, the element relies
         on vacant sites, including sites in Specific Plan Areas and zoned as mixed-use.
         Additionally, to accommodate a shortfall, the City will be implementing a housing
         overlay zone. To demonstrate the adequacy of these sites and strategies to
         accommodate the City’s RHNA, the element must include complete analyses:

         Realistic Capacity: HCD’s prior review stated that the element needed to provide
         support for realistic capacity assumptions in the inventory. The revised element
         reduced to the capacity assumptions from 85-100 percent of maximum allowable
         density to 80 percent of max densities on vacant sites and 50 percent on nonresidential
         zoned sites. Additionally, while the revised element included a few examples of recent
         developments and discussion of regional tax credit projects, to demonstrate that the
         City develops at the densities assumed in the inventory (80 percent of maximum
         allowable densities), the element should include clear supporting information on typical
         built densities of existing or approved residential developments at a similar affordability
         level. For example, the element included Table 7-4 on page 208 with four affordable
         housing projects and density, but did equate those densities to the allowable densities
         for those zones to support the 80 percent assumption. To relate these examples to the
         analysis, the element could identify the zone, allowable density, level of affordability,
         and presence of exceptions such as a density bonus.

         Large Sites: The element identifies several large sites ranging from 13-36 acres for
         lower income households. While the revised element included information regarding

      3. An analysis of potential and actual governmental constraints upon the maintenance,
         improvement, or development of housing for all income levels, including the types of

         Land-Use Controls: HCD’s prior review stated that the element must 1) analyze land-

         included a program to amend minimum lot size for specific plans, it did not include a
         cumulative analysis of land use controls. Please see HCD’s prior review for additional
         information.

         care facilities of seven or more subject to the same restrictions as other residential
         uses. This does not address the finding. HCD’s prior review stated that the City does
         not allow residential care facilities for six or fewer and seven or more in several zones
         that allow residential uses such as in specific plan areas and high density zones
         (pp. 162-163) which can constrain the availability of housing choices for persons with

      4. An analysis of potential and actual nongovernmental constraints upon the

             element also included the time it takes for building permit approvals. However, this
             does not address the finding. The element must identify the length of time between
             project approval and request for a building permit.

   B. Housing Programs

      1. Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning period
         with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and facilities to
         accommodate that portion of the city’s or county’s share of the regional housing need
         for each income level that could not be accommodated on sites identified in the
         inventory completed pursuant to paragraph (3) of subdivision (a) without rezoning, and
         to comply with the requirements of Government Code section 65584.09. Sites shall be
         identified as needed to facilitate and encourage the development of a variety of types
         of housing for all income levels, including multifamily rental housing, factory-built
         housing, mobilehomes, housing for agricultural employees, supportive housing, single-
         room occupancy units, emergency shelters, and transitional housing.
         (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

         As noted in Finding A2, the element does not include a complete site analysis,
         therefore, the adequacy of sites and zoning were not established. Based on the results
         of a complete sites inventory and analysis, the City may need to add or revise
         programs to address a shortfall of sites or zoning available to encourage a variety of
         housing types. In addition, the element should be revised as follows:

         Shortfall of Sites: The element acknowledges that the City has identified several sites
         that does not currently have zoning in place to permit densities appropriate for the
         lower-income RHNA. While the element included Program 2.4 (Ensure Adequate Sites
         to Accommodate the RHNA) to commit to rezoning through the implementation of an
         overlay zone, the element did not identify which sites will be rezoned and what the total
         shortfall is by income category. The element should clearly indicate the total number of
         shortfall units by income category.

         Proposed Housing Overlay: HCD’s prior review stated that the element should include
         a program to commit to sufficient incentives to assure the intended residential uses and
         affordability. While the revised element included Program 2.4 (Ensure Adequate Sites
         to Accommodate The RHNA) committing to meeting with landowners and developing a
         menu of incentives, the element did not include a timeline for completing each of these
         actions.

         Previously Identified Nonvacant and Vacant Sites: The revised element did not address
         this finding. The element has identified vacant sites that were identified in two or more
         consecutive planning periods, the sites are inadequate to accommodate housing for
         lower-income households unless:

   C. Quantified Objectives

      Establish the number of housing units, by income level, that can be constructed,
      rehabilitated, and conserved over a five-year time frame. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (b)(1
      & 2).)

      The revised element still does not include quantified objectives for each income
      category. For example, the element did not include the number of housing units that will
      be rehabilitated or conserved/preserved. Conservation objectives may include the
      variety of strategies employed by the City to promote tenant stability, code enforcement
      and repair programs that conserve the housing stock, and the preservation of units at-
      risk of conversion to market rate.
