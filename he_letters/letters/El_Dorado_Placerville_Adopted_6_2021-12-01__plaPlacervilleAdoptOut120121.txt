         RE: Placerville’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Adopted Housing Element

         Dear Cleve Morris:

         Thank you for submitting the City of Placerville’s (City) housing element adopted on
         August 31, 2021 and received for review on September 3, 2021, with additional
         revisions submitted November 20, 2021. Pursuant to Government Code section 65585,
         subdivision (b), the California Department of Housing and Community Development
         (HCD) is reporting the results of its review.

         The adopted housing element has made significant progress and addresses most of the
         statutory requirements described in our July 13, 2021 review; however, revisions will be
         necessary to comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code),
         as follows:

             1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15
                (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
                assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
                (c)(10)(A).)

                  Promote and affirmatively further fair housing opportunities and promote housing
                  throughout the community or communities for all persons regardless of race,
                  religion,sex, marital status, ancestry, national origin, color, familial status, or
                  disability, and other characteristics... (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(5).)

                  Disproportionate Housing Needs, including Displacement Risk: The adopted
                  housing element now includes some information on disproportionate housing
                  needs, including displacement risk. However, the element should analyze any
                  patterns of disproportionate housing needs within the City. For example, the
                  element mentions a higher risk of displacement for lower-income households, but
                  it could also discuss patterns of lower-income households and utilize local data
                  and knowledge, beyond federal and state level data, to better formulate policies
                  and programs to address displacement risks.

      Goals, Actions, Metrics, and Milestones: Based on the outcomes of a complete
      analysis, the element should re-assess and prioritize contributing factors and add
      or modify goals and action. For example, programs should be expanded to
      enhance housing mobility, place-based strategies for community revitalization
      and displacement protection. In addition, actions must have specific commitment,
      metrics, and milestones and be significant and meaningful enough to overcome
      identified patterns and trends.

   2. Include an inventory of land suitable for residential development, including vacant
      sites and sites having the potential for redevelopment, and an analysis of the
      relationship of zoning and public facilities and services to these sites (Gov. Code,
      § Section 65583(a)(3)).

      Identify actions that will be taken to make sites available during the planning
      period with appropriate zoning and development standards and with services and
      facilities to (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(1).)

      Infrastructure: While the element now includes information on the availability of
      infrastructure, it must also clarify whether sufficient total water capacity (existing
      and planned) is available to accommodate the regional housing need allocation
      and include programs, if necessary.

      Environmental Constraints: The element discusses how environmental
      constraints were considered in the calculation of residential capacity. As noted in
      the prior review, the element should also describe any known environmental
      constraints and other known conditions that preclude or impact development in
      the planning period.

   3. The housing element shall contain programs which assist in the development of
      adequate housing to meet the needs of extremely low-, very low-, low- and
      moderate-income households. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd. (c)(2).)

      The element now includes Program B-9 to assist in the development of housing
      for some special needs populations. However, Programs should be added or
      modified to address the needs of persons experiencing homelessness, as noted
      in the review of the 5th cycle housing element (Program 7, p. 2-12).


The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it
has been revised to comply with the above requirements.

cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD encourages the

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.

For your information, some general plan element updates are triggered by housing
element adoption. For information, please see the Technical Advisories issued by the
Governor’s Office of Planning and Research at:
