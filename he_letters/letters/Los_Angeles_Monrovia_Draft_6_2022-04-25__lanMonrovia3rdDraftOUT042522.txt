         RE: City of Monrovia’s 6th Cycle (2021-2029) Revised Draft Housing Element

         Thank you for submitting the City of Monrovia’s (City) revised draft housing element
         received for review on February 24, 2022. Pursuant to Government Code section
         65585, subdivision (b), the California Department of Housing and Community
         Development (HCD) is reporting the results of its review. Our review was facilitated by a
         conversation on April 20, 2022 with you; Sheri Bermejo, the Planning Division Manager;
         Teresa Santilena, Associate Planner; and Lisa Brownfield and Diana Gonzalez, the
         City’s consultants.

         The revised draft element addresses most statutory requirements described in HCD’s
         December 17, 2021 review; however, the following revisions will be necessary to
         comply with State Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 of the Gov. Code).

             1. Affirmatively further[ing] fair housing in accordance with Chapter 15
                (commencing with Section 8899.50) of Division 1 of Title 2…shall include an
                assessment of fair housing in the jurisdiction. (Gov. Code, § 65583, subd.
                (c)(10)(A).)

                  Disparities in Access to Opportunity: While the element was revised to add data
                  on local and regional data for patterns and trends in transportation, economic,
                  and education factors, the individual subcategories must be revised with
                  analysis. For example, while the element mentions top performing schools and
                  states that there is no concentration of high performing schools in the City, there
                  should be discussion around lower performing schools and potential overlaps
                  with concentrations of protected classes. The element should also evaluate the
                  presence or lack of policies, practices, and investment to promote access to
                  more proficient schools or that contribute to a disparity in access to opportunity.
                  The element could discuss whether there is equal access to high performing
                  schools throughout the City or whether there is concentration in areas with higher

      Sites Inventory and Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing (AFFH): While the
      element was revised to evaluate all sites relative to socioeconomic patterns, the
      element demonstrates that most sites are located south of the I-210 highway
      where there is already a high concentration of multifamily housing. The element
      should discuss how programs can mitigate this concentration by supporting
      housing choice mobility and improve conditions south of the Metro L Line where
      many of the sites are concentrated. In addition, the majority of sites identified for
      lower-income households are identified in moderate opportunity areas but not the
      high or highest areas of opportunity. The element must analyze why identified
      sites are not located in areas of higher opportunity and describe how the location
      of sites exacerbate or mitigate identified fair housing issues. Lastly, the element
      must describe and analyze where sites are identified for above-moderate
      income.

      Priorities, Metrics, and Milestones: While the element was revised to update
      Program 5.1 to include a table that connects identified fair housing issues with
      contributing factors and actions, this finding is still unmet. All programs that are
      listed in Program 5.1 as pertaining to AFFH should include metrics and
      milestones to create meaningful impact and overcome contributing factors to fair
      housing issues. For example, Program 1.3 (Planning HOMe) could include
      metrics on ADUs and Single Room Occupancy (SROs) and indicate if there will
      be outreach in certain geographic areas of the City. Program 4.7 (Code
      Enforcement/Neighborhood Preservation) could include metrics which target
      certain areas in the City that need more rehabilitation. Program 4.10 (Housing
      Choice Vouchers) could include a metric on doing outreach to homeowners to
      encourage acceptance of vouchers in higher opportunity areas. For more
      information, please see pgs. 55-56 of the AFFH guidebook.

      Program 1.3 (PLANNING HOMe): The element must be revised to address
      HCD’s finding on Lot Consolidation, and as a result this program should be
      updated to be more specific on incentives and policies that are included as part
      of the action to amend the small lot subdivision regulations beyond removing the
      conditional use permit requirement.

      Program 1.6 (Accessory Dwelling Units): The program should be revised to
      adjust assumptions on ADU production and affordability after each biennial
      review, not just in 2024, if the City is not meeting the production and affordability
      assumptions in the element. Lastly, this program must include an action to make
      ADU updates, if needed, according to HCD’s review of the ADU ordinance.

The element will meet the statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law once it
has been revised and adopted to comply with the above requirements.

As a reminder, the City’s 6th cycle housing element was due October 15, 2021. As of
today, the City has not completed the housing element process for the 6th cycle. The
City’s 5th cycle housing element no longer satisfies statutory requirements. HCD
encourages the City to revise the element as described above, adopt, and submit to
HCD to regain housing element compliance.

The City’s statutory deadline to adopt a housing element was October 15, 2021. For
your information, pursuant to Assembly Bill 1398 (Chapter 358, Statutes of 2021), if a
local government fails to adopt a compliant housing element within 120 days of this
statutory deadline, then any rezoning to accommodate the RHNA, including for lower-
income households, shall be completed no later than one year from the statutory
deadline. Otherwise, the local government’s housing element will no longer comply with
State Housing Element Law and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial compliance
pursuant to Government Code section 65585, subdivision (i).

Several federal, state, and regional funding programs consider housing element
compliance as an eligibility or ranking criteria. For example, the CalTrans Senate Bill
(SB) 1 Sustainable Communities grant; the Strategic Growth Council and HCD’s
Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities programs; and HCD’s Permanent
Local Housing Allocation consider housing element compliance and/or annual reporting
requirements pursuant to Government Code section 65400. With a compliant housing
element, the City will meet housing element requirements for these and other funding
sources.
