         The California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) has
         reviewed the City of Del Mar’s (City) obligations under the housing element and hereby
         issues its written findings that the City has failed to implement Program 1E (Rezone of
         Vacant North Bluff and/or South Stratford Properties) to rezone sites to satisfy its lower-
         income Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA) shortfall pursuant to Government
         Code section 65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i).

         HCD requests that the City provide a written response to the findings below within 30
         days and no later than November 16, 2026. HCD will consider the City’s written
         response before taking any enforcement action authorized by Government Code section
         65585, subdivisions (i) and (j). As discussed in this letter, such actions may include
         revoking HCD’s finding that the City’s housing element is in substantial compliance with
         Housing Element Law and/or referral to the California Office of the Attorney General.

         Housing Element Law

         HCD must review any action or failure to act by a local government that it determines to
         be inconsistent with an adopted housing element or Government Code section 65583
         generally, and it must issue written findings accordingly. 1 HCD must give the local
         government a reasonable time, no longer than 30 days, to respond to these findings. 2 If
         HCD does not receive a written response within 30 days, or the response does not
         demonstrate that the program action has been implemented, then HCD may revoke its
         findings that the local jurisdiction’s housing element substantially complies with Housing
         Element Law. 3

      Findings

      On May 31, 2023, HCD found the City’s housing element in substantial compliance with
      Housing Element Law. HCD based its compliance findings on, among other things, a
      commitment to implement housing element Programs 3A (Fairgrounds Affordable Housing)
      or 1E, which are fundamental to the City’s ability to meet its 6th cycle RHNA. These key
      programs commit the City to develop a minimum of 54 units of affordable housing on the
      Fairgrounds site or implement a contingency rezone program, pursuant to Government
      Code section 65583.2, subdivisions (h) and (i). The City is relying on the Fairgrounds site to
      accommodate nearly half of its total lower-income RHNA.

      On December 11, 2024, HCD sent the City a Letter of Technical Assistance, clarifying
      the implementation requirements of these key programs. Although the City met the
      initial requirements of Program 3A by entering into an Exclusive Negotiating Rights
      Agreement (ENRA) with the state 22nd District Agricultural Association (DAA), HCD
      informed the City that a development agreement, such as a ground lease with the DAA,
      is required to effectively implement Program 3A. In addition, HCD clarified that Program
      1E remains a requirement should the ENRA be terminated or development of affordable
      housing on the Fairgrounds becomes infeasible within the planning period.

      On January 15, 2026, HCD sent a Letter of Inquiry regarding the City’s progress. The
      letter acknowledged new deadlines provided by the City, which include reaching an
      agreement with the DAA on the site and lease terms by November 2026. Importantly,
      the letter stated that any delays may render the Fairgrounds site infeasible within the
      planning period and would trigger implementation of Program 1E. The City provided a
      written response on February 10, 2026, confirming its understanding of the required
      program actions and deadlines.

      On August 18, 2026, the DAA Board of Directors voted to terminate the Affordable
      Housing Site Due Diligence and Development and Feasibility Analysis Agreement
      (formerly called the ENRA), effectively ending Fairgrounds affordable housing discussions
      with the City. HCD met with the City on September 1, 2026, to inquire about Program 1E
      implementation. The City provided a written response on September 16, 2026, stating
      that it would implement Program 1E by rezoning the North Bluff properties. However, the
      City did not confirm implementation of the requisite “by-right” provisions to satisfy the
      lower-income RHNA shortfall. 5 The City’s response claimed that it had sufficient lower-
      income RHNA capacity based on alternate sites that were not analyzed nor identified in
      the City’s adopted sites inventory. As a reminder, HCD’s December 11, 2024 letter

      currently shows a shortfall of 54 lower-income units to meet the RHNA.

      Housing Element Law, and HCD may revoke its finding of substantial compliance.
